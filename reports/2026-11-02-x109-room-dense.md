# X109 — both room results survive dense logging, and crowding keeps its domain

**Plan:** [`2026-11-02-x109-room-dense-plan.md`](2026-11-02-x109-room-dense-plan.md) (20:15).
**Aggregates:** `l1_field_x109_room_dense.json`, `l1_field_x106_crowding.json` (rewritten on the `x108` prefix — the sparse values are in
git history and in X106's report). Analysis of X108's twenty runs; no new runs.

## Why it was worth doing

X107's headline — *heredity is not a shared-room effect, the room costs 0.064 of 0.519* — is the line's answer to the most obvious
referee question, and it had been measured only on the series X105 showed to be compressed (maze heredity 0.384 instead of 0.480). If
the room correction grows with the sample, the answer changes. X108's twenty runs are the same worlds with a field at every logged
step.

## Room: it replicates

| inflow | pairs | room coefficient (mother) | control ρ(mother, daughter) | partial given room | both rooms | **drop** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.030 | 2 143 / 2 211 | **+0.389** | 0.538 | **0.469** | 0.434 | **0.069** |
| 0.040 | 1 739 / 1 951 | −0.025 | 0.487 | **0.487** | 0.480 | **0.000** |

Registered verdict: **the room result survives**. Both controls fired — the heredity coefficient reproduces X108's 0.548 / 0.480 within
0.01, and the room coordinate keeps its sign and magnitude (sparse: +0.397 / −0.038). The drop is **0.069 and 0.000** against 0.064 and
0.000 on the sparse series: the same number to within a hundredth, on a sample whose maze heredity is a quarter larger.

So the answer to *"isn't this just daughters staying near their mothers?"* does not move with the sample: about **13 %** of the lattice
coefficient is room, the rest is not. The room-adjusted heredity values on the dense sample are **0.469 / 0.487**.

## Crowding: same domain, weaker lifetime component

| inflow | ρ(d_near, daughter area) | ρ(d_near, **mother's** area) | ρ(d_near, lifetime) | worlds lifetime > 0 | heredity control |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.030 | **+0.360** | **+0.394** | +0.091 | **5/10** | 0.530 |
| 0.040 | +0.187 | −0.016 | +0.031 | 5/10 | 0.468 |

Registered verdict: *mixed* by X106's rule (the "property of places" condition needs both cells), and the substance is unchanged from the
sparse series: in the lattice the coordinate predicts the **mother's** own area as well as her daughter's (+0.394 against +0.360), so it
describes places; in the maze it does nothing (−0.016). The crowding coefficient itself replicates (sparse 0.322, dense 0.360; X103d
0.358).

What *did* change is the lifetime reading: `+0.171` with 9/10 worlds on the sparse series became **+0.091 with 5/10** — i.e. exactly
what a coin looks like. X106's "growth rather than persistence" reading therefore gets stronger, not weaker: on the better sample the
lifetime component is indistinguishable from noise, while the area component stays at 0.36.

## Reading

The three-term picture of size now has all three terms measured on the dense sample:

| term | lattice | maze | status |
|---|---|---|---|
| inherited excursion width (parent → daughter) | **0.548** | **0.480** | survives, and is larger than the sparse numbers |
| room (distance to the nearest non-ancestor peak) | +0.36 … +0.39, a property of places | ≈ 0 | survives; lifetime component is noise |
| maternal decay (distance from the mother's peak) | ≈ 0 | **−0.329** (X105) | survives |

and the room cannot account for the first: partialling it out costs 0.069 in the lattice and nothing in the maze.
