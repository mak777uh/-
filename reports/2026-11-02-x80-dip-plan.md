# X80 — with the amplitude held fixed, does memory dip where the mixture is richest (w = 0.5)?

**Why.** X79 swept the weight `w` of the imposed second scale and came out non-monotone (0.76, 1.03, 0.96 for w = 0.25, 0.50, 0.75)
because the mixture's variance is `(1−w)² + w²` — minimal at `w = 0.5`. The sweep moved **weight and amplitude together**. The runner
now renormalises the composite to the amplitude of a single pattern (`G ← (G − mean)/sd · sd₁ + 0.5`, clipped), anchored bit-for-bit
with the composed flags off (`x74_f030_pattern_s171` reproduces: `series`, `uid_pairs`, `uid_outcomes` identical).

With amplitude fixed, the three weights differ **only in spectral content**:

- `w = 0.25`: mostly the 12.0-cell scale, a little 8.7;
- `w = 0.50`: equal parts — the richest mixture, closest to a "two preferred scales" world;
- `w = 0.75`: mostly 8.7, a little 12.0.

**Prediction, stated as the deciding contrast.** If shallow memory is about *mixedness*, the half-life should be **shortest at
w = 0.5** and longer at both wings (a dip, not a slope). If it is about *which* scale dominates, the half-life should be monotone in
`w`. The two predictions disagree at w = 0.25 and w = 0.75, which is exactly why the amplitude fix matters.

## 1. Manipulation is connected

The manipulation is `w` at fixed amplitude. Connectedness: every cell must stay patterned (≥10 components per world, ≥400 lag-1
pairs) and all three cells must show the spectral mixture — with fields saved at 6000, the fraction of worlds carrying **two peaks**
(near q ≈ 8 and q ≈ 11) must be highest in the middle cell. If the middle cell is not the most two-peaked, the manipulation did not
do what the design assumes and the comparison is void.

## 2. Positive control fires

The control fires if the wings reproduce a **single-scale-like** memory: half-lives at w = 0.25 and w = 0.75 must both be **above
1.0** (the single-scale value at this inflow is 1.47; a wing that is already at 0.8 would mean "a little of the other scale is
enough" and the dip test loses its reference).

## 3. Readout exists and varies

Same depth readout as X73–X79 (ancestor → descendant peak area, lags 1–3, `λ`, half-life), plus the two-peak fraction per cell from
the saved fields.

## 4. Sensitivity arithmetic

Three cells × five worlds = fifteen runs at ~90–110 s, so ~25 minutes. Each cell should give ~800 lag-1 pairs; the Fisher band on
`λ` is ~±0.1, so the declared detectable difference is 0.15 divisions — smaller than the band effect (2.1–2.5×) but not smaller than
the noise of the 0.030 cell across series (1.47 vs 1.88 in X74/X78).

## 5. Refusal rule

**Void** if a cell collapses (<10 components) or is underpowered (<400 lag-1 pairs), or if the middle cell is not the most
two-peaked.

**Dip (mixedness)** if `half(0.5) < min(half(0.25), half(0.75)) − 0.15`.
**Slope (dominant scale)** if the three half-lives are monotone in `w` with a total spread ≥ 0.2.
Anything else is **inconclusive**, printed in full.
