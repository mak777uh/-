# X113 — size buys generations: the line's first measured consequence

## The currency

Every previous attempt at "consequence" used **lifetime** (X92, X93), which is entangled with size and sits at the noise floor. This uses
**lineage depth**: how many generations a line reaches. Not a size, not a lifetime, not a count of daughters at one moment.

| 0.030 | 1 073 | **0.467** | **0.459** | −0.185 | 0.548 ✓ | **+1.479 generations** | **[1.289, 1.654]** | 1 142 |
| 0.040 | 832 | **0.332** | **0.340** | −0.070 | 0.480 ✓ | **+2.213 generations** | **[1.962, 2.489]** | 1 195 |

Registered verdict: **size buys generations** — the sister contrast excludes zero in both cells, and the partial of area given lifetime is
0.459 / 0.340, far above the 0.10 bar. Both controls fired: heredity is 0.548 / 0.480 on these pairs, and depth is **not** lifetime in
disguise — the two correlate **negatively** (−0.185, −0.070), so the readout is doing different work.

Median depth is 3 generations in both cells; the maximum is larger, and the horizon truncates some lines (a run of 6 000 steps cannot carry
unbounded depth), which is a caveat on all of the above.

## What this is, and what it is not yet

It is the **first consequence the line has measured**: a lineage whose structures are larger reaches more generations, and the effect is not
a restatement of size (depth, not area), not a restatement of lifetime (partialled, and negatively correlated), and not the sister effect of
X93 (paired on 1 142 and 1 195 pairs, far more than X93's sample).

It is **not yet** evidence that the larger line is *faster* or *fitter*. Depth counts generations, and a bigger structure has more cells that
can divide, so it may reach more generations in the same time by arithmetic rather than by advantage — the same worry X97b addressed for the
granddaughters' sizes. The number that separates the readings is a **rate**: generations per unit of lifetime. The fact that depth and
lifetime correlate *negatively* while the size–depth partial is strongly positive is a hint that the rate version is even stronger, but that
is a hypothesis, not a measurement.

So the honest sentence joins X97b's: **size is inherited, it makes a lineage reach more generations, and whether that is faster propagation
or simply more division events per cell is the next question** — registered as X113b (generations per 1 000 steps, same pairs, same controls).

## Caveats

- Horizon truncation: median depth 3, and lines alive at step 6 000 cannot extend. Depth is therefore a count *within* the run, and the
  effect could partly reflect which lines are still alive at the end.
- A uid with no descendants is excluded (a leaf cannot propagate); the denominator is stated per cell (1 073 / 832).
- The lattice and the maze differ in magnitude (1.5 against 2.2 generations) and the maze is where X93's lifetime advantage reverses — so the
  two cells agreeing in sign here is itself information, not a given.
- Areas are threshold-0.20 excursion areas (X112b: about a tenth of cut correction); one seed set, one box.
