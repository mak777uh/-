## The question and the answer

X113–X114 showed the larger sister's lineage reaches more generations and reaches them faster, on equal windows. The currency that matters for
"conditions for life" is whether that line still **exists at the end**. Paired within sister pairs — same mother, same frame, same world.

| cell | sister pairs | heredity | **survival difference** (larger − smaller) | 95 % CI | only the larger survives / only the smaller | both / neither |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.030 | 1 142 | 0.548 ✓ | **+0.250** | **[0.220, 0.279]** | **324 / 39** | 641 / 138 |
| 0.040 | 1 195 | 0.480 ✓ | **+0.137** | **[0.116, 0.159]** | **178 / 14** | 40 / 963 |

Registered verdict: **faster lines persist better** — both intervals are far from zero, and the direction is the same in both cells. Among the
discriminating pairs the asymmetry is stark: **324 against 39** in the lattice (8:1) and **178 against 14** in the maze (13:1).

Both controls fired: heredity is unchanged (0.548 / 0.480), and the depth contrast reproduces X113 (+1.479 / +2.213 within 5 %), so these are the same
pairs measured for a new question.

The secondary readout agrees: the difference in the **number of living descendants** is +0.349 (CI 0.319–0.380) and +0.141 (CI 0.120–0.163), so the
larger sister's line does not merely survive more often, it survives in greater number.

## The arc, closed in three currencies

| what is claimed | measurement | cells |
|---|---|---|
| size is inherited | ρ₁ = 0.548 / 0.480; room-corrected 0.469 / 0.487 | both |
| it is a lineage property across a generation | granddaughters larger by 12.68 cells (median), 98 % of the mean, branch counts within 4 % | both |
| it buys generations, at a faster rate | +1.479 / +2.213 generations; +6.06 / +18.96 per 1 000 steps; survives equal windows | both |
| **and it buys survival** | **+0.250 / +0.137 in end-survival, 324:39 and 178:14** | both |

So the sentence the line has been building all day can finally be said without a hedge about the currency: **a medium that transmits a size faithfully
also pays for it — in generations and in persistence.** The earlier hedge ("nothing yet shows that inheriting it buys anything") is retired, and the
claim it belonged to (X92's "size is not a currency") stays true in its own terms: *lifetime* is not the currency, and the maze reverses it. Survival
of the line is a different quantity, and there size pays in both cells.

## Caveats

- **The maze barely survives at all**: in 963 of 1 195 pairs neither branch is alive at the end (81 %), so its effect is measured on the 232 pairs where
  something survives. The direction is clear, the base rate is not.
- "Alive at the end" is presence in the last logged step — one threshold-0.20 observation, not a resilience test. A line could be present and about to
  die.
- The mechanism is not identified: a bigger line has more cells and more branches, so it can lose members and still be present. X97b ruled that out for
  one generation (median, branch counts), not for survival.
- One seed set, one box, one grid, the 0.20 cut; the depth control ties this analysis to X113's exact pairs.

## Next
