# X121 — discrete-time hazard: does dividing raise the chance of disappearing, given age?

**Why.** X119 found that at fixed size more daughters predicts lower own-survival; X120 showed divisions happen early, so "time to divide" is not the explanation.
What remains is the **hazard** reading: an older structure has had more time to die before the horizon, and on a single snapshot a dead structure's age *is* its
outcome, so an OLS on the last step cannot separate age from division.

A hazard model over the **intermediate snapshots** can. For every uid present at a logged step, ask whether it is absent at the next logged step, and model that
probability on its age and its daughters so far. Age becomes a predictor rather than an outcome.

## 1. Manipulation is connected

X108's twenty runs (dense logging, a snapshot every 10 steps). **Person-period** rows: one per uid per consecutive pair of logged steps where the uid is present
at the earlier step. Per row: `death` (absent at the next step), `age` = current step − first appearance, `daughters_so_far` (children born up to the current
step), `log_area` (area at the current step).

Connectedness: at least **20 000 person-period rows** per cell; the number of uids and of death events is reported, because a hazard model with few events says
little.

## 2. Positive control fires (numeric)

Three controls, all required:

1. **Heredity** `≥ 0.35` in both cells (0.548 / 0.480).
2. **The events exist:** the death rate must lie between 1 % and 50 % in each cell — a rate of zero or near one makes the model meaningless.
3. **The known direction of size holds:** the `log_area` coefficient must be **negative** (bigger structures disappear less often per step) and exclude zero in at
   least one cell. If size does not reduce the hazard, the coding of the table is suspect and the daughters coefficient cannot be trusted.

## 3. Readout exists and varies

Per cell: a discrete-time linear probability model `death ~ 1 + age + daughters_so_far + log_area`, with 500 world-level bootstrap draws; all coefficients and
intervals; the base death rate; the number of rows and events. Variation exists: the alive rates at the horizon are 29 % and 2 %, so deaths are common in the
lattice and rare in the maze.

## 4. Sensitivity arithmetic

20 000–60 000 person-period rows, but rows within a uid are dependent, so the effective sample is the **number of uids** (≈ 2 000). The bootstrap resamples
**worlds**, which is the level at which the sample varies; intervals are reported from that bootstrap, and a coefficient whose interval contains zero is not
called.

## 5. Refusal rule (fixed before the run)

**Void** if a cell has fewer than 20 000 rows, heredity below 0.35, a death rate outside [1 %, 50 %], or if `log_area` is not negative with an interval excluding
zero in at least one cell.

**Dividing raises the hazard** if the `daughters_so_far` coefficient is **positive** with the interval excluding zero in both cells.
**Dividing does not raise the hazard** if both intervals contain zero.
**Mixed** otherwise, cells reported separately — and if `age` takes the positive sign while daughters does not, the report says that age, not division, is what the
model finds.
