# X178 — The crossing is a cliff, not a floor: radius 6, gap 9 on thirty seeds (04.11)

**Question.** X158's crossing report had radius 6 fusing 3 of 10 at surface gap 9; X176 then found 0 of 15 at gap 10, which with
X158's 0 of 10 makes that cell 0 of 25. A share falling from 0.30 to 0.00 inside one cell of gap is either a **cliff** (the
crossing really is a threshold) or a **floor** (the share at gap 9 is small and ten seeds happened to see three fusions). This
item doubled the seeds at the deciding cell: **radius 6, gap 9, `F` = 0.030, thirty fresh seeds**.

Pre-registered: share in **[0.05, 0.55]**, with the gap-10 cell as the registered negative control.

## Result: the share holds up, and the cliff stands

| readout | value |
|---|---|
| fusion share at gap 9 | **10 of 30 = 0.33** (Wilson [0.19, 0.51]) |
| pooled with X158's same cell | **13 of 40 = 0.33** |
| the gap-10 control (X158 + X176) | **0 of 25** |
| corridor at contact (secondary) | 0.419 (sd 0.042, 10 contact events) |

Both parts of the registered rule are satisfied. The share sits inside [0.05, 0.55], so the cell fuses; the gap-10 control fuses
nothing, so the threshold lies between the cells. The item's own question is answered on the "cliff" side: thirty fresh seeds
reproduce X158's three-of-ten, the pooled estimate is 0.33 with an interval that excludes "essentially never", and one cell out
the share is zero over twenty-five runs. **Radius 6's crossing is a threshold at gap 10 and not a slowly-fading floor.**

## A second number, and it weakens X174 rather than strengthening it

The corridor at contact for this cell is **0.419** (sd 0.042, n = 10). X171 measured the same cell with seven contacts and saw
0.470; the two seed families differ by 0.05, which is about one spread, so the honest reading is that radius 6 at gap 9 reads
0.42-0.47 depending on the seeds.

That matters because X171's size-independence claim rested on it. With the larger sample, three arms in the same block read:

| arm | share | corridor |
|---|---|---|
| radius 6, gap 9 | 0.33 | 0.419 |
| radius 3, gap 9 | 0.88 | 0.411 |
| radius 4.5, gap 10 | 0.25 | 0.492 |

The first two agree closely at very different shares, and the third sits 0.07 higher at a lower share. X174's ordering by share
does not hold across these three: the arm with the *middle* share reads the *lowest* corridor. So X178 is not only a refinement
of X158 — it is a second counterexample to the single-curve reading, independent of X175's.

## Limits

The registered share interval was wide by design, so "inside it" is not a sharp claim; the resolution comes from the pooled
13 of 40 against 0 of 25, not from the interval. The gap-10 control uses earlier series rather than fresh seeds of the same
family, and the corridor comparison rests on 10 events against X171's 7.

## What would kill it

A gap-10 run at radius 6 that fuses even twice in twenty-five — the pooled control would then be 2 of 27 and the "cliff" reading
would become "small share on both sides of one cell". The other route is showing that X158's and X176's zeroes came from a
seeding or jitter difference rather than from the dynamics.
