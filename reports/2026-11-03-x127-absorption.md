# X127 — no registered outcome fired: a lineage usually ends by being absorbed, and that is true in all three cells

**Plan:** [`2026-11-03-x127-absorption-plan.md`](2026-11-03-x127-absorption-plan.md) (05:06).
**Aggregate:** `l1_field_x127_absorption.json`. No new worlds: the three cells of X108 and X126, thirty dense logs already on disk.

## What was asked

X126 ended with a trade-off but no mechanism: in the percolating cell the larger sister's branch survives to the end **+0.1372** of the time, against **+0.2496** in
the lattice. The plan said "count cuts". Reading the tracker before writing the instrument said something narrower and more useful: a uid ends either by
**dividing** or by **disappearing**, and disappearing has two meanings — the cells **vanish**, or they become **part of a neighbour** (absorption). The logged
areas decide which: absorbed if the surviving uids grow by ≥ 50 % of the dead uid's area at its death step, vanished if by ≤ 10 %, unclear in between.

## Controls — all four held

End-survival reproduces X126 **to four decimals** (+0.2496 / +0.2984 / +0.1372) with the same pair counts (1 142 / 1 106 / 1 195); heredity is 0.548 / 0.421 /
0.480; the **area budget closes on every one of the 18 000 step transitions** in all thirty worlds (0 failures, tolerance 2 cells); the three fates sum to the death
count in every cell. The classification is not marginal: the median growth ratio among absorbed deaths is **2.07–2.28**, four times the 50 % threshold.

## The result: the pre-registered dichotomy does not hold, and that is the finding

The rule had two branches. **Homogenising** required the absorbed share to rise by more than 0.10 — it *falls* by 0.089. **Lethal** required that fall plus
vanished deaths dominating — vanishing rises to 16.5 %, which is four times the lattice's 8.4 % but nowhere near a majority. Neither branch fired.

What the three cells actually say:

1. **Absorption dominates everywhere.** In the percolating cell, where a single body fills 935 cells, three of every four lineage deaths are still absorptions, not
   cells dying. "Death" in every persistence number this line has published (X115, X116, X126) is overwhelmingly **loss of identity with the cells staying alive**.
   That is a stronger statement than the plan expected and it applies to the lattice too (84.5 %).
2. **What changes across the habitats is the number of deaths, not their kind.** Identity deaths rise ×2.1 (524 → 1 102) and **real vanishings rise ×4.1**
   (44 → 182). The maze does not kill in a new way; it ends identities more often, and among those a growing minority are genuine.
3. **The things that die get bigger** — median dead area 27 → 55 → 114 cells — which is what a percolating body implies: the unit that loses its identity is a
   larger piece of the world.
4. **The smaller sister's lineage ends by absorption more often than the larger's** — 248 against 183 pairs in the lattice, 187 against 98 in the maze, paired
   difference −0.0569 and −0.0745 (CI −0.101…−0.047, interval excluding zero). This is coherent with the X93/X92 reading that in a percolating body the larger
   structure is the one about to be cut for real: the small one gets swallowed, the large one is destroyed.

So the honest sentence for the manuscript is not "the maze is lethal" and not "the maze is homogenising" but: **across these three habitats, a lineage's death is
usually the death of its name, not of its cells — and the maze is the cell where names are lost most often and most expensively.**

## A defect in my own run, caught before publication

The first verdict printed "the maze is lethal" although the registered condition for that branch was false on its second clause. The logic had been written to a
two-sided dichotomy with no third branch, so the fall-through inherited the second branch's label. Fixed before anything was published, and the run is
deterministic, so the numbers never changed — but the failure mode is worth a rule: **a refusal rule must contain a "neither" branch**, otherwise the world's third
answer gets reported as whichever side the code fell to. Written into [`docs/design-checks.md`](../docs/design-checks.md).

## What this means for the conditions of life

X126's trade-off stands, with its mechanism narrowed. The intermediate habitat is the best place for a large lineage not because cells die less there — absorption
is the norm everywhere — but because identities end less often (844 against 1 102) and when they end, four times fewer of them take the cells with them. In this
medium **"to persist" means "to keep one's name"**, and the habitats differ in how often a name is lost, not in what losing it costs.

## Next hypotheses

1. **X128 — the identity half-life.** Since absorption is the dominant death, measure how long a lineage keeps its name after its cells are gone: time from the
   subtree's last own-uid appearance to the last descendant appearance. This turns "persistence" into a rate of name loss per 1 000 steps, comparable across cells.
2. **X129 — is absorption competition?** If a lineage is absorbed by a neighbour, the neighbour's area grows; check whether the absorbing structure is the same one
   that later dominates (i.e. whether absorption is a route to takeover) or whether the body is one continuous entity and "absorption" is an artefact of the split
   convention.
3. **X130 — the vanishings.** 182 real deaths in the maze against 44 in the lattice: are they at the edge of the body, where `Dw` cuts (X96d's finding), or in its
   interior? The logs carry centroids, so this needs no new runs.

## Caveats

- The absorbed/vanished split is a **budget argument on areas**, not a cell-level lineage trace: a structure that shrinks while its neighbour grows is classified as
  absorbed even if the cells are different cells. The 50 % / 10 % thresholds are declared, and 7–9 % of deaths fall in the unclear band in every cell.
- One seed set (301–310), ten worlds per cell, one box, the 0.20 cut; the intervals are bootstrap over deaths within these worlds, not over worlds.
- `0.035` is the band's edge where X93's own sister contrast was weakest; the middle cell's numbers are a band, not a point.
