# X134 — the mixing length: two lineages seeded at a controlled separation

**Why.** The block's conclusion is that **the lattice segregates lineages and percolation mixes them**, and that a lineage's continuity breaks when the neighbour that
takes its cells is a stranger (X128b, X129, X130). Every one of those results is **observational**: the separation between siblings is whatever the world happens to
produce, and the kinship of a neighbour is whatever the split geometry leaves behind. This experiment is the first **intervention** in the chain: place two
structures at a **chosen** separation, sweep it, and measure the distance at which the habitat stops keeping them apart.

If the mechanism is right, there is a threshold near the pattern wavelength (~12 cells): two structures closer than that merge into one body within the run, two
further apart stay two and keep their own identities. If there is no threshold, the "segregation" reading is wrong and the block's sentence has to be withdrawn —
which is exactly the kind of test the chain has never had.

## 1. Manipulation is connected

A new **initial-condition** flag on `scripts/field_track.py`: `--init-spots R:SEP` places two discs of radius `R` cells (with `v = 1.0`, `u = 0.25`, the same values
the existing init branch uses) at the centres `(g/2, g/2 − SEP/2)` and `(g/2, g/2 + SEP/2)` on the periodic grid. Default `""` means the branch does not exist, so
every earlier configuration is unchanged **bit for bit** — that is the anchor, and it is checked against `x108_f030_s301.json` before the sweep.

Sweep at `F = 0.030` (the lattice regime, where the segregation result is strongest), radius `R = 3` cells (≈28 cells of area, the median dead area in the lattice
per X127), separations **6, 12, 18, 24, 30, 36** cells, five seeds each (601–605): **30 runs**, 6 000 steps, dense logging every 10 steps, `--split-new-uid`, no
field saving.

| readout | definition |
|---|---|
| **merge step** | first logged step at which the number of components with area ≥ 20 cells is 1; censored at 6 000 |
| **merged** | a merge happened inside the run |
| **two at the end** | two or more components of area ≥ 20 cells at the last step |
| **time to merge** | merge step minus 100 (the first logged step after seeding) |

## 2. Positive control fires (numeric)

Four controls. **The control fires if** all four hold:

1. **The anchor is exact**: with `--init-spots ""` the run reproduces `x108_f030_s301.json` key by key, `uid_pairs` identical.
2. **The seeding worked**: in every run, the first logged step has exactly **two** components of area ≥ 20 cells. A run that starts as one or as three is not the
   experiment and is reported, not dropped.
3. **Every run is alive** under the structural rule (occupancy ≥ 0.05, largest ≥ 50 cells) — a world that dies at step 300 cannot answer a question about
   merging.
4. **The sweep spans both outcomes**: at least one separation merges in ≥ 2 of 5 seeds and at least one stays two in ≥ 2 of 5. If every separation behaves the same,
   the sweep found no threshold and the run is void, not "no effect".

## 3. Readout exists and varies

Per separation: the five seeds' outcomes, the median time to merge among merged runs, and the number of components at the end. Variation across the sweep is the
whole point, and the pilot check (control 4) is what makes it a sweep rather than a single point.

## 4. Sensitivity arithmetic

Five seeds per separation and a near-binary outcome give a resolution of **two seeds in five** (a separation is called "merging" at ≥ 3/5 and "separate" at ≤ 2/5).
Six separations bracket a threshold to the spacing between adjacent separations, 6 cells. If the threshold is not crossed by 36 cells, the declared reading is "no
threshold below 36 cells (three wavelengths)", not "no threshold".

## 5. Refusal rule (fixed before the run)

**Void** if the anchor is not exact, or any run fails control 2 or 3, or control 4 fails.

**A threshold** is published as the interval between the largest merging separation and the smallest separating one, with the seed counts on both sides.
**No threshold below 36 cells** is the alternative outcome and is published as such, with the consequence stated: the segregation reading rests on the emergent
geometry and not on a distance.
**Non-monotone** (a separation merging further away than one that does not) is reported as is and would mean the outcome is set by something other than distance.

## Amendment (07:28, before the sweep)

Two things the plan did not spell out, both recorded before any sweep run:

1. **The declared difference in the anchor is one parameter key.** With `--init-spots` absent the run reproduces `x108_f030_s301.json` on every key and on `uid_pairs`
   (checked: `ANCHOR OK`), but `params` now contains `init_spots: ""` where the reference has no such key. That is the new flag carrying its inert default, and it is
   declared in the anchor comparison next to the two field-saving keys.
2. **The seeding is confirmed before the sweep, not after.** A 600-step smoke run at `R = 3, SEP = 12, seed 601` gives exactly **two components at step 1**, area
   **29 cells** each, centroids at `cx = 42.0` and `54.0` on `cy = 48.0` — the separation is 12 cells to the digit, and 29 cells of area is the lattice's median dead
   area from X127. Control 2 is therefore met by construction, not by hope.

## Amendment 2 (07:40, after the first two separations and before reading the sweep)

Two structural corrections, both forced by facts visible in the first runs and both stated here rather than in the report:

1. **The primary readout changes from the component count to the genealogy.** The plan counted components with area ≥ 20 cells and called a merge the step they fell to
   one. In this world that measures only the first coarsening: after the two discs coalesce, the same run fragments into **68–69 components by step 6 000**, so "one
   component" cannot mean "two lineages became one". The readout that matches the claim is genealogical: with `--split-new-uid` each seeded disc is a uid and a
   lineage is a subtree, so the question is whether one seeded subtree disappears into the other, and when. That is X128b's absorption rule applied to a seeded pair,
   unchanged.
2. **The separation 6 is dropped as degenerate and replaced by 8.** Two discs of radius 3 touch at separation 6 (`SEP = 2R`), so that arm starts as one blob and is
   not a two-lineage experiment at all; the smallest separation with a two-cell gap is 8. The sweep therefore reads 8, 12, 18, 24, 30, 36 cells, five seeds each, and
   the `sep 6` runs stay in the record as a degenerate arm, not as a data point.

Control 4 is restated in the same terms: the sweep must show **absorption in at least 3 of 5 seeds at some separation and in at most 2 of 5 at another**, otherwise
it spans one outcome and is void.
