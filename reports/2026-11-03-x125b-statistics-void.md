# X125b — void by the floor, but the filter reproduces X113c to three decimals and shows it rests on half the maze pairs

**Plan:** [`2026-11-03-x125b-definitions-plan.md`](2026-11-03-x125b-definitions-plan.md) (03:09).
**Aggregate:** `l1_field_x125b_statistics.json`.
**Preceded by:** [X125 void](2026-11-03-x125-definitions-void.md).

## What it was for

X125 voided because the rate "missed X113c by a factor of three and eight". Its diagnosis: the gap is not a unit of aggregation (X124's reading) but an
undeclared **sample filter** — X113c keeps a pair only if both sisters' generation times are ≥ 50 steps. X125b printed the filter and asked whether that is the
whole difference.

## Finding 1 — the filter is the whole difference, exactly

With `g ≥ 50` on both sisters the pair-weighted rate contrast is **6.056** and **18.96** — identical to the published values to every printed digit, not within
10 %. So:

- X113c's number was always pair-weighted; **X124's diagnosis that the two differed by unit of aggregation is withdrawn**;
- the entire order-of-magnitude gap (36.6 → 6.06, 321.5 → 18.96) is generated by 185 and 557 short-lived pairs;
- a filter that changes a published number by a factor of six to seventeen was never printed next to it.

**New rule** ([`docs/design-checks.md`](../docs/design-checks.md)): *a filtered statistic names its filter and its denominator in the same sentence as the
number.* This is the third member of a family the line has now hit three times in one night — X113c's lower bound on the denominator, X93's exclusion of ties
(below), and X125's mean of ratios where a ratio of means belonged. All three are **undeclared sample rules**, not physics.

## Finding 2 — X113c's maze rate rests on 53 % of the pairs

`r = 18.96` at `F = 0.040` is computed on 638 of 1 195 sister pairs; the other 557 have at least one sister whose generation time is under 50 steps — 5 logging
quanta — so their rate is not measurable at this resolution. The lattice cell keeps 84 %. The number is not wrong; it is **unqualified**, and the qualification
is a property of the world: in the maze, generations are short enough that half the pairs fall below the measurement floor. Every quotation of 18.96 now carries
"638 of 1 195 pairs".

## Finding 3 — generation time is shorter for the larger sister under every statistic here, and the tail is the reason the mean looks so large

All three statistics agree in direction: mean −412.4 steps (interval excludes zero), ratio of means 0.716 (the larger sister's generation time is 72 % of the
smaller's), fraction of pairs with the larger sister longer 0.409. The median is **zero**: generation times are quantised at 10 steps, so ties are common, and a
median over ties lands on the tie.

The mean is tail-dominated, and the decomposition says how: 28 % of pairs differ by more than ±1 000 steps; the average positive difference is +752 steps and the
average negative one −1 952. So the larger sister's advantage is **frequent and small when positive, infrequent and large when negative** — exactly the shape in
which a mean and a majority sign are both true statements about different questions.

## Finding 4 — and X93's 0.530 is not contradicted by these 0.409

X93's `within_fraction` is computed with **ties excluded**: `x93_analyse.py` skips a pair when `life(a) == life(b)`. This run counted ties in the denominator and
as "not larger". With ties making up a large share of pairs (§Finding 3: median at zero), the two fractions are not the same quantity, and 0.409 against 0.530 may
be entirely the tie rule. The tie share is the missing number and is the subject of X125c.

So the X124 "generation-time contradiction" now has a complete explanation: **X93 measured a tie-excluded proportion; X124/X125 measured a mean.** Neither sign was
wrong, and the "share of the pair's mean" that both plans attributed to X93 never existed.

## The void

The registered refusal rule voided the run: fewer than 1 000 pairs survive the filter (957 and 638). The floor was mine and it was set without computing the drop —
a defect of the declaration, not of the medium. Its lesson is the one that matters for the next design: **for a definitional audit the sensitivity condition is
the interval width, not a count of pairs.** At 638 pairs the maze rate's interval is ±4.2 on a mean of 19.0 (22 %), which is ample for the question "does this
reproduce 18.96"; it is not ample for an effect size. Void as an effect claim; the diagnostic stands, and the numbers above are published as diagnostics with their
intervals, not as new effects.

## What goes into the manuscript and CLAIMS

1. Rate: always `d/g × 1000`, **pair-weighted**, with `g ≥ 50` on both sisters and its denominator (957/1 142; 638/1 195).
2. Generation time: the statistic is named — mean for magnitude, fraction for frequency, median reported as a tie. The three agree in direction here.
3. X93's proportion is quoted as **tie-excluded** from now on, or recomputed (X125c).
4. The depth leg (+1.479 / +2.213, both intervals inside 10 % of X113) is the one consequence number that has survived every definitional check unchanged.

## Caveats

- One seed set (X108's twenty runs), one box, the 0.20 cut; generation times quantised at 10 steps.
- X93's series is not on disk, so its 0.530 cannot be recomputed from its own logs — only compared, or X108 can be made to speak its definition.
- The filtered rate's coverage (84 % / 53 %) is a property of these twenty runs, not yet of the cell.
