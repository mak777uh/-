# X166 — The contact gap at one-step resolution: the band is real, and "the same at every size" is not (04.11)

**Question.** X159 showed that the 1.9-cell reading of X157 is an upper bound, because the log samples every tenth
integration step and the bodies grow inside that quantum; it predicted a window of 1.3 cells and asked for a one-step log.
This item supplies it: five arms of the fusion block re-run with `--sample-every 1`, 2 000 steps, five fresh seeds each
(911-915), everything else identical.

Pre-registration: [`2026-11-04-x166-one-step-threshold-plan.md`](2026-11-04-x166-one-step-threshold-plan.md).

## Result 1 — the resolution question is settled, in favour of the band

The one-step log makes the residual window **0.056 cells** (median growth of the two radii per integration step), against
1.3 cells at ten-step sampling — a factor of 23. The between-arm spread of the last two-body gap is **2.07 cells**, and the
within-arm scatter is 0.44. So the window is now two orders of magnitude smaller than the effect it was hiding, and the
effect is still there: **the contact gap is genuinely a distributed quantity, not a threshold blurred by sampling.**

That closes the question X159 opened. The answer is the second branch of the pre-registered refusal rule: "about two cells"
is a band by right, not by resolution. The article's phrasing needs no further softening — but it also cannot be sharpened
into a single number, and this is now measured rather than conceded.

## Result 2 — "the same at every seeded size" does not survive

The per-arm values (gap at the last step with two bodies, cells):

| arm | fused | values | median | fusion step |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| r3, gap 8 | 5/5 | 1.174, 1.214, 1.274, 1.299, 1.420 | **1.27** | 44-54 |
| r3, gap 9 | 5/5 | 1.706, 1.752, 1.860, 1.872, 2.898 | **1.86** | 60-85 |
| r4.5, gap 9 | 4/5 | 2.926, 3.070, 3.213, 7.848 | **3.14** | 67-108 |
| r5.2, gap 7 | 5/5 | 1.034, 1.053, 1.074, 1.085, 1.304 | **1.07** | 35-41 |
| r6, gap 8 | 5/5 | 2.933, 2.980, 3.006, 3.118, 4.662 | **3.01** | 48-68 |

X157 reported 2.07 / 1.89 / 1.69 / 1.93 at radii 3 / 4.5 / 5.2 / 6 and concluded that the reading is "the same at every
seeded size". At one-step resolution the same quantity is **1.27 / 3.14 / 1.07 / 3.01** — the smallest arm and the largest
arm differ by a factor of three, and the radius-4.5 arm moves from 1.89 to 3.14. The earlier uniformity was partly the
sampling rate (a coarse log overwrites the fine structure with its own quantum) and partly an artefact of taking the median
over runs whose fusion times differ by a factor of two.

The structure that does survive is different and more interesting: **the later the fusion, the wider the gap at which it
happens.** Within every arm the widest value belongs to the slowest run (2.898 at fusion step 85 against 1.71-1.87 at 60-65;
7.848 at step 108 against 2.93-3.21 at 67-69; 4.662 at step 68 against 2.93-3.12 at 48-51). A pair that takes longer to
merge does so from further away, which is what one expects if the merging is driven by the field between the bodies and
that field has time to build a bridge: the gap is not a fixed trigger distance but the state of a process that runs at its
own pace.

## Controls

The positive control fires: gap 8 fuses 5/5 at radius 3 and 5/5 at radius 6, matching the block's 5/5 and 10/10. The readout
varies well beyond its pre-registered floor (spread 2.07 against ≥ 0.1). Radius 4.5 at gap 9 gives 4/5, the same share as
in X158 (4/5), so the one-step series reproduces the block's outcomes.

One number in the aggregate is unreliable and is flagged as such: the per-step growth of the two radii is **negative** in
the radius-4.5 and radius-6 arms (−0.045 and −0.085 cells per step), because near the merge the two silhouettes are already
overlapping and the component areas are being clipped. It is used only to size the window, and even with the sign taken
away the window stays below 0.1 cells; it is not used to correct the gaps.

## What this changes in the article

§4.1 currently says the contact reading is "the same at every size we seeded: 2.07 at radius 3, 1.89 at 4.5, 1.69 at 5.2,
1.93 at 6". That sentence has to be replaced: at one-step resolution the values are 1.27 / 3.14 / 1.07 / 3.01, the spread
is real, and the fine structure carries a new regularity (later fusion → wider gap). The "about two cells" band survives as
the *typical* value, with the honest statement that the individual event ranges from about one cell to about five.

## Limits

Five seeds per arm and 2 000 steps, so the tail (one run per arm) is illustrated rather than characterised. The arms do not
share a fusion time, and the correlation between gap and fusion time is computed within arms, over five points each — it is
a pattern, not a slope. The radius-4.5 arm lost one run to the 2 000-step limit (4/5), so its median rests on four values.

## What would kill it

A ten-seed repeat of the two arms that carry the spread (radius 4.5 at gap 9 and radius 6 at gap 8) would show whether the
wide values are a tail or the rule; if the medians converge to 1.3 at every size, the size-dependence is five-seed noise.
Equally, logging the field between the bodies (not just the components) during a late fusion would test the "bridge takes
time" reading directly.

## Next hypotheses

1. **The bridge, not the distance**: save the field in the corridor between the two bodies at one-step resolution for one
   arm and measure when the corridor's concentration crosses the fusion threshold, rather than the silhouette gap.
2. **Does the gap scale with the size at contact?** A ten-seed sweep at radius 3, 4.5, 5.2 and 6 at a fixed fusion time
   (achieved by choosing the initial gap) would separate "size" from "time".
3. **The tail**: the runs that fuse from 5-8 cells away — do they pass through a normal contact first and bounce, or do they
   approach monotonically and merge at a distance?
