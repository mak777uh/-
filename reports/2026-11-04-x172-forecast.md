# X172 — A prediction at an unrun rate: the corridor lands, the share does not (04.11)

**Question.** X170's ladder — fusion shares 4/15, 7/15, 10/15 and corridor 0.516, 0.479, 0.392 at `F` = 0.026, 0.030, 0.036 —
is a description fitted to three points. A description becomes a law when it predicts a cell it was not fitted to. This item ran
the intermediate rate `F` = 0.033 at surface gap 10, radius 3, twenty seeds (986-1005), against **two predictions registered
before the run**: the share between 9 and 15 of 20 (point 0.567) and the corridor at contact 0.436 ± 0.08.

Pre-registration: [`2026-11-04-x172-forecast-plan.md`](2026-11-04-x172-forecast-plan.md). Positive control: the gap-8 arm at
this rate, five seeds, must fuse 5/5.

## Result: one hit, one miss by a single run

| readout | predicted | observed | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| corridor at contact | 0.436 ± 0.08 | **0.442** (sd 0.029, 8 contact runs) | **lands** |
| fusion share | 9-15 of 20 | **8 of 20** = 0.40, Wilson [0.22, 0.61] | **miss**, by one run |

The control held (gap 8 fused 5/5 at this rate, so the world is in the same regime), the peak stayed in band, the corridor read
below the peak in every contact run, and the readout varies.

So the pre-registered rule is applied as written and gives a **partial**: the corridor interpolates between the two measured
neighbours almost exactly (0.442 against 0.436), and the share does not. But the miss deserves its own arithmetic rather than
only the label. The prediction's point value was 0.567; the observation's 95 % interval [0.22, 0.61] contains it, and the
neighbour's own interval at `F` = 0.030 — 7 of 15, [0.25, 0.70] — contains 0.40 as well. The two arms are therefore
**indistinguishable**, and the honest statement is that twenty seeds resolve neither the interpolation nor its absence. What the
item does establish is that the corridor's dependence is smooth enough to interpolate across this range, and that the share's is
not yet established as linear, because its noise is larger than the difference being predicted.

## Why the share is the noisier readout

The corridor is a property of the runs that fused and it varies little (sd 0.03); the share is a count over twenty runs, so its
standard error at `p` ≈ 0.5 is 0.11 — comparable to the entire range of the ladder (0.27 to 0.67). Predicting a share to within
0.1 therefore needs about a hundred seeds per cell, not twenty. The ladder's own three points rest on fifteen each and their
intervals overlap pairwise; only the trend test (X170's Cochran–Armitage, p = 0.014) distinguishes them.

## Limits

Twenty seeds at one rate, one size, one gap. The prediction was registered honestly and one of the two halves missed; the
interpretation above is post hoc and is labelled as such. The corridor's sd (0.029) is inside the pooled spread of X171 (0.040),
so this arm is consistent with the size-independence result but does not test it.

## What would kill it

A second unrun rate with the same design — `F` = 0.028 or 0.034 — would say whether the corridor interpolates repeatably and
whether the share's miss was noise. If the corridor misses there, the smoothness found here is an artefact of one arm.

## Next hypotheses

1. **A share prediction with the power to test it**: one hundred seeds at a single intermediate rate, which would resolve a 0.1
   difference in the share and turn the ladder into a calibrated curve.
2. **The corridor across sizes at a second rate** — X171's prediction, still untested: 0.39 at every size at `F` = 0.036.
3. **Interpolate the crossing, not the share**: the crossing in cells is a threshold on a smooth quantity, so predicting *it*
   from the corridor's growth is a better-posed forecast than predicting a share whose noise dominates.
