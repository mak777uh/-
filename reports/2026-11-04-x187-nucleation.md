# X187 — why the law flips sign at the largest size (04.11)

**Question.** X186 re-read the 57-cell table in the local inflow and found the per-cell association positive in 9 of 11 cells —
as X183/X184's law predicts — with both exceptions at the largest measured radius: r_eff 5.86 at gap 7 (slope −0.132) and at
gap 9 (−0.478 over 54 runs). The preregistered hypothesis
(`reports/2026-11-04-x187-nucleation-plan.md`) was that a **warm medium nucleates bodies of its own**, so a large pair in a warm
site never reaches one component even when its fronts meet, which would invert the law's sign there.

**Design.** Two arms, radius 6 gap 9 and radius 3 gap 10, each at two levels of the local inflow at the **colder** of the two
seeded bodies (cold `F_local` ≈ 0.0170, warm 0.0300), six seeds per group, 3 000 steps, samples every 10 steps.
One run failed on a transient DLL load and was re-run; 24 of 24 completed.

**The registered readout turned out not to be a readout.** "Bodies besides the pair, within 8 cells of its midpoint, at the
sample where the total count is smallest" came out at **1 exactly when the pair fused and 0 exactly when it did not — in
24/24 runs**. The reason is mechanical: the readout excludes components within three cells of
the **seeded** centres, and the merged body drifts further than that, so the pair's own single component was counted as a body
besides the pair; an unfused pair stays inside that radius and counts as none. With one component on the field there is nothing
besides it, so the quantity is the outcome restated and cannot speak about nucleation. Recorded as **readout invalid, mechanism
untested**, not as evidence against nucleation.

**What the experiment does establish: the flip is real and designed.** The same manipulation of one variable moves the share the
law's way in one arm and the other way in the other:

| arm | cold `F_local` | share | warm `F_local` | share | difference |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| radius 3, gap 10 | 0.0173 | 0 of 6 = 0.00 | 0.0300 | 4 of 6 = 0.67 | **+0.67** |
| radius 6, gap 9 | 0.0170 | 6 of 6 = 1.00 | 0.0300 | 4 of 6 = 0.67 | **-0.33** |

Wilson intervals are wide on six runs per group ([0.3, 0.9032] for the widest), so each arm is a direction, not a point;
but X186's flip was a per-cell observation over existing runs and this is the same flip under selection, which removes the
possibility that it is an artefact of which cells were pooled.

**Quantities that are not functions of the outcome.** The minimum count averages
2.0 at radius 3 cold (never two-to-one) against 1.33 at radius 3 warm, and
1.0 against 1.33 at radius 6; the share of samples spent with one component is about
0.000 / 0.007 (radius 3) and
0.008 / 0.010 (radius 6), consistent with the transient coalescence the review
found in the ladder: a merged pair holds one component for well under one per cent of the run.

**Limits.** Six runs per group; the mechanism question is *not answered* and cannot be answered by this readout, so the flip
still has no mechanism. The corrected readout that would answer it is a count of components whose identities are neither the two
seeded bodies nor their descendants, at a **fixed** sample rather than at the sample of minimum count — the component log carries
uids, so that is a re-analysis of these runs rather than a new series.

**Next hypotheses.** (1) Nucleation as a uid test at fixed samples, on these 24 runs; (2) hold the parity of the separation fixed
so the arms share an effective radius (X186); (3) the same law's sign across the radius ladder, which is now a designed question
rather than a per-cell observation. Artifact: `morphogenesis/experiments/E09_pond/l1_field_x187_nucleation.json`.
