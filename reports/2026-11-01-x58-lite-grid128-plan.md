# X58-lite — is "no wavelength selection" a property of the box?

1 November 2026, 06:45. Preregistration before the run.

X60 found that the homogeneous world selects a mode (45 % of low-q power at q = 8, λ = 12 cells; entropy 1.52 of 2.48)
while the heterogeneous world stays broadband (peak share 0.24, entropy 2.23). Both were measured on a **96** grid, and
the obvious objection is that the heterogeneous arm's broad spectrum is a finite-size effect: a 96-cell box has only
eleven modes below q = 12, so "no selection" could be "no room to select".

This run refines the grid to **128** while keeping the *physical* domain and the physical diffusion the same, and asks the
same question.

**Prediction, written before the data.** If the 96-grid result is physical, then at 128 the homogeneous arm still selects
(q ≈ 8, peak share > 0.4, entropy < 1.7) and the heterogeneous arm is still broadband (peak share < 0.35, entropy > 2.0).
If the heterogeneous arm selects a mode at 128, the X60 claim is a finite-size artefact and is withdrawn.

## Scaling, stated so it can be checked

At grid 96, Δx = 1 and the code's Laplacian is the dimensionless four-neighbour sum, so `Du` in the code plays the role of
`D·dt/Δx²`. Refining to 128 over the same physical domain halves nothing exactly (Δx = 96/128 = 0.75), so:

- `Du = 0.16 / 0.75² = 0.2844`, `Dv = 0.08 / 0.75² = 0.1422` — physical diffusion preserved;
- `dt = 0.5` (from `dt·Du = 0.142 < 0.25`, the explicit-Euler bound), `steps = 12000` — physical time 6000, as before;
- inflow correlation length 12 physical units = **16 cells** at this Δx, so `--hetero-scale 16`;
- fields saved at steps 4000, 8000, 12000 = physical times 2000, 4000, 6000.

A 600-step probe at these settings ran clean (no blow-up, 2 components), which is the stability evidence for the choice.

## 1. Manipulation is connected

The two inflow fields differ on the same seed at 96 (`628da5a16fcb` against `e32e8f6e185c`, different component series), and
the same construction is used here with the correlation length rescaled. The manipulation is the same one, on a finer grid.

## 2. Positive control fires

- **Control arm:** homogeneous inflow at 128. If the scaling is right, this arm **must** still pattern — the physical
  wavelength is unchanged, so it must appear at the same q = 8 with an even sharper peak (more modes available).
- **Observed at 96:** peak share 0.45, entropy 1.52.
- **Threshold that counts as "fires":** homog peak share > 0.4 and entropy < 1.7 at t = 4000 or t = 6000.

If the homogeneous arm fails to pattern at 128, the scaling is wrong and nothing about the heterogeneous arm can be read.

## 3. Readout exists and varies

- **Readouts:** seed-averaged spectral shares over q = 1..20, spectral entropy, q of the peak; plus β₀, β₁ and the largest
  cluster share as in X60.
- **Field in the artifact:** `spectrum_seed_mean[].power_share_q1_12` and `.spectral_entropy`; at 96 these vary across
  arms and times (0.22–0.45 peak share; 1.52–2.25 entropy).

## 4. Sensitivity arithmetic

Five seeds per arm, seed-averaged spectrum, no p-values. The two outcomes are separated by a wide margin at 96
(entropy 2.23 against 1.52), so five seeds are enough to see which side of the gap an arm lands on. The criterion is a
**threshold**, not a difference of means: hetero peak share < 0.35 and entropy > 2.0; homog peak share > 0.4 and
entropy < 1.7.

## 5. Refusal rule

Worthless, not negative, if:

1. any run produces NaN or an empty field (the scheme is unstable at these settings);
2. the homogeneous arm does not pattern (scaling wrong, positive control failed);
3. both arms come out broadband, or both come out sharply peaked — then the 128 grid cannot discriminate the two
   hypotheses, and the run says nothing about finite size either way.
