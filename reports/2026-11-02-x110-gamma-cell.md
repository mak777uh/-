## X110's liveness rule was wrong, and the amendment is registered before the data it judged

X110 counted components and required ten. The lattice column produced a counter-example at once: `γ = 0.50`, seed 423 ends with **16
components**, which a count calls alive, while its neighbours at the same coupling end with one 37-cell blob. The 16 are sixteen equal
**23-cell** specks — a shattered field, not a pattern.

The amendment (registered while the lattice column was complete and the maze column did not exist): **alive ⇔ `occupied ≥ 0.05` **and**
largest component ≥ 50 cells**, with two alternative threshold sets printed beside it. Under all three sets the answer below is the same.

## Correction to X96d's fragility reading

X96d concluded that the maze cell "lives closer to the edge of existence" because at `γ = 0.05` it lost half to three quarters of its
diffusion lengths while the lattice kept all four. X110b shows that this is a statement about **`Dw`**, not about `γ`: at a fixed short
second length (`Dw = 0.16`) both cells survive the same coupling ceiling, 0.30, and die at the same one, 0.50.

The correct statement is narrower and still interesting: **the maze cell tolerates a long-range inhibitor less** — its collapse happens
when the second species diffuses four to eight times further than `v`, and not when the coupling alone is raised. X96d's report and
§10gc are corrected by an appended note rather than edited.

What differs between the cells is *morphology under coupling*, not survival: at `γ = 0.10–0.30` the maze is one percolating body
(largest component 596–3 030 cells, 9–41 % occupied) while the lattice is 1–49 separate structures. Both are "patterns" by the amended
test; they are not the same kind of pattern, and the liveness rule now says so explicitly instead of counting blobs.

## Reading

`γ_cell = 0.30` means both cells survive a **23 % cut** in the autocatalysis inside a structure (`1/(1+0.30) = 0.77`) and die by 33 %
(`1/1.5 = 0.67`). That is a quantitative boundary for this world — the first one the line has measured — and it is the same in both
cells, which is a negative result about "distance to the edge" as a property that distinguishes habitats.

For the life question the useful part is the number itself: a medium whose autocatalysis can be cut by a quarter and still carry
structure has a margin, and the margin is not what differs between the lattice and the maze. What differs is how a long-range inhibitor
breaks them.

## Caveats

- One box, one grid, one `k`, one `Dw` for the coupling sweep; `γ_cell` is a bracket on a five-point grid (0.30 → 0.50 is one step,
  so the true ceiling lies between them and closer resolution is a new series).
- The maze's "alive" states are single percolating bodies; the amended liveness test accepts them by design, and the table prints the
  largest component so the difference is visible rather than hidden.
- Three seeds per point, two-of-three rule: one seed's disagreement flips a cell's bracket only if it happens at the top live coupling.
