# E20 — does moving cost survival?

**Pre-registration:** [`2026-11-09-e20-movement-plan.md`](2026-11-09-e20-movement-plan.md), written before the
run. **Artifacts:** `morphogenesis/experiments/E20/` (80 runs). **Analysis:**
`python scripts/analyse_e20.py`.

## 1. What we looked at

Four arms of one world, twenty seeds each, 300 steps. They differ only in whether the cell moves and what a move
costs:

| arm | moves | price per move |
|---|---|---|
| `still` | no | — |
| `moves_cheap` | yes | 0.00 |
| `moves_base` | yes | 0.05 |
| `moves_dear` | yes | 0.20 |

The question was whether the **price** of moving is what hurts survival, comparing `moves_cheap` with
`moves_base`. The pre-registration predicted a difference of at least 0.15.

## 2. What we found

| arm | runs | fraction alive at 300 | median alive | median moves | median energy |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `still` | 20 | **0.90** | 524.5 | 0 | 1740.9 |
| `moves_cheap` | 20 | **0.80** | 656.5 | 11859 | 1519.3 |
| `moves_base` | 20 | **0.80** | 620.0 | 11746.5 | 1517.0 |
| `moves_dear` | 20 | **0.80** | 647.5 | 12410.5 | **1260.4** |

**The manipulation check passed:** the arms differ in their moves, and the energy falls with the price
(1519 → 1517 → 1260).

**The prediction did not appear.** `moves_cheap` and `moves_base` are **identical at 0.80**. **The price of
moving reaches the energy budget and does not reach survival.**

## 3. What the numbers do say

**The only difference that survives is between moving and not moving: 0.90 against 0.80.**

A move costs 0.05 of energy and the dearest costs 0.20, and the total energy at the end differs by **17 %**
between the cheapest and the dearest arm — **yet survival is the same to two decimal places.** Whatever kills
the moving cells, it is not the energy they spend on moving.

**The monotone check the pre-registration asked for is satisfied** — 0.90, 0.80, 0.80, 0.80 in order of
increasing moves — **but it is satisfied by a single step, not by a gradient.** The relation is "moving or not",
not "more moving is worse".

## 4. What this refutes

**The mechanism proposed in the pre-registration** — a move costs energy and takes the cell away from food, so
more expensive moves should mean less survival — **is refuted.** The energy cost is real (the budget shows it)
and it is not what decides survival.

**So the E19 observation has a different cause than the one we guessed.** E19 found that the arms that move
survive worse than the arm that does not. E20 shows that this is not about the price. The remaining candidates,
none of which this series tests:

1. **Moving changes the neighbourhood.** A cell that moves is a cell that is no longer where it was, so the
   colony's connectivity changes, and connectivity decides budding in this world.
2. **Moving costs the colony, not the cell.** The move is paid per cell, but the consequence may be at the
   component level.
3. **The `still` arm is not a fair control.** It does not move *because* it has no record at all
   (`plastic_eta = 0`), so it also does not run the plasticity update. The comparison "moving against not
   moving" is confounded with "having a record against not having one".

**The third is the most likely and the most uncomfortable:** the only arm that survives better is the arm that
differs in two things at once. **A fair control would be an arm that has a record but never acts on it** — which
is what `record_random` in E19 was, and it survived at 0.75, *worse* than `still`. **So the confound runs the
other way: having a record without acting is worse than having no record at all.** That is a new question and it
is not answered here.

## 5. What this changes in the next experiment

1. **Do not test the price of moving again.** It is measured: it reaches the budget and not survival.
2. **Test the neighbourhood instead.** The next arm pair should hold the number of moves fixed and vary whether
   a move changes the component's connectivity — for example by moving within a component against moving out of
   it. The prediction: survival depends on connectivity, not on distance.
3. **Separate "having a record" from "acting on it" properly.** E19's `record_random` (0.75) is worse than
   `still` (0.90) and worse than `record_pred` (0.80), which means the record is doing something in both
   directions and we do not know what.

## 6. What this does not establish

1. **It does not say movement is harmless.** It says the price of movement is not what hurts.
2. **It does not say the colony-level explanation is right.** It names it as the next candidate, not as a result.
3. **It does not say anything about the living.** It is one measurement of one mechanism in one world.
