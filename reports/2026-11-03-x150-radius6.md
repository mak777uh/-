# X150 — the inward shift of the boundary saturates, and the time stays the gap's at a third radius

## The three radii in one matrix

| radius | disc area | gap 6 | gap 7 | gap 8 | gap 9 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **3.0** | ≈ 28 cells | M 5/5, t 29 | M 5/5, t 39 | M 5/5, t 49 | M 3/5, A 2/5, t 69 |
| **4.5** | ≈ 64 cells | – | M 5/5, t 39 | **M 3/5, A 2/5, t 49** | M 2/5, A 2/5, P 1/5, t 64 |
| **6.0** | ≈ 113 cells | M 5/5, t 29 | M 5/5, t 39 | **M 3/5, A 2/5, t 49** | – |

The registered outcome that fires is **saturation**, not the trend I predicted. I had registered "5/5 at gap 6 and mixed
at gap 7" for radius 6, reading the 3 → 4.5 move as half a cell per unit of radius. The measurement says otherwise:
radius 6 coalesces in **5/5 seeds at gap 7** and breaks only at **8** — exactly where radius 4.5 breaks. The prediction
for the boundary was wrong, and the registration had a branch for it, so the run is valid and the answer is the branch.

So the boundary is not a smooth function of the radius. It moves inward once, between radius 3 (universal to a gap of 8
cells) and radius 4.5 (universal only to 7), and then **stops**: at 113 cells the discs break at the same gap as at 64.
Whatever sets the boundary is not "the bigger the structure, the earlier it fails" — it is something that saturates
between 28 and 64 cells of disc area.

## What holds exactly, a third time

The **timing law** came out of this run untouched, and more sharply than it was registered:

- at gap 6 all three radii dissolve the pair at **29** logged steps; at gap 7 all three at **39**; at gap 8 all three at
  **49** — six all-coalescing arms and two mixed ones, thirty seeds in total, identical to the logging quantum;
- and the gap-8 row is the important one: at radius 3 five of five seeds coalesce, at radius 4.5 and 6 only three of
  five, yet in **all three** the pair is one body by step **49**. The radius changes *whether* a division intervenes,
  never *when* the two bodies meet.

That is now the cleanest statement in the block: the two candidate laws are separate and neither reduces to the other.
Time is the gap's; the outcome is the pair's, and the pair's part saturates.

## Controls

The timing control registered before the run passed on all six all-coalescing arms (29 / 39 / 49 with the expected
values as exact integers), every radius-6 arm has five usable runs, all arms start as two components and none ends
empty, and every median is printed with its n (the mixed arms carry n = 3 coalescing seeds out of 5).

## Caveats

- The saturation is bracketed by two radii on one side and one on the other: 4.5 and 6 agree, 3 differs. A radius of 3.75
  or 5.25 would say whether the stop is a threshold or a plateau, and it is the cheapest next run in this block.
- Radius 4.5 has no arm at gap 6 and radius 6 none at gap 9, so the rows are not complete rectangles.
- The disc areas are geometric (πR²), not measured from the first logged frame, and are used only to name the sizes.
