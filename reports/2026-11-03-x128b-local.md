"something somewhere grew". This run restricts the budget to the dead structure's own neighbourhood — survivors within `r_dead + r_survivor + 2` cells — and adds
the category the lattice needs: a death with **no live neighbour at all**.

## Correction to X127, and it cuts both ways

**X127 overstated absorption** (84.5 / 78.7 / 75.7 % → 55.3 / 47.8 / 48.4 %) because it counted the world's growth, not the neighbour's. Its "three of four deaths
are absorptions" is withdrawn. What survives is the *direction* (absorption is the largest single fate) but the magnitude was the world's book-keeping, not a
transfer.

**And X127's verdict was too generous to the maze.** Its registered dichotomy could not see the category that matters here: **isolated** deaths — a structure that
dies with nothing next to it. With the local instrument the maze is the *more lethal* habitat after all: real vanishings rise **10.5 % → 21.7 % → 27.4 %** of all
deaths, i.e. **55 → 183 → 302 events**, a factor of 5.5 from the lattice to the maze, while the lattice's own deaths are three times more often isolated (31.3 %
against 21.2 %). So the honest sentence is not "the kind of death does not change" — it is:

> **everywhere about half of a lineage's endings are absorptions; what changes is the other half — the lattice's structures die alone, the maze's are destroyed.**

The unclear band collapsed from 7–9 % to 1–3 %, which is the direct measure of how much sharper the local instrument is: the world-level version was leaving real
deaths in the residual.

## The kinship answer, which X128 could not give

X128's partner coverage was 27 % / 51 % / 69 % with the world-level budget and its kin shares rested on those subsets. With the local budget the coverage is
290/443, 403/664 and 531/834 and the contrast becomes decisive:

- in the lattice, **87.2 %** of absorptions go to kin and **more than half to siblings** — a split's daughters settle next to each other and re-merge, so a
  lineage's name-loss is a family book-keeping event;
- in both percolating cells, **61–62 %** of absorptions go to **strangers** — a structure that disappears inside the body is taken by whatever surrounds it.

That is the sharpest result of the night's consequence work, because it says what the percolating habitat does to a lineage: not only does it destroy more of them
outright, the ones it does not destroy are **handed to non-relatives**, so whatever a lineage was cannot continue in the thing that took its cells.

## What this means for the conditions of life

X126's trade-off and X127's "name not cells" are now both narrower and more useful:

1. **The intermediate habitat is the best for a large lineage** because its identities end least often (844 against 1 102) and its vanishings least often in
   absolute terms (183), while its generations still run faster than the lattice's.
2. **The lattice is a family world**: structures die alone or are re-absorbed by siblings; nothing much eats anything, and nothing much travels either (X126: depth
   +1.479, the lowest of the three).
3. **The maze is a market of strangers**: identities end most often, a quarter of the endings destroy the cells, and the cells that survive change hands outside the
   family. Depth is highest there (+2.213) precisely because the body can be cut into new pieces, and persistence is lowest (+0.1372) because those pieces belong to
   whoever was next to them.

For a "digital environment where life could persist", that is a concrete condition: **persistence needs structures that are close enough to shield each other and
separate enough that what takes their cells is their own kin.** The lattice has the kinship and lacks the proximity; the maze has the proximity and destroys the
kinship; the middle cell has some of both.

## Caveats

- The neighbourhood radius is `r_dead + r_survivor + 2` cells, one declared number; a smaller `+0` would raise the isolated share and a larger `+4` would lower it.
- Area is the logged area above the 0.20 cut, so a structure that falls below the cut is indistinguishable from one that vanished; that is the main residual risk and
  it applies to the vanished share.
- Ten worlds per cell, one seed set, one box. The kin contrast (87 % against 38 %) is far outside its interval, but the absorbed-share contrast between lattice and
  maze (55.3 % against 48.4 %) barely excludes overlap and should not be quoted alone.
