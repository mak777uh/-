# X189 — is the bridge between the bodies weaker when the site is warm? (pre-registration, 04.11)

**Status:** pre-registered, not yet run.

## 1. Manipulation is connected

X186 found the per-cell association between the outcome and the local inflow at the colder body to be positive in 9 of 11 cells —
the law's direction — with both exceptions at the largest measured radius (r_eff 5.86, gaps 7 and 9). X187 reproduced that flip
under selection with the same manipulation (radius 3 gap 10: +0.67; radius 6 gap 9: −0.33) but its registered mechanism readout
turned out to be the outcome restated, and X188's corrected, continuation-based count found **no** size-specific difference in the
bodies around the pair (warm-minus-cold +0.234 at radius 6, below the registered bar of 0.5). The registered refusal rule of X188
therefore moves the question to the **field** rather than to more component counting, which is what this item does.

The hypothesis to test comes from the line's own earlier measurement: X170 found that the field between two bodies at contact is
**less filled the stronger the source** — 0.52 / 0.48 / 0.39 of the body peak as the nominal rate rose. If a warm site leaves a
weaker bridge, then at the largest size the pair can touch and separate again instead of merging, and the local-inflow law's sign
inverts without any new bodies being involved.

## 2. Positive control fires

The control fires if (a) every run's recomputed local inflow at the colder body lands inside its level (tolerance 0.0025) —
registered in X187 and achieved by the same seed arithmetic, (b) the two levels differ by at least **0.008** in `F_local` at both
arms, (c) the body peak on the saved slices is at least **0.3**, so the bridge ratio's denominator is not noise, and (d) at least
two runs per group show a **nonzero** minimum field value on the segment between the bodies at some saved step, so the segment
readout is not identically zero. Thresholds: 0.0025, 0.008, 0.3, two runs.

## 3. Readout exists and varies

Primary: on each saved slice, the **waist ratio** — the minimum of the field `v` along the straight segment between the two
initial seeded centres, divided by the maximum of `v` over the two discs of radius 3 around those centres. Secondary: the same
ratio's minimum over the saved slices (the tightest moment), and the sample at which it occurs. The segment and the discs use the
**initial** positions, which the bodies have not left at these steps (X187's contact time was about step 70 for radius 6).

The readout must vary inside a group (the samples differ) and must not be a deterministic function of the outcome: unlike X187,
the waist is measured on the same slices whether or not the run later ends as one component, and the aggregate records the
cross-tabulation of "waist below its median" against "fused" so a repeat of that mistake names itself.

## 4. Sensitivity arithmetic, and the prediction

12 runs (2 arms × 2 levels × 3 seeds), 400 steps, 7 saved slices each, so 84 slices and 84 waist ratios. X170's span of 0.52 to
0.39 across its whole rate range is 0.13, so a difference of **0.05** in waist ratio between two levels of one arm is at the scale
the phenomenon has shown, and it is the registered bar. Registered **before the run**:

- **radius 6 gap 9:** the warm level's waist ratio at the tightest saved slice is at least **0.05 lower** than the cold level's
  (a weaker bridge where the pair coalesces less);
- **radius 3 gap 10:** the difference is in the **same direction** but **smaller than 0.05**, because there the warm level
  coalesces more — the bridge is weaker too, but a small body's contact is not rejected by it.

## 5. Refusal rule

If the radius-6 difference is below 0.05, the item is recorded as **the bridge is not weaker in a warm site** and the flip's
mechanism remains unexplained by both the bodies around the pair (X188) and the bridge between them (this item); what is left is
the bodies' own boundary dynamics, which needs a different probe and is written up as an open question rather than as another
counting item. If the radius-3 difference is at least 0.05 and larger than the radius-6 one, the item is recorded as **the size
dependence is in the geometry, not in the bridge** and X170's reading is extended rather than replaced. If the body peak check
fails, the item is recorded as **readout invalid** and no claim is made.

## 6. What would kill it

A waist ratio is one number for a whole segment, so a bridge that is thin in one place and thick in another reads as thin
everywhere; the segment's mean and its 90th percentile are reported beside the minimum, and a disagreement between them is
reported rather than resolved. The saved slices are every 20-100 steps, so a bridge that forms and breaks between two slices is
invisible — the tightest slice is therefore read together with the component log's minimum count and its sample. Three seeds per
group make each level's mean a direction, not a point: the registered bar is a difference of means over three runs, and the
per-run values are reported so the reader can see the spread rather than the mean alone.

## 7. Implementation notes

Runner `scripts/x189_bridge.py` reuses X187's seed arithmetic and runs the four groups with `--save-fields` at steps
40/60/80/100/140/200/300 into `_hourly/x189_fields/` (gitignored, 96×96 float32 slices). Analysis
`scripts/x189_bridge_analyse.py` recomputes each run's level from the logged first sample, reads the slices, and reports the waist
ratios with their spread for the four groups. Aggregate `morphogenesis/experiments/E09_pond/l1_field_x189_bridge.json`. Cost about
ten minutes of CPU.

## 8. Amendments made while reading the data (04.11; the bar and the prediction are unchanged)

1. **The bodies drift, so the frame follows them.** The plan assumed the pieces stay near their seeded centres ("the bodies have
   not left at these steps"). They do not: the field's maximum is already about five cells from the seeded centre at step 40, i.e.
   0.12 cells a step. The segment and the discs are therefore taken at the bodies' **current** centres, read from the component log
   at each saved step. Made before any between-group comparison produced a number at all (the first pass returned nothing usable).
2. **The bodies are rings, so the peak is taken over a radius-10 disc.** A disc of radius 3 around the logged centroid contains the
   ring's **hollow**, where the field is background: the peak over it read 0.10-0.15 against the registered floor of 0.3, so no
   slice was usable and every group came out empty. The peak is now the maximum over a radius-10 disc, which contains the ring, and
   the bridge is read midway between the centres. Still before any between-group comparison existed.
3. **The registered "tightest saved slice" summary is reported but not relied on.** The tightest ratio lands on step 40 — the
   seed-independent initial profile — in 9 of 12 runs (6 of them in the radius-3 arm). This was found **after** seeing the
   registered summary's between-group contrast, so it is a post-hoc correction and is recorded as one: the registered number
   (−0.087 at radius 6, +0.004 at radius 3) stays visible in the aggregate beside the contact-moment reading
   (−0.202 at radius 6, +0.090 at radius 3), which is the last saved slice on which the bodies are still separate.
4. **"Tracks the outcome" is not a degeneracy test here.** X187's readout was an identity by construction; an empirical association
   between a continuous readout and the outcome is a finding. The analysis therefore reports Fisher's p for "above or below the
   pooled median" against the outcome (0.88) instead of a degeneracy verdict; the degeneracy test that matters for X187 is kept in
   X188's aggregate, where the readout there is genuinely not a function of the outcome.
