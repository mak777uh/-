# X112b — the cut moves the level a little and the specificity not at all

**Plan:** [`2026-11-02-x112b-cuts-series-plan.md`](2026-11-02-x112b-cuts-series-plan.md) (21:44).
**Aggregate:** `l1_field_x112b_cuts.json`. Twenty runs (seeds 301–310 × thresholds 0.15 and 0.25), `F = 0.030`, X100's flags otherwise.

## Why this design is paired

`--threshold` belongs to the observer, not to the physics: it decides which pixels count as a structure, not how the field evolves. The
ten seeds at each cut are therefore **the same twenty worlds** X108 measured at 0.20, and the 0.20 comparison is X108's own number
(`ρ₁ = 0.548`, gap 0.302) rather than a borrow from another sample. X112 died trying to get this for free off saved fields; this is the
route that works.

## The asymmetry is the result

The line now has two different statements about how fragile its numbers are, and they point in different directions:

| perturbation | what happens to the **level** | what happens to the **specificity gap** |
|---|---|---|
| changing the *kind* of definition (X102: excursion set → drainage basin) | falls 0.531 → 0.300, 0.480 → 0.110 | **collapses**, 0.312 / 0.215 → 0.017 / −0.010 |
| changing the *cut* of the same definition (X112b) | −0.006 and −0.103 | **holds**, 0.284 / 0.302 / 0.326 |

So specificity is not a fragile quantity in general — it is robust to *where* the excursion level sits and not robust to *whether the
definition is an excursion at all*. That is a cleaner statement than either result alone: the mother-versus-stranger contrast depends on
the object being an excursion set, and once it is one, moving the level by ±25 % does not change it.

A second, smaller finding: **a higher cut produces more pairs, not fewer** (3 161 at 0.25 against 2 262 at 0.15). Cutting higher
fragments structures, and fragments divide more often than they disappear — the median area falls 118 → 49 cells while the genealogy
gets denser. That is worth remembering whenever a cut is chosen for convenience.

The direction X112's four failed anchoring schemes suggested (higher at 0.15, lower at 0.25) was **half right**: the 0.25 part held,
the 0.15 part did not (+0.006, not higher). Recorded because it was written down before the runs.

## What this does and does not change

- **Does:** the level's cut sensitivity in this cell is **13 %** of its value between 0.15 and 0.25 (0.554 → 0.445), far gentler than the
  half-life's factor of three (X99). Any statement about the *level* still quotes its cut, but the correction is small in this cell.
- **Does not:** X102's conclusion. The specificity's dependence is on the definition kind, and no cut tested here recovers or removes it.
- **Is still open:** the maze cell (0.040) was not swept — the same twenty-run design there is the natural continuation, and X99's
  reversal between inflows at the 0.25 cut says the maze may behave differently.

## Caveats

- One cell (`F = 0.030`), ten worlds per cut; coefficients on 1 874–2 854 pairs, SE 0.019–0.023.
- The −0.103 at 0.25 is just past the registered 0.10 bar: the honest phrasing is "the level moves by about a tenth", not "the level
  collapses".
- The specificity gap at 0.25 rests on 3 161 pairs but its stranger control is drawn from the same frame, so a higher cut changes both
  sides of the comparison at once — which is presumably why it holds.
- The worlds are X108's; this is a re-measurement of them at another cut, not a new sample.
