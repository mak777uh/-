# X150 — a third radius: does the boundary keep moving inward, and does the time stay put?

**Why.** X147 turned a single comparison into a small law with two halves that disagree. The **time** to mutual
coalescence is the surface gap's: 39 and 49 logged steps at gaps 7 and 8 on both radius 3 and radius 4.5, identical to
the step. The **boundary** where coalescence stops being universal is not: radius 3 holds 5/5 out to a gap of 8 cells
and breaks at 9, radius 4.5 already breaks at 8. One comparison cannot say whether that shift is a trend, a step, or an
accident of two radii — a third radius can.

The two radii measured differ by 1.5 cells of radius (a factor 2.2 in area) and the boundary moved by 1 cell in gap. A
linear reading of that predicts radius 6 (area 113 cells) to break at a gap of **7**: all five seeds coalescing at 6,
mixed at 7.

## 1. Manipulation is connected

Same probe (`--init-spots R:SEP`, seed-dependent placement, anchored bit-for-bit by X142's probe control), same inflow
`F = 0.030`, same seeds 601–605, same 6 000 steps, same dense logging every 10 steps, same readout (`world_row`, no
distance tolerance). The manipulation is the **surface gap** at a third radius, so the radius is the variable that
changes between arms and the gap the variable that changes within them.

| arm | radius | gap | separation | runs |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `x150_g6_r60` | 6.0 | 6 | 18 | 5 new |
| `x150_g7_r60` | 6.0 | 7 | 19 | 5 new |
| `x150_g8_r60` | 6.0 | 8 | 20 | 5 new |

## 2. Positive control fires (numeric)

Four controls. **The control fires if** all four hold:

1. **The timing law repeats a third time.** Median time to mutual coalescence in the arms that are 5/5 M must be **29**
   steps at gap 6, **39** at gap 7 and **49** at gap 8 — the same numbers radius 3 and radius 4.5 gave, to the logging
   quantum. This is the strong prediction: it does not depend on how many seeds coalesce.
2. **Every arm starts as two components** of area ≥ 20 cells at the first logged step (area at radius 6 is expected
   around 113 cells for the pair).
3. **No arm ends empty** (occupancy ≥ 5 % of the grid at the last step).
4. **The medians are printed with their n**, and the number of coalescing seeds is stated per arm.

## 3. Readout exists and varies

The class already spans all three values in this population (M at the inner gaps, A and P outward), and the readout is
the same one that put the two earlier radii apart; at radius 6 the expected transition falls inside the three arms, so
the readout has room to vary within the sweep.

## 4. Sensitivity arithmetic

Five seeds per arm at one-cell gap spacing, three arms: the resolution is one cell and one seed, and the declared
minimal effect is the difference between "5/5 M" and "at most 3/5 M" — two seeds — which is exactly the difference the
two earlier radii showed across one cell of gap. The shift is therefore measured by the same yardstick that produced it.

## 5. Refusal rule (fixed before the run)

**Void** if control 1 fails in the arms that are 5/5 M (the timing law must repeat, otherwise the comparison across
radii is not the same world), if any arm fails to start as two components, or if any arm ends empty.

The registered outcomes:

- **The shift is a trend**: radius 6 coalesces 5/5 at gap 6 and is mixed at 7 (at most 4/5 M). Combined with radius 3
  (break at 9) and radius 4.5 (break at 8) the boundary moves inward by about half a cell per unit of radius, and the
  time stays fixed throughout — two laws, one of timing and one of packing, measured on three structures.
- **The shift is a threshold that saturates**: radius 6 is still 5/5 at gap 7 and mixed at 8, i.e. the boundary stops
  following the radius after 4.5. Then the inward move is a property of the *disc pair* up to some size and something
  else takes over, and the report must say where it stops.
- **The shift accelerates**: radius 6 is already mixed at gap 6 (at most 4/5 M). Then the boundary falls faster than
  linearly and the next step is a finer radius ladder, not a fourth radius.
- **Indeterminate**: an arm is exactly 4/5 M at the boundary and the neighbouring arm is 5/5. Then one seed separates
  the arms and the sweep is unresolved at this resolution; the next run doubles the seeds rather than adding a radius.
