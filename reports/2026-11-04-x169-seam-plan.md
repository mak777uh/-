# X169 — The seam, from the field: does a merged body still have two centres? (pre-registration, 04.11)

**Status:** pre-registered, not yet run.

## 1. Manipulation is connected

X167b ended with a negative that it could not turn into a positive: components cannot say whether a fused body is the same
object as its parts, because any elongated blob divides at its ends and produces the same geometry. The only remaining
discriminator is the **field**: if the merge preserved the pair's structure, the merged body is not a single smooth bump but
carries two maxima with a saddle between them — a seam at the old contact. If the merge erased it, the body is one bump and
the seam is gone.

This item seeds the block's standard fusing pair (radius 3, surface gap 8, separation 14), samples the field every 10 steps,
and measures the profile of the field along the line joining the two original parent centres — the axis on which a seam must
lie. Nothing in the world's code changes; the field is only written out.

## 2. Positive control fires

The control is a single body grown from the default seed in the same world at the same steps, dumped the same way. Its
profile along an arbitrary diameter must be **unimodal**, with a bimodality index (the saddle ratio of §3) at least 0.9. The
positive control fires if the single body's index is ≥ 0.9 on every sampled step; if it is not, the measure is not
discriminating a seam but detecting noise, and the item stops.

## 3. Readout exists and varies

For each field dump, the profile v(s) is sampled along the segment joining the two original parent centres (20 points), and
the **bimodality index** is the minimum of the profile between the two outermost local maxima, divided by the larger of those
two maxima. An index near 1 means one smooth bump (no seam); an index well below 1 means two centres with a saddle between
them (a seam). The readout must vary: across dumps within a run the index must move by at least 0.1 (standard deviation
≥ 0.03), otherwise nothing is being measured.

## 4. Sensitivity arithmetic

Before the fusion the index is trivially low (two separate bodies, a deep saddle). After the fusion, if the seam persists it
should stay below the single-body control's 0.9 by a visible margin; the question is by how much. The profile is sampled at 20
points over a segment of 14 cells, i.e. every 0.7 cells, and the field's interface width is about one to two cells (the
pattern wavelength is 6 to 8 cells), so a saddle of 10 % depth is resolvable. Three seeds per arm are enough for a
first-look screen of a yes/no distinction; a false negative is the risk, and the refusal rule says so.

## 5. Refusal rule

If the positive control does not fire at 0.9, the item reports the measure's failure and stops. If the post-fusion index is
statistically indistinguishable from the single-body control's across all seeds and all post-fusion dumps, the item is
recorded as **the seam is gone: the merged body is one bump, the merge erases the pair's structure, and the language of
identity leaves the fusion work for good.** If the post-fusion index is lower than the control's by more than the run-to-run
spread, the seam survives and the fusion line has its first field-level result.

## 6. What would kill it

A post-fusion index inside the single-body control's range would kill the seam hypothesis. Conversely, a control whose index
sits at 0.75 rather than 0.9 would mean the measure has no headroom and the item cannot conclude either way — that is the
failure mode this design is most exposed to, and the reason the control is checked on every sampled step rather than once.

## 7. Implementation notes

Runner `scripts/x169_seam.py` (two arms × three seeds, 1 500 steps, `--sample-every 10`, `--save-fields` every 10 steps,
fields written to a gitignored directory), analysis `scripts/x169_seam_analyse.py` (profile sampling, bimodality index per
dump, index trajectory around the fusion and the next division, control comparison). Aggregate
`morphogenesis/experiments/E09_pond/l1_field_x169_seam.json`. The field dumps are large and stay local; the aggregate carries
the numbers and the seeds.
