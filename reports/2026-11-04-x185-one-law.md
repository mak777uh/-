# X185 — one law, two windows (04.11)

**Question.** X182 controlled the pattern value at the pair's **midpoint** and found the share falling 1.00 → 0.00; X184 controlled
the local inflow at the **colder body** and found it rising 0.00 → 1.00. Both were pre-registered, both are highly significant,
and their manipulated variables have opposite signs. Two laws, or one?

**Result.** One. Put both experiments on the colder body's inflow — the axis X184 identified — and they agree line by line:

| colder-body F_local | coalesced | share | of which X182 | of which X184 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.000-0.019 | 0 of 22 | 0.00 | 10 | 12 |
| 0.019-0.024 | 5 of 11 | 0.45 | 6 | 5 |
| 0.024-0.028 | 1 of 2 | 0.50 | 1 | 1 |
| 0.028-0.032 | 15 of 15 | 1.00 | 9 | 6 |
| 0.032-0.040 | 10 of 10 | 1.00 | 4 | 6 |

The reason is in the design: `--init-spots` places the two bodies 16 cells apart, which is 120 degrees of the pattern's 12-cell
period, so a cold midpoint implies warm bodies. That is why X182's coldest midpoint level coalesced 1.00 while its warmest
coalesced 0.00 — and it is why a covariate chosen by intuition gave the wrong sign in X180.

**Controls.** Both source experiments' own registered controls: X182's levels recomputed inside their bands and its extremes
0.87 apart in G; X184's extremes 0.0210 apart in F_local with every run's
recomputed level inside tolerance.

**Limits.** This is a comparison of two controlled experiments, not a new measurement; it inherits the min-versus-mean ambiguity
that X183 could not resolve, and the bins resting on one or two runs carry no weight on their own.

**Next hypotheses.** The mechanism behind the two signs at once (a warm body grows toward the gap; a warm gap may nucleate bodies
of its own) is a candidate for a short series with dense field logging; and the radius-6 cliff should be re-read in the local
inflow before any new series is built. Artifact: `morphogenesis/experiments/E09_pond/l1_field_x185_one_law.json`.
