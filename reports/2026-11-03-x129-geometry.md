# X129 — who lives next door: the neighbour is the same distance away in every cell and a different relative

**Plan:** [`2026-11-03-x129-geometry-plan.md`](2026-11-03-x129-geometry-plan.md) (05:24).
**Aggregate:** `l1_field_x129_geometry.json`.

## What it asked

X128b found the absorber is kin in 87.2 % of lattice absorptions and a stranger in 61–62 % of the percolating ones. The deflationary explanation: daughters are born
adjacent, so in a world of isolated spots the only structure close enough to take a dying one is its own sibling — the "family world" would then be a restatement of
"the spots are far apart". The plan tested it by matching on **contact distance** `δ` (centroid distance divided by the sum of the two radii).

All controls held: the partner count reproduces X128b exactly (290 / 403 / 531), kin are nearer than strangers in the lattice (0.656 against 0.724), heredity and
end-survival reproduce their sources, and the band holds at least 100 absorptions in every cell.

## The matching degenerated, and the degeneracy is the finding

**Every partnered absorption is a contact event**: 100 % fall inside `δ ≤ 1.5`, so there is no "outside the band" population to compare against and the matched
design cannot separate anything — partly because the neighbourhood itself was defined as contact plus two cells, which makes the band a tautology. That defect is
recorded and the rule is written down: *a variable that the instrument's own definition fixes cannot be used to match*.

What the degeneracy leaves is stronger than the matching would have been. Absorption happens at the **same contact geometry in all three cells** (median `δ` 0.66–0.72,
i.e. the two structures overlap), the dying structure has **one neighbour within reach** in every cell (median neighbourhood size 1.0, X128b), and the absorber is
not appreciably closer or bigger when it is kin than when it is a stranger (the areas differ by 20 %, in the same direction as the contrast but far too small to
carry a 50-point difference). **At the same distance, with the same kind of neighbour, the genealogy of that neighbour differs by 49.6 points between habitats.**

So the deflationary explanation is not supported, and the positive statement is sharper than "kinship matters":

> **The lattice segregates lineages in space — a dying structure's only neighbour is its own sibling. The percolating body mixes them — the only neighbour is a
> stranger. What breaks a lineage's continuity in the maze is not distance and not destruction alone, it is spatial mixing.**

For the question this line exists to answer, that is the most concrete condition produced so far: persistence requires that a structure's neighbour be its kin,
which requires the habitat to keep families spatially apart — and percolation is exactly the mechanism that stops it.

## What this does to the earlier chain

- X128b's kin contrast survives a serious deflationary test: it is not a distance effect, not a size effect, and not a count-of-neighbours effect.
- X126's trade-off gains its mechanism: the maze buys generations (+2.213) by making the body cuttable, and pays with persistence (+0.1372) because cuttability
  means mixing, and mixing means a lineage's cells are taken by someone else.
- The middle cell (39.2 % kin) is between the two, as its survival (+0.2984, the highest) would require.

## Caveats

- 100 % inside the band means the band statistic and the overall statistic are the same number; the report quotes it once, not twice.
- `δ` uses `sqrt(area/π)` as a radius, which over-states the radius of an irregular structure; the median below 1 therefore means "the two bounding circles overlap",
  not "the cells overlap".
- One seed set (301–310), ten worlds per cell, one box, the 0.20 cut; the intervals are bootstrap over absorptions.
- The kinship label is genealogical: two siblings are also two neighbouring structures, and nothing here says what the absorbed cells carry.
