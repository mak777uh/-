**The floor is confirmed** (the plan's saturation test): gaps 1, 2 and 4 give medians of **1, 1 and 10 steps**, a spread of 9 against a declared 10-step resolution. At contact the merge happens at the first logged step, i.e. the pairing is a single unstable structure from the outset. The rise then belongs to the range 8–10 cells (40, 50, 79 steps), where it roughly doubles towards the crossing.

**And the 11-cell arm is a third fate, not a separation.** In all five seeds **both** seeded uids end, neither is absorbed by the other, and each leaves **no descendants at all** (subtree size 1 for both) while the world itself stays alive. At a five-cell gap the two structures do not merge and do not coexist: they **annihilate**.

So the outcome as a function of the gap is not monotone in kind:

| gap | outcome |
|---|---|
| 1–4 cells | merge, at the logging quantum |
| **5 cells** | **both lineages destroyed, 5/5 seeds** |
| 8–10 cells | merge, slower and slower (40 → 79 steps) |
| ≥ 11 cells | both survive, separate |

## What this changes

1. **The readout had a blind spot and the run exposed it.** "Not absorbed and not both alive" was being counted as "kept apart" — the first pass printed the nonsense verdict "mixing length between 14 and 11, non-monotone at 11". The analysis now has an explicit `unresolved` class and excludes such an arm from the crossing, which moved the bracket back to **14–16** where it belongs (X136's crossing is 16).
2. **The inner edge of the mixing length is not where the merging stops being gentle — it is where merging turns into mutual destruction**, at least at this one gap. A five-cell gap is 5/12 of a wavelength; the two inhibitory halos overlap enough to suppress each other's autocatalysis, and neither survives to fuse.
3. **The annihilation band is unmapped.** One point is not a band: gaps 3, 5, 6 and 7 at one-cell spacing are the obvious next sweep, and until they are run the report must not say how wide the band is.

## Controls

The reused arms reproduce X136 exactly (medians 19, 49, 79 and the same per-seed values), every new arm starts as two components, every run is alive under the structural rule (including the annihilation arm, whose world is alive while both seeded lineages are not), and the plan's monotonicity clause is what flagged the anomaly for inspection rather than smoothing it.

## Caveats

- One inflow, one radius (3 cells), five seeds per point; the annihilation is seen at a single gap and at the logging resolution of 10 steps.
- The "both lost" reading is genealogical: both seeded uids end without issue. Whether the cells die or are absorbed by structures that are not the partner is not distinguished by this readout — at a five-cell gap the partner should be the only candidate, but that is an inference.
- Nine separations in the analysis, but the reused ones come from a different series with the same probe and flags.

## Correction, 03.11 16:05 — what "the world stays alive" means in this arm

The controls paragraph says every run is alive, including the annihilation arm. That is not what the aggregate
records: its `live` flag is the structural rule — total area ≥ 5 % of the grid **and** a largest component ≥ 50
cells — and it is **false for one seed of the annihilation arm** (separation 11, seed 602: 66 components, largest
37 cells) and for one seed at separation 14. The field does not empty in either case; occupancy at the last step is
about a quarter of the grid, but it fragments into small patches and the 50-cell rule then reads it as not alive.
The sentence was true of the emptiness, not of the rule. The corrected statement is: **the field never empties, and
it passes the structural liveness rule in 4 of 5 seeds** of the annihilation arm.

Nothing in the conclusion moves — both lineages still end with no descendants, which is what the arm is about — but
the control is weaker than it was described, and the readout that would have caught this earlier is the fragmentation
count per arm, not the boolean.

The same inspection found a second thing worth writing down: **X136's full logs for separations 12, 15 and 18 were
retired before the compact projections existed** (only 14, 16 and 17 were compacted), so the gap-6 point — the
merging neighbour on the other side of the hole — cannot be recomputed from artifacts, only quoted from the
aggregate. X142 re-runs it rather than quoting it.
