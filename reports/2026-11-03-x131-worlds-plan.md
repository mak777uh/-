# X131 — which cell differences survive resampling **worlds**?

**Why.** Every interval published in this block — the absorbed share, the isolated share, the kin share, the re-merge share, the survival and depth contrasts — was
bootstrapped over **pairs** or **deaths** inside ten worlds. That answers "how precisely is this measured given these ten worlds", not "would another ten worlds say
the same". The distinction decides the three claims the block rests on: that the middle cell has the fewest collisions (8.6 % against 18.4 % and 17.4 %), that the
percolating cells hand their absorptions to strangers (61–62 % against 12.8 %), and that the maze destroys more (27.4 % against 10.5 %). If the world-level spread
swallows those gaps, the claims are about these ten seeds and must say so.

No new runs: the thirty dense logs already on disk. This is the check the block's own caveats have been promising.

## 1. Manipulation is connected

Ten worlds per cell. Each statistic is computed **per world**, then bootstrapped over the ten worlds (2 000 resamples with replacement). The published value must
reproduce the aggregate it comes from, and the pair-level interval must be reported next to it for comparison.

| statistic | defined per world as |
|---|---|
| absorbed / isolated / vanished share | X128b's local budget, counts of fates over that world's deaths |
| kin share of absorptions | X128b's partner rule and X128b's kinship, over that world's partnered absorptions |
| re-merge share | X130's clock, over that world's sister pairs |
| end-survival contrast | X126's readout, mean over that world's sister pairs |
| depth contrast | X113's readout, mean over that world's sister pairs |

## 2. Positive control fires (numeric)

Four controls. **The control fires if** all four hold:

1. **Every statistic reproduces its published aggregate** within 0.005 (the means over worlds equal the pooled values up to weighting, which is stated).
2. **The world-level interval is wider than the pair-level one** in every cell and statistic — if it is not, the per-world values are degenerate (all ten identical)
   or the bootstrap is wrong, and the run is void.
3. **Every world contributes**: no cell may have a statistic defined in fewer than 8 of its 10 worlds; a statistic with more missing worlds is reported as
   unavailable, not as an interval.
4. **Heredity** `ρ₁ ≥ 0.35` per cell (sanity that the same worlds are being read).

## 3. Readout exists and varies

Per cell and statistic: the ten per-world values (printed in full), the world-level 95 % interval, the pair-level interval, and the ratio of the two widths. Variation
across worlds is a fact to be measured, not assumed.

## 4. Sensitivity arithmetic

Ten worlds per cell means the world-level bootstrap has a resolution of roughly ±0.3 of the cell's own spread (SE ≈ sd/√10). The declared decision rule:
a difference between cells is **world-level** if the two world-level intervals do not overlap; **seed-bound** if they overlap; and **undecided** if the point
estimates differ by less than half the average world-level half-width.

## 5. Refusal rule (fixed before the run)

**Void** if a published aggregate is not reproduced, or a world-level interval comes out narrower than the pair-level one, or a cell has fewer than 8 worlds with the
statistic defined.

**World-level** and **seed-bound** are both real results and are reported as such. No cell difference may be quoted as a property of the habitat unless it is
world-level; a seed-bound difference is quoted with "in these ten worlds" attached.
