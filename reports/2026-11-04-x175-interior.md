# X175 — The ninth cell breaks the single curve (04.11)

**Question.** X174 put eight arm-cells on one line — the corridor at contact falls as the arm's fusion share rises, Pearson
−0.95 — and that line explained both of the block's earlier results. A relation fitted to eight non-independent cells needs a
cell inside the (size, gap) plane that neither family covered. **Radius 4.5 at surface gap 9** was that cell: radius 4.5 had
only been run at gap 10 (share 0.27, corridor 0.49) and radius 3 at gap 9 (share 0.80, corridor 0.41), so interpolation gave a
predicted share near 0.6 and a predicted corridor of 0.42-0.46.

Pre-registration: [`2026-11-04-x175-interior-plan.md`](2026-11-04-x175-interior-plan.md). Fifteen seeds (1061-1075), 2 000 steps.

## Result: the arm was not where the interpolation put it

| readout | predicted | observed | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| corridor at contact | 0.42-0.46 | **0.437** (sd 0.045, 4 contact events) | hit |
| fusion share | 0.45-0.72 | **4/15 = 0.27** | **miss** |

The pre-registered rule applies as written: with the share outside its interval the arm sits elsewhere on the curve than
intended, so the corridor's agreement is **not** evidence about the slope. But the raw numbers say something sharper than "a
placement miss", and it is worth stating because it is the item's real finding.

## At the same share, different arms read different corridors

Three arms now sit at share 0.27, and their corridors are not the same:

| arm | share | corridor at contact |
|---|---|---|
| radius 4.5, gap 9 | 0.27 | **0.437** |
| radius 4.5, gap 10 | 0.27 | 0.492 |
| radius 3, gap 10, `F` = 0.026 | 0.27 | 0.516 |

Same size and same share for the first two, one cell of gap apart: **0.437 against 0.492**, a difference of 0.055 with
within-arm spreads of 0.045 and 0.022. So the corridor is not a function of the share alone. The third cell adds the rate axis:
the same share with a colder source reads 0.516.

That is enough to refute the single-curve reading of X174, and it leaves a two-factor picture that fits every cell measured so
far:

1. **marginality (share)** — arms close to their crossing fuse only in their luckiest runs, and those merges happen with a
   more-built bridge (the selection effect X174 identified);
2. **the distance the bridge spans (initial gap)** — at the same share, a closer pair merges with a less-filled bridge than a
   farther pair (0.44 against 0.49 at one cell of gap).

The rate enters through both: it changes the share and, at fixed share, still shifts the corridor (0.52 in the cold arm).

## What this does to X174's line

X174's eight-cell line is **a mixture of two effects that happen to run together** in the cells the block had: the arms with
high share are also the arms with short gaps, and the arms at the crossing are also the arms with long gaps or high rates. The
ninth cell, chosen to sit inside the plane, broke the mixture. The correlation was real as a description of those eight cells and
is not a law.

What survives from the last four items is a weaker but real statement: **a merge happens when the field between the bodies is
between about 0.37 and 0.52 of the body peak**, and where inside that band a given merge falls is set by how marginal the pair
is and by how far apart the bodies started — not by a constant threshold, and not by the size of the bodies.

## Limits

Four contact events at this arm, so its median rests on very little; the sd 0.045 is the largest of any arm measured, which
makes the comparison with radius 4.5 at gap 10 suggestive rather than settled. The pre-registered interval for the share was
0.45-0.72 and the observation is 0.27, which is a large miss: the interpolation that produced it assumed the two axes add,
and this arm shows the share is much more sensitive to the gap than that assumption allowed — at radius 4.5, gap 9 fuses no
more often than gap 10 does.

## What would kill it

A cell inside the plane whose share *is* where interpolation says and whose corridor lands on the eight-cell line. The natural
one is radius 6 at gap 10, where the crossing is at 9: the share there should be low (0.1-0.2) and the two-factor picture
predicts a corridor of 0.50-0.55 (marginal, long bridge). If that arm lands near 0.47 instead, the gap effect is smaller than
this item's single comparison suggests.

## Next hypotheses

1. **Two factors, one prediction** — radius 6 at surface gap 10, where the crossing has already passed: predicted share 0.05-0.25
   and corridor 0.50-0.55 (X176).
2. **The share's sensitivity to the gap at a fixed size is much larger than assumed.** At radius 4.5, gaps 9 and 10 both fuse
   4 of 15 — the crossing for that size may be a plateau rather than a point, which the crossing's own report (X158) described
   as "gradual over two cells" without noticing the plateau.
3. **The band, measured directly.** If a merge requires the bridge to be within 0.37-0.52 of the peak, an arm run at a gap far
   below the crossing should show merges at the band's lower edge and nothing below it — a test that does not depend on where
   the crossing is.
