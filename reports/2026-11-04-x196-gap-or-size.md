# X196 — is the fusion window set by the body size or by the gap? (04.11)

**Question.** X195 measured **zero** fusion events in 288 worlds at radius 4 gap 12 and radius 5 gap 10, both with a centre separation
of 20 cells, and read the result as a window of body sizes. The window can equally be set by the separation, and the two are separable
by holding one and moving the other: radius 4 with the gap closed to 10 and 8 (separations 18 and 16), and radius 6 at a separation of
20 — the same separation as the fusion-free radius-4 reference — where its gap is 8. Preregistration
`reports/2026-11-04-x196-gap-or-size-plan.md`.

**Result: the gap is the binding variable, and X195's size window is corrected to a gap window.**

| radius | gap | separation | worlds | fused worlds | fusion share |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 4 (X195 reference) | 12 | 20 | 144 | 0 | **0.0** |
| 4 | 10 | 18 | 144 | 34 | **0.1883** |
| 6 (X192 reference) | 9 | 21 | 144 | 58 | **0.402** |
| 6 | 8 | 20 | 144 | 126 | **0.8338** |
| 4 | 8 | 16 | 144 | 138 | **0.9607** |

Ordered by the **gap** the table is monotone — 0.00, 0.19, 0.40, 0.83, 0.96 as the surfaces come from 12 cells apart to 8 — while ordered
by **radius** it is not: radius 4 fuses nothing at a separation of 20 and 0.96 at 16, and radius 6 fuses 0.83 at the same separation
of 20 where radius 4 fuses nothing, because its gap there is 8 rather than 12. So the variable that turns fusion off and on in this
band is the **distance between the surfaces**, and X195's reading of its two arms as a window of sizes was a reading of their gaps
(12 and 10 at a separation of 20) rather than of their radii. The size is not absent — at a matched gap of 8 the smaller body fuses
0.9607 and the larger 0.8338 — but it is a shift of that order, not the on/off variable.

All three registered criteria pass: the radius-4 share does not fall as the gap closes (0 → 0.1883 → 0.9607, the last two
above the 0.1 bar); radius 6 at a separation of 20 still fuses (126 of 144 worlds, above the gap-9 reference); and
the phase decides the bridge in every arm (spreads [0.42229, 0.49272, 0.35942] against a 0.05 bar).

**What this changes.** X195's conclusion — a window of sizes with no fusion — is **withdrawn** and replaced by a window of gaps: at these
sizes and this inflow, a pair whose surfaces are 12 cells apart does not fuse; at 10 about a fifth of phase classes do; at 9 about two
fifths; at 8 between five sixths and nineteen twentieths. That is also consistent with the earlier boundary work of X158, which placed
the fusion transition at a gap of about 10 cells at radius 3, 4.5 and 6 — the same variable the line is now measuring from the phase
end. The bridge part of the mechanism is untouched by the correction: the phase still decides the bridge at every arm, and the bridge's
association with the inflow still runs in the radius-6 direction.

**Limits.** The gap is changed by moving the bodies, which also changes which cells they occupy, so "the gap binds" is a statement about
this geometry band at one nominal inflow, and the measured gaps use the rasterised radii. Each arm is one representative world per
phase class. And the two radii are not compared at a *single* matched gap of 8 for every phase: the phase classes differ by radius
because the pattern is sampled at different positions, so the size shift quoted here is a difference between two such samples rather
than a controlled size contrast.

**Next hypotheses.** (1) The controlled size contrast at a fixed gap of 8 across three or four radii, which is the clean way to
measure the size shift the table above suggests. (2) The gap boundary at fixed radius over a finer grid of gaps (9, 10, 11, 12) with
every phase class, to see whether the transition is a step at 10 cells or a smooth curve — X158's earlier reading used seeds, not
classes. (3) The outcome term at a gap where the share is near one half, where a within-value contrast has the most power.
