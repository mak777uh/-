# X200 — is the "world" a cluster? Clone pairs at one (cell, phase) (05.11)

Pre-registration: [`2026-11-05-x200-clone-pairs-plan.md`](2026-11-05-x200-clone-pairs-plan.md) (committed before the registered
analysis; the reconnaissance that motivated it is disclosed there).
Aggregate: [`l1_field_x200_clone_pairs.json`](../morphogenesis/experiments/E09_pond/l1_field_x200_clone_pairs.json).
Code: [`scripts/x200_clone_pairs.py`](../scripts/x200_clone_pairs.py) (`--write`). Zero GPU: committed compact slices only.

## Question

X199 (§10jh) fitted a logistic random intercept per **world** — the arm's geometry together with the pair's phase (the two
seeded positions modulo the stripe period of 12 cells) — and reported σ = 8.368 and an **ICC of 0.955**, "essentially all of
the latent-scale variance is between worlds, not between repeats of a world". Its own report named the decisive test it could
not run: *two seeds at one (cell, phase); if they disagree, the world unit is not a cluster*. X200 runs that test.

## Sample

A **design group** is a maximal set of committed two-body slices with an identical parameter vector (the ten required keys:
`F`, `steps`, `grid`, `dt`, `Du`, `Dv`, `k`, `eps`, `threshold`, `sample_every`; the other nine are recorded when present).
Inside a group a **world** is the same key X199 used: (radius to 0.1, gap, F) × (sorted seeded positions mod 12).

| element | value |
|---|---|
| runs loaded (two-body at the first sample, all required keys present) | **3113** |
| skipped | 263 not two-body at the first sample; 3113 slices with at least one optional key absent (recorded, not used to split groups) |
| design groups | **9** |
| eligible groups (overall share strictly inside 0.15–0.85) | 6 |
| worlds with two or more runs in eligible groups (P1) | **841**, carrying 1950 runs |
| P2 = X199's observational pool (phase-selected arms excluded) | 742 runs, 108 worlds with repeats, 4 disagreeing |

## Result

**44 of the 841 worlds disagree in outcome (0.052, Wilson [0.0392, 0.0695])** — below the registered 10 %, and 2.15× the
beta-binomial prediction at ICC = 0.955 (registered bar: 3×). The ICC refitted on this pool is **0.9816** (σ 13.26; the σ
profile interval is [13.24, 15.03] and the ICC interval around the σ profile mode is [0.6698, 0.6906] — the two disagree
because the slope–σ ridge is flat, exactly the weakness X199 reported; the joint fit favours the larger value). **X199's
0.955 is not overturned.**

The disagreement is not spread out; it is in two cells:

By design group: 42 of 736 worlds at 400 steps / F = 0.03, 2 of 40 at 2000 steps / F = 0.03, 0 of 65 elsewhere.

## What the two disagreeing cells have in common

Both are the cells where the 144 seeds of the arm fall into **fewer phases than seeds**: at gap 9 into 108 phases, at gap 10
into 72 — i.e. every phase is covered by **two** seeds, and those two seeds are far apart in the domain (their phase matches
modulo 12, but their absolute positions differ by one or two stripe periods). The other six arms of the same series have 144
distinct phases and therefore no repeats at all (the 109 "other cells" repeats come from older series, where repeated phases
sit close together).

The descriptive check agrees with that reading. In a disagreeing world the median difference in the distance to the nearest
edge between the two runs is **6.0 cells**, in an agreeing world **0**; only **2.3 %** of the disagreeing worlds have both
runs at the same edge distance.

## Verdict

The label (cell, phase) is **not a sufficient statistic for the world**: it fixes the local inflow (which is exactly why R4
of X199 holds — the covariate cannot vary inside the label) and leaves the distance to the boundary free, and that free
coordinate moves the outcome. The world is *nearly* a cluster — 95 % of the eligible worlds with repeats agree, and the
recomputed ICC is at least as high as X199's — but the ~5 % that disagree do so where the two seeds of one phase sit at
different distances from the edge. Registered criteria C1 and C2 **fail**, C3 passes by its letter while its two intervals
contradict each other for the reason X199 already recorded, and C4 is descriptive and supports the boundary reading.

Consequences for our own record:

1. §10jh stands as written (σ 8.368, ICC 0.955, between-world slope) with one sentence added by this item: the label fixes the
   inflow and not the position, so an ICC computed on it is an ICC of a *labelled* world, not of a sealed environment.
2. §10jg's X198 curve keeps its shares, but the arm-level table now has a design footnote: at gaps 9 and 10 each phase was
   sampled by two seeds that are one to two stripe periods apart, so 42 of those 108 phases are inside-world mixtures. The
   curve is unchanged (the shares are computed over runs, as registered); what changes is the interpretation of the scatter.
3. X201 is registered as the interventional version: move one pair, at a fixed phase and fixed parameters, to different
   distances from the edge and read the outcome. The observational reading here is a correlation with a free coordinate; the
   intervention is what can turn it into a cause.

## Next hypotheses

1. **X201 — the intervention (registered next).** At a fixed phase and identical parameters, place the same pair at several
   distances from the boundary and read the fusion share against distance. If the share moves with the distance, the free
   coordinate is causal; if it does not, the X200 correlation is a confound of the two disagreeing arms.
2. **Stratify by edge distance.** Redo X199's ICC with a second random effect on the (rounded) edge distance band. If the
   ICC collapses when the band is included, the "world" variance is partly a position effect.
3. **A cleaner clone-pair design.** For a few worlds, choose seeds that keep the phase *and* the edge distance, and measure
   the true within-world disagreement rate without the position confound.
4. **Re-sample the disagreeing arms.** Run gaps 9 and 10 again with seeds chosen to give distinct phases (one seed per
   phase); if the new arm shares differ from 0.188 and 0.646, the original arms were partly measuring position.
