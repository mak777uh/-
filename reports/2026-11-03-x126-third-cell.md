# X126 — the third cell: the generation clock is a gradient, lineage depth is a threshold, and persistence peaks between them

**Plan:** [`2026-11-03-x126-third-cell-plan.md`](2026-11-03-x126-third-cell-plan.md) (03:45, amended 03:52 before the series).
**Aggregate:** `l1_field_x126_third_cell.json`. **Series:** `x126_f035_s301…310.json`, ten worlds, ~75–115 s each, 28 MB.

## What was asked

Every consequence number in this line came from two cells that differ by a factor of nearly two. Ten worlds at `F = 0.035` on X108's own seeds make a
**three-cell** comparison, paired by seed, and decide whether the habitat acts as a gradient in inflow or as a step at percolation. The midpoints and the
gradient/step rule were fixed before the data.

## Finding 1 — the generation clock moves continuously

The below-floor share goes 16.2 % → 30.2 % → 46.6 % and the tie-excluded fraction 0.526 → 0.472 → 0.388, both within one standard error of the midpoint. So
generations shorten **smoothly** with inflow and the larger sister's directional advantage decays smoothly with them. The maze is not a different kind of place:
it is the far end of one axis, and the third cell sits where the axis says it should. This also retires the worry that the maze's 47 % of unmeasurably short
generations was a property of that cell: it is the continuation of a trend that begins at the lattice.

## Finding 2 — lineage depth is a threshold, and the threshold is percolation

Depth is **+1.479 → +1.416 → +2.213**: the middle cell is indistinguishable from the lattice (its interval contains the lattice's point value) and sits 4.65 SE
below the chord to the maze. Half again as much food buys **nothing** in lineage depth; the jump happens only where the pattern percolates (largest component
935 cells against 441). This is the first readout in the line with a **threshold** character, and it is the sharpest thing the three-cell design produced: the
depth advantage is not a function of inflow, it is a function of the regime.

## Finding 3 — the rate accelerates, which is what Findings 1 and 2 predict

Rate = depth / generation time. If generations shorten in the middle cell while depth does not move, the rate must rise — and it does, from 6.06 to 9.01, below
the chord (3.19 SE). Then at the maze both the numerator and the denominator move and the rate jumps to 18.96. The three readouts are one story: **the clock
runs faster everywhere as the food rises; the lineage only deepens when the body percolates.**

## Finding 4 — and persistence reverses: the middle cell is the best habitat for a large lineage

End-survival of the larger branch is **+0.2496 → +0.2984 → +0.1372**, i.e. a maximum in the middle cell and 5.99 SE above the chord — the only readout that
leaves the range of its neighbours. The pair counts say the same in another currency: pairs where only the larger branch is alive rise 324 (28 %) → **413
(37 %)** → 178 (15 %), and pairs where **both** branches are alive collapse 641 (56 %) → 241 (22 %) → 40 (3 %).

Two honest qualifications. The **rise** from the lattice to the middle cell is not resolved — the intervals [0.221, 0.279] and [0.265, 0.334] overlap by 0.014 —
so what is established is that the middle cell is far above the maze, and that it is not below the lattice. The **fall** into the maze is unambiguous.

## What this means for the conditions of life in this medium

The line can now say something it could not say with two cells: **the habitat that maximises generations is not the habitat that maximises a lineage's chance of
still being there.** Depth needs percolation; persistence is destroyed by it. The intermediate regime — structure dense enough that a lineage is not one cut away
from extinction, not yet a single body that gets cut — is where a large lineage survives best (0.298 against 0.250 and 0.137), while it pays for that in
generations (1.416 against 2.213).

That is a trade-off measured in one currency the line already uses, and it is the most direct answer so far to the question of what conditions the medium must
satisfy: not "more food" and not "more structure", but a place between the two, where structures are close enough to hold a line and separate enough not to be
severed together.

## A vocabulary defect found and fixed before publication

The plan's third category was "non-monotone", and the first run printed it for the rate (9.01 — which is **between** 6.06 and 18.96). The label was measuring
distance from the chord, not monotonicity. The script now carries a **position tag** (`between` / `above` / `below` the neighbours' range), and the report's
labels are `bend` for a value between the neighbours but off the chord and `reversal` for one outside their range. The registered rule and the decision were
unchanged; only the word was wrong, and it was wrong in exactly the way X125 found three times in one night — a statistic published without its filter.

## Next hypotheses

1. **X127 — do cuts explain the maze's low persistence?** Count, per lineage, the number of times its subtree loses components in the percolating body; if the
   maze's 0.137 is cut-driven, cut counts must concentrate where the larger branch dies. This is the mechanism test for Finding 4 and needs no new worlds
   beyond dense logs already on disk.
2. **X128 — how sharp is the depth threshold?** Two more cells at `F = 0.037` and `0.038` (20 runs, ~40 min) would place the transition between 0.035 and
   0.040; the percolation point can be read from the largest-component growth already logged.
3. **The trade-off as a claim:** the sentence "the best habitat for a lineage is intermediate" now has three measured support points and one resolved
   comparison; a fourth cell at `F = 0.033` would test whether the hump's left side is real.

## Caveats

- One seed set (301–310), ten worlds per cell, one box, the 0.20 cut; the intervals are bootstrap over pairs within these ten worlds, not over worlds.
- The liveness measure is the logged component area above the 0.20 cut, not the field's own occupancy — the same proxy X110b's rule uses.
- Heredity is lowest exactly where persistence is highest (0.421 at `F = 0.035` against 0.548 and 0.480). With ten worlds this is a note, not a finding, but it
  is the first hint that the middle regime inherits less strongly, and it is worth a control in X127.
- `F = 0.035` sits at the edge of the band (§10fq), where X93's own sister contrast was the weakest of its four cells — a second reason to treat the middle
  cell's numbers as a band rather than a point.
