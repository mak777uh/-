# X145 — a lone radius-3 disc splits at 180 steps, so the mixing length is not a race the pair loses narrowly

**Plan:** [`2026-11-03-x145-lone-disc-plan.md`](2026-11-03-x145-lone-disc-plan.md) (16:10).
**Aggregate:** `l1_field_x145_lone_disc.json`. **Runs:** 5 new (one radius-3 disc, `--init-spots 3:0`) plus 10 reused
(radius 4.5 from X140, radius 6 from X138).

## The numbers

| radius | first split, median | per seed | components at end (median) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | **180 steps** | 320, 160, 150, 180, 180 | 68 |
| 4.5 | 190 steps | 280, 190, 230, 150, 150 | 68 |
| 6 | 290 steps | 210, 460, 290, 290, 290 | 56 |

The registered outcome that fires is the **third**: the disc splits late, in line with the two larger radii. A lone
radius-3 disc does not divide for about 180 steps, and it is only weakly sensitive to the radius between 3 and 4.5
(180 against 190); at radius 6 it is later (290).

## What that does to the mechanism of X142

X142 left a mechanism on the table: *a pair coalesces when the discs reach each other before either grows a
daughter*. Put against these numbers, the simple form of it is **refuted**. At gaps 1–8 the pair is one body by step
10–50 — three to four times sooner than a lone disc would divide at all — so those arms are not races lost narrowly,
they are mergers that happen long before any clock of division could matter.

What survives is a weaker and more interesting statement. At gaps 9–11 the merge itself takes 70–115 steps, and in the
seeds that end as absorption (A) or persistence (P) one or both seeded discs **do** divide — at steps 100–170, i.e.
**earlier than the 180 steps the same disc needs on its own**. So the approach to another body advances the division.
The boundary between mutual coalescence and persistence is therefore a race between the merge time and a split time
that the pair itself sets, and the split time is not a property of the disc alone.

| gap | merge time (median) | does a seeded disc divide first? | outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1–8 | 10–50 steps | never | M, both lineages end in the body |
| 9 | 70 | in 2 of 5 seeds | 3/5 M, 2/5 A |
| 10 | 80 | in 3 of 5 seeds, at 100–170 | 2/5 M, 2/5 A, 1/5 P |
| 11 | 115 | in 3 of 5 seeds, at 150–170 | 2/5 M, 3/5 P |

That is not yet a mechanism with a number — it is a mechanism with a **shape**: the merge dilates the body, and a
dilated body divides. Whether the division is set by the size of the fusion product is the obvious next question and it
can be asked on the artifacts already in git: the area of the single component at the fusion step is 99, 121, 165, 151,
267, 309 and 287 cells at gaps 1, 2, 4, 5, 8, 10 and 11 — not monotone in the outcome, so size alone is not it, but
the comparison has not been made at one-cell spacing across the boundary.

## Controls

The two reused radii reproduce their split times **seed by seed** (4.5: 280, 190, 230, 150, 150; 6: 210, 460, 290, 290,
290), every run starts as **one** component, no run ends empty, and none is structurally fragmented (the fields end at
about a quarter of the grid in sixty-odd patches, largest ≥ 50 cells, in every seed).

One defect in my own control is worth recording: the reused arms were first compared against **step numbers** while the
analyser stored **offsets from the first logged step**, which differ by exactly one, and the control fired on all ten
reused runs. The fix was to carry both — `first_split_step` (the number the reports quote) and `first_split` (the
offset) — instead of picking one convention and comparing across it.

## Caveats

- One radius is new; the two larger radii are reused from series that were not run for this question.
- "First split" is the first logged step at which a uid present at the start has a daughter; the logging interval is
  10 steps, so 150 means "in the window 141–150".
- The split times of a disc **in a pair** (100–170) are read from arms that were not designed to measure them, and the
  comparison with the lone disc (180) is across different arm types.
