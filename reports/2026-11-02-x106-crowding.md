# X106 — the crowding term is a property of places, and only in the lattice

**Plan:** [`2026-11-02-x106-crowding-plan.md`](2026-11-02-x106-crowding-plan.md) (17:11 MSK, before the run).
**Aggregate:** `morphogenesis/experiments/E09_pond/l1_field_x106_crowding.json`. X100's logs, no fields, no GPU.

## What was asked

X103d found, while looking for a confound, that a daughter born farther from the nearest **non-ancestor** peak grows larger
(`+0.358` in the lattice, ten worlds out of ten; `+0.103` in the maze). Three readings: it describes **places** (so it should predict
the mother's area too), a **constraint on growth** only, or a **constraint on persistence** (it should predict lifetime as well).

## Numbers

the 50-step logging interval.

Both controls fired: heredity on the kept pairs is 0.483 / 0.391, and the crowding coefficient reproduces X103d within 0.036 and
0.011.

Registered verdict: **mixed** — the strict "property of places" rule required the condition in **both** cells, and it holds only in
the lattice cell. The word *mixed* is doing no explanatory work here, though, because the two cells are not disagreeing about a sign:
in the lattice the effect is present for mothers and daughters alike; in the maze it is absent for both.

## Reading

**In the lattice, room is a property of the place.** The distance to the nearest non-ancestor peak predicts the *mother's* own area
`+0.397` — slightly better than it predicts her daughter's `+0.322` — so the coordinate is not about inheritance at all: it describes
where a structure sits in the packing. A structure with no neighbour within ~9 cells grows larger, whoever it is. Lifetime goes the
same way but weakly (`+0.171`, 9/10 worlds, below the 0.20 floor this plan set for a coarse, 50-step-quantised quantity), and the area
effect survives controlling for it (0.343), so the primary reading is growth in space rather than duration.

**In the maze there is no room effect at all** (0.114 for daughters, −0.038 for mothers, 0.056 for lifetime). That is what a
labyrinth should do: the width of a structure is set by the geometry of the arms, not by how far away the next peak happens to be,
because "the next peak" is usually in the neighbouring arm one wall away. The coordinate that was informative in the lattice is
uninformative where the medium itself fixes the width.

So the line now has a *medium-geometry* term with a stated domain: it exists where structures compete for open space, and it vanishes
where the space is already structured. That is a different kind of statement from everything in X100–X103 (which are about
ancestry), and it is the first one in this line that a reader could check against the morphology of the two cells by eye.

## Caveats

- The maze cell's coverage is 0.454: as everywhere in this line, its numbers describe structures that lived long enough to be logged
  (X105 is the dense-logging series that removes that condition, and it is running).
- Lifetime is quantised by the logging interval (450 and 150 steps are medians of multiples of 50); the 0.171 coefficient is reported
  with its 9/10 world agreement rather than as a point estimate.
- `d_near` for the mother is measured at her own peak step with her ancestors excluded, so the mother and daughter columns use the
  same definition at different frames — positions are physical, but a frame change is a frame change.
- The area effect is measured at the 0.20 cut, so X99's cut sensitivity applies.
