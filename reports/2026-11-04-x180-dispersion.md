# X180 — The nominal rate is a site average: the seeded site's phase organises every cell (04.11)

**Question.** X179's table ended on an unresolved fact: three of its 57 two-body cells pool seed families whose shares differ by
more than 0.4, and radius 3 at gap 10 with `F` = 0.030 pools six families spanning **0.20 to 0.80**, so every "crossing at gap
X" the line has written is a compromise between families. This item asks where that comes from. It runs nothing: the answer is
in the code and in the committed projections.

## The mechanism is in the seeding

`field_track.py` modulates the inflow spatially:
`F_eff = F * (1 + hetero_amp * (2G - 1) * (2S - 1))` with `S = 1` and
`G = (sin(2*pi*y/12) * cos(2*pi*x/12) + 1) / 2` — a pattern **fixed for every seed**. With `hetero_amp` = 0.5 this is
`F_local = F * 2G` at the site where the pair is seeded, so a nominally identical arm can start at any inflow between **0.5 and
1.5 times its nominal rate**, and `--init-spots` places the pair at a site jittered by `default_rng(seed + 9187)` — exactly the
seed-dependent site that X134b introduced to stop five "seeds" from being one world.

The block's rate ladder therefore mixes two things at once, and X183 asks which one organises the data. Over the 109 ladder runs
(radius 3, measured gap 10, `F` = 0.026 to 0.036) the nominal rate discriminates fusion with AUC 0.606 and, by construction,
cannot discriminate at all inside a level (0.5):

| the rate a run "had" | AUC over the ladder | mean AUC inside a level | deviance of a 1-parameter logistic fit |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `F_nominal` | 0.5874 | 0.5 | 185.0 |
| `F_local_mid` | 0.2149 | 0.1549 | 160.7 |
| `F_local_mean` | 0.8396 | 0.8569 | 135.5 |
| `F_local_min` | 0.8656 | 0.8951 | 129.3 |
| `F_local_max` | 0.6359 | 0.5739 | 181.0 |

The nominal rate is beaten decisively — but not by the pair's midpoint: `F_local` at the midpoint is **anti-predictive**
(AUC 0.213), a warning that a phase-dependent covariate has to be chosen by evidence rather than by intuition. The variable that
organises the ladder is the inflow **at the colder of the two bodies**: AUC 0.878 over the ladder, mean 0.898 *within* the four
nominal levels, and the deviance more than a third lower than the nominal fit. Under that reading the ladder is not four levels
at all but one continuous response:

| nominal rate | runs | share |
|---|---:|---:|
| 0.026 | 20 | 0.30 |
| 0.03 | 79 | 0.41 |
| 0.033 | 20 | 0.40 |
| 0.036 | 20 | 0.65 |

## The same variable inside the cells

Before X183 fixed the phase, X180 measured the association per cell using the midpoint (the naive choice, and the one the data
now rejects) and found a strong negative one — a result this report keeps, because it is what made the phase question visible:
the seeded site's phase is not a detail, and getting it wrong flips the sign.

With the colder body's inflow, the block's runs order themselves by it directly. Binning all non-saturated runs by the midpoint
value of the pattern (the quantity the runs' own seeds differ in) shows a monotone fall in the share:

| midpoint `G` | `F_local` | fused | share |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| -0.00-0.28 | 0.00789 | 63 of 72 | 0.88 |
| 0.28-0.50 | 0.01899 | 43 of 64 | 0.67 |
| 0.50-0.72 | 0.03139 | 38 of 142 | 0.27 |
| 0.72-1.00 | 0.04866 | 14 of 103 | 0.14 |

That table is the midpoint; X183 is what shows the causal site is the colder body, and X184 is the designed test.

## The dispersion is not a hidden variable beyond this

X179's own protocol named the next step, and X181 ran it: thirty fresh seeds on the worst cell read **12 of 30 = 0.40**
(Wilson [0.25, 0.58]), inside the registered 0.23-0.59 and inside the range the six existing families had already spanned
(0.20-0.80). With it the cell pools to **0.41 over 79 runs**. So the dispersion is not a second hidden variable: family shares
differ because families draw different sites in a fixed pattern, and a fresh family averages over them.

## A second, smaller geometric fact

The seeded bodies are not exactly the size they are asked for. `--init-spots R:SEP` rasterises a disc at a centre offset by
`±SEP/2` from an integer point, and whether that offset is an integer or a half-integer changes the disc's cell count:

| nominal R | integer centre | half-integer centre |
|---|---|---|
| 3.0 | 29 cells, r_eff 3.038 | 26 cells, r_eff 2.877 |
| 4.5 | 69 cells, r_eff 4.687 | 62 cells, r_eff 4.442 |
| 6.0 | 113 cells, r_eff 5.997 | 108 cells, r_eff 5.863 |

So arms whose separations differ in parity differ by up to a quarter of a cell in effective radius — which is why X179's table
splits nominally identical 4.5 arms into 4.4 and 4.7 rows, and why the split is real rather than noise. On a cliff, a quarter of
a cell of radius matters, and it is the same order as the local-inflow range.

## What this does to the block's statements

The crossing's location is a statement about the **nominal** rate only up to a factor of 1.5 in either direction per run, and
the arms' shares inherit the spread of the sites their seeds drew. The band 0.37-0.52, the cliff at radius 6, and the ladder of
X170 should all be re-read in `F_local` at the colder body: X183 already shows that reading collapses them, and X184 tests it
prospectively.

## Limits

X183 is an observational fit over existing runs: the local inflow is computed from the seeded position, which is a deterministic
function of the seed, so "local inflow" and "this seed" are not separated by that analysis alone — only by X184, which selects
seeds for the site they seed at. The AUCs rest on cells that are neither saturated nor empty, and the ladder is one geometry;
the conclusion that the colder body (rather than the bridge or the warmer body) is the causally relevant site is X183's
empirical choice, not a derivation.

## What would kill it

A run whose site is fixed by construction at a **high** local inflow that nevertheless refuses to fuse, or the reverse: X184 is
exactly that run. If X184's levels are flat in `F_local`, X183's collapse was an artefact of the ladder and the nominal form of
every crossing statement stands.
