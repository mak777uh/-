# X131 — which cell differences survive resampling worlds, and which belong to these ten seeds

**Plan:** [`2026-11-03-x131-worlds-plan.md`](2026-11-03-x131-worlds-plan.md) (05:59).
**Aggregate:** `l1_field_x131_worlds.json`.
**Correction to this report's own method:** see §"The test was changed, and why" below.

## What it asked

Every interval in this block was bootstrapped over **pairs** or **deaths** inside ten worlds, which answers "how precisely is this measured in these worlds", not
"would another ten worlds say the same". This computes each statistic **per world** and tests the cell differences at the world level.

## Controls

All held: each statistic's **pooled** value recomputed from the per-world numerators and denominators reproduces the published number exactly — 0.5534 / 0.4775 /
0.4837 (absorbed), 0.313 / 0.294 / 0.212 (isolated), 0.105 / 0.217 / 0.274 (vanished), 0.8724 / 0.3921 / 0.3766 (kin), 0.1839 / 0.0859 / 0.1741 (re-merge),
0.2496 / 0.2984 / 0.1372 (survival), 1.479 / 1.416 / 2.213 (depth). Every world contributes to every statistic.

## The test was changed, and why

The plan's rule was "a difference is world-level if the two **marginal** world-level intervals do not overlap". The three cells use the **same seeds** (301–310), so
the design has a paired structure the marginal overlap test throws away; the right test is the **per-seed difference**, bootstrapped over the ten seed pairs. Both
are reported and they agree wherever the marginal test finds separation; where they disagree (absorbed, and depth lattice-vs-maze) the paired test resolves what the
marginal test cannot, and the paired test is the one to quote. The registered rule was not overruled *after* seeing a favourable answer — it was replaced by a more
powerful test of the same hypothesis, and the marginal result is printed next to it.

## What survives, what does not

**World-level and quantitative.** The percolating cells destroy more (vanished +0.178 against the lattice, monotone across all three pairs); they hand their
absorptions to strangers (−0.443 kin, a step between the lattice and both percolating cells); the lattice absorbs more (−0.13 against both); and the middle cell's
low collision rate is real (lower than both neighbours, −0.068 and +0.090).

**Marginal.** The depth contrast between the lattice and the maze is +1.049 with a lower bound of 0.114 — it survives, barely, and it does **not** resolve the
middle cell from either neighbour, which is exactly what X126 said (a step at percolation, not a gradient).

**Not resolved at the world level, and this is a correction to the block's rhetoric.** The **survival contrast** between the lattice and the maze — +0.2496 against
+0.1372, the number X115, X116 and X126 all build on — is a difference of −0.077 across ten seed pairs with an interval **crossing zero**. At the pair level it is
17 σ; at the world level ten worlds cannot separate it, because the world-to-world spread (sd 0.14) is as large as the effect. The same holds for the middle cell's
rise over the lattice (+0.074, ✘). What **is** resolved in survival is the fall from the middle cell into the maze (−0.151).

So the honest form of the consequence claim is: *within a world, the larger sister's lineage survives more often, by +0.25 in the lattice and +0.14 in the maze; the
difference between those two habitats is not established with ten worlds, and only the drop from the middle cell to the maze is.* The claims in `CLAIMS.md` that
quote the cross-habitat survival comparison now carry that qualifier.

## What this changes in practice

1. **Ten worlds is enough** for the death-fate composition and for kinship, because those vary little between worlds relative to the gap.
2. **Ten worlds is not enough** for the survival and depth contrasts across habitats; those are ~2–4 SE from resolution. A power calculation on the observed spread
   (sd 0.14 for survival, 0.69–1.67 for depth) puts the requirement at **30–40 worlds** for a paired test at 80 % power. That is the concrete price of the next
   claim, and it is cheaper than a new world design.
3. **The pair-level intervals must stop being quoted as if they were habitat-level.** They are within-world precision, and the manuscript's table should carry both
   columns.

## Caveats

- Ten worlds per cell, one seed set, one box, the 0.20 cut; the paired test has 10 numerator pairs, so its bootstrap is coarse by construction.
- The paired test assumes the seeds are exchangeable across cells, which they are by protocol (same seeds, same flags) but not by construction of the world —
  inflow changes the dynamics, so "the same seed" is the same initial noise, not the same world.
- `depth` has a per-world sd of 0.69–1.67 against a contrast of 1.0–2.2, which is why its interval is the widest of the seven.

## Next hypotheses

   driven by a computed power requirement rather than by convenience.
2. **X133 — does the survival *within* a world depend on the habitat at all?** The cross-habitat claim is unresolved; the within-world claim is not. A model that
   pools the ten worlds with a world random effect would say how much of the within-world effect survives the world-level variance — cheaper than new runs and it
   uses the paired structure.
3. **X134 — the abolished-variance check.** Since the world-level sd is what blocks the claim, measure what in a world makes the survival contrast swing between
