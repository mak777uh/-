# X138 — the length is set by neither the structure nor the wavelength, but by the gap between surfaces

**Plan:** [`2026-11-03-x138-scale-plan.md`](2026-11-03-x138-scale-plan.md) (11:08).
**Aggregate:** `l1_field_x138_scale.json`. **Series:** 35 runs at `F = 0.030` with a **radius-6** seed (113 cells of area), six separations plus the single-disc control.

## The answer is a third rule, and the plan allowed for it

The plan set two hypotheses: the **pattern** sets the length (crossover stays at 16 when the structure doubles) or the **structure** sets it (crossover doubles to 32). Neither holds:

| radius | area | crossover (centre distance) | surface gap at the crossover | in wavelengths (12 cells) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | 29 cells | **16 ± 1** | 16 − 6 = **10 cells** | 0.83 |
| 6 | 113 cells | **20 ± 2** | 20 − 12 = **8 cells** | 0.67 |

Doubling the radius moved the crossover by only **4 cells**, not 16. So the centre distance is not the invariant — but neither is the structure's size. What is nearly invariant is the
**gap between the two surfaces**: 10 cells at radius 3, 8 at radius 6, i.e. **9 ± 1 cells ≈ 0.75 wavelengths**.

> **Two lineages merge when the gap between their surfaces is smaller than about 9 cells — three quarters of a pattern wavelength — whatever the size of the structures themselves.**

And the slowing is back, with the same shape as at radius 3: the median time to absorption rises 9 → 19 → 39 steps as the separation approaches the crossing at 16, 18, 20. Two
different radii produce the same two signatures (a crossover and a slowdown), which is why the gap reading is more than a curve fit: it is the same phenomenon seen twice at different
scales.

## What this changes

1. The number in the block's mechanism is no longer "16 cells" (a centre distance that depends on the seed's size) but **"a gap of about 9 cells"**, which is scale-free in the right way
   and can be compared with the pattern's own length: three quarters of a wavelength.
2. The mixing length is therefore a property of **the medium's reach**, not of the object: a bigger structure does not buy a proportionally bigger clearing.
3. It also explains why the percolating cell has no seeded mixing length at all (X135): its structures are not separate objects with surfaces to be 9 cells apart — they are one body.

## Caveats

- Two radii (3 and 6 cells), one inflow, five seeds per point; the R = 6 crossing is bracketed 18–20 with 20 a coin (2 of 5) and 24 clean (0 of 5).
- The gap is computed as `SEP − 2R`, i.e. from nominal radii; the logged areas are 29 and 113 cells, so `sqrt(area/π)` gives 3.0 and 6.0 — the nominal and effective radii agree, which is
  why the gap is quoted in cells rather than in units of the structure.
- A third radius would test the line directly: R = 4.5 should cross at about `2·4.5 + 9 = 18` cells (X140).
