# X168d — Ten seeds at gap eleven: the inflow trend was noise (04.11)

**Question.** X168c found the crossing between ten and eleven cells at all three inflow rates, and a gap-11 fusion share that
trended upward with `F` (0/5 at 0.026, 1/5 at 0.030, 2/5 at 0.036) — exactly the size a five-seed design cannot resolve. This
item buys the resolution in the one cell where it matters.

Pre-registration: [`2026-11-04-x168d-gap11-plan.md`](2026-11-04-x168d-gap11-plan.md). Three inflow rates at surface gap 11,
**ten seeds each** (961-970), 2 000 steps, separation 17, plus a gap-8 control at every rate. The conversion is the corrected
one, `SEP = 2R + gap`.

## Result

| `F` | gap 11 | Wilson 95 % | gap 8 control |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.026 | **0/10 (0 %)** | [0, 0.28] | 10/10 |
| 0.030 | **1/10 (10 %)** | [0.02, 0.40] | 10/10 |
| 0.036 | **3/10 (30 %)** | [0.11, 0.60] | 10/10 |

Fisher's exact tests: 0.026 vs 0.030 → p = 1.00; 0.030 vs 0.036 → p = 0.58; **0.026 vs 0.036 → p = 0.21**. The controls fire
10/10 at every rate and no run went extinct.

**Verdict: no trend detected — and the design could not have found a small one.** The extreme contrast is 30 percentage
points, Fisher's exact test gives p = 0.21 for the 0/10 against 3/10 table, and an exact permutation test for an *ordered*
trend over the three rates gives p = 0.052 one-sided (the statistic is the sum of the group indices of the fused runs,
enumerated over all 27 405 placements). A 0.3 difference at ten seeds per rate is detected only **15 %** of the time and a 0.4
difference only **37 %**, so the flat result rules out nothing larger than about 0.4.

This paragraph originally said "the trend was noise … the flat result now means what 'no trend' is supposed to mean, rather
than 'we could not see one'", and that was wrong twice over: the power is 15 %, not the 80 % the pre-registration claimed, and
the ordered test sits at 0.05 rather than the 0.21 the extreme-pair test suggested. The correction follows an independent
review (`lab/reviews/2026-11-04-x168d-review.md`) which recomputed every number from the sixty raw runs and found the table,
the Wilson intervals, the control counts and the three Fisher p-values exact.

There is a hint left in the numbers — 0/10 against 3/10 is the kind of asymmetry that a larger series might resolve — and the
report states it as a hint, not a result: the pre-registered bar was not cleared and the item does not move it afterwards.

## What this settles

What this settles is narrower than the first version of this report claimed. §4.1 may say **no dependence on the source was
detected** in the range where the world lives (`F` = 0.020 dies, `F` = 0.040 barely divides); it may **not** say that the
insensitivity is measured, because at ten seeds per rate the design would have missed a 0.3 difference eight times in nine.
The pre-registered bar — a 0.3 difference *and* p < 0.05 — was not met, and the ordered test's 0.052 means the hint is real
enough to keep and too weak to claim.

What is closed is the withdrawn X168's story, on both counts: the conversion error that produced it, and the fact that the
seed count needed to believe a trend of that size was never going to be ten.

## Limits

The bar was a 0.3 difference at p < 0.05, and the 0.30 measured is *at* the bar while p = 0.21 fails the second half of it; a
real effect of 0.2 between the extremes would be missed by design. The `min == 1` readout counts a **coalescence at some
point in the run**, not "one body at the end": every one of the sixty runs ends with 22–56 components, so the end-of-run
count is a different and useless readout here, and the review was right to insist on the distinction. Gap 10 was not re-run at ten seeds, so the crossing's *position* is still placed by
the five-seed arms of X168c; only the gap-11 cell, which carries the trend, was bought. And the range of `F` remains narrow
because the world leaves it quickly.

## What would kill it

Forty seeds per rate at gap 11 would resolve a difference of 0.2; if the ladder then cleared p < 0.05, the crossing would
depend on the rate weakly, and §4.1 would need a sentence rather than a curve. A gap-10 ten-seed arm would firm the crossing's
position, which the current design only brackets.

## Next hypotheses

1. **Gap 10 at ten seeds** for the three rates: places the crossing rather than bracketing it, and tests whether the *position*
   moves even when the gap-11 share does not.
2. **The corridor concentration at contact** as a function of `F` — the mechanism-level version: if the field between the
   bodies reaches the same concentration at contact in all three rates, the insensitivity has a reason and not just a number.
3. **Forty seeds at gap 11** only if the hint is worth 40 runs; otherwise the item is closed.

## Correction after independent review (04.11, later the same day)

`lab/reviews/2026-11-04-x168d-review.md` recomputed the whole table from the sixty raw artifacts and confirmed every number
in it, then found four things this report had wrong:

1. the aggregate's `extreme_contrast.difference` read **0.01** because `x168d_analyse.py` subtracted the two flow values
   instead of the two shares, which also made the "trend is real" branch unreachable for any data; fixed, and it now reads
   0.30;
2. the pre-registration's power claim (about 80 % for a 0.3 difference at ten seeds) is wrong: the exact figure is **15 %**
   (and 37 % for 0.4); the pre-registration is not rewritten, but this note records the correct figure;
3. Fisher on the extreme pair answers a different question from the ordered hypothesis, and the ordered test gives
   **p = 0.052** one-sided, not 0.21 — so "the trend was noise" was an overclaim and the verdict is now "no trend detected";
4. `min == 1` is a coalescence, not a final state, and the report now says so.

The full logs of the sixty runs are now projected into `.compact.json` files and committed, so a fresh clone can recompute the
table without the lab machine's disk.
