# X191 — the bridge as a dose–response in the local inflow (04.11)

**Question.** X190 established the bridge mechanism over five distinct sites per level at two levels (radius 6 gap 9: −0.169, exact
Mann-Whitney p = 0.016) and showed that seeds are clones, so the budget for the next question goes on levels rather than on copies.
This item sweeps **five levels** of `F_local` with five phase-stratified sites each, one seed per site: is the bridge a dose–response
in the inflow, or a two-population difference that happens to look like one? Preregistration
`reports/2026-11-04-x191-site-sweep-plan.md`, including its section 8 amendment.

**Result: the trend survives, the link to the outcome does not reach its registered bars.**

Registered criteria, one at a time:
1. **monotone in the level means with at most one inversion and Spearman ≤ −0.5 — PASSES at the margin**: the means are [0.36295, 0.27178, 0.16727, 0.29468, 0.0318]
   with 1 inversion, and the Spearman correlation over the 30 sites is **-0.509**.
2. **top level's fusion share ≥ 0.6 — FAILS**: the shares are [1.0, 0.4, 0.0, 0.6, 0.2] and the top level reads **0.2**.
3. **site-level correlation between the bridge ratio and the outcome with |ρ| ≥ 0.5 — FAILS narrowly**: **0.441**, the right sign
   (a thicker bridge goes with more coalescence) but below the bar.

So the bridge *is* weaker where the site is stronger along the band, but this design cannot turn that into a statement about the
outcome: with five sites per level and within-level spreads of [0.14276, 0.21425, 0.19282, 0.12796, 0.022], single steps are unresolved, which is what the plan's sensitivity
arithmetic predicted before the run (the level spacing is about one standard error of a level mean).

**The first run of this item is kept in the aggregate as a phase artefact, because the flaw is instructive.** Its picker required
distinct jitters but not distinct phases, and at `F_local` = 0.030 all five of its sites turned out to be **one phase**: the identical
ratio 0.39916 five times (sd 0.0000), and its level means rose above the trend there. X190's clone finding applied one level deeper —
three *seeds* per site were one world, five *sites* per level were one phase — so what the readout depends on is the **phase**, the
position modulo the pattern's 12-cell period, and a sample has to count phases. The corrected picker requires five distinct phases per
level; every level now reports its distinct phases and distinct ratios in the table above.

**What this does and does not say.** It does not replace X190: the two-population comparison at the largest size stands on its own
registered statistic. It does say that the mechanism is a **graded** effect along the inflow band rather than an artefact of the two
levels chosen, with the grading at the edge of what five sites can resolve. And it says that the outcome side of the mechanism needs
either more sites per level or a level design with a narrower band, not another two-level comparison.

**Next hypotheses.** (1) The same sweep with **fifteen phases per level**, which the round's arithmetic allows (the phase classes are
finite and enumerable) — that is the design that can put the outcome correlation past 0.5 if the effect is real. (2) Read the
*fusion share* per phase class rather than per site, since the share is the outcome and the phase is the variable: a logistic fit over
phase classes with the bridge ratio as the regressor. (3) The clone and phase lessons apply to the whole line: X187's radius-6 Fisher
p of 0.39, X183's 139-run AUC and X189's site caveat should all be re-expressed with the distinct-phase count of their pools.
