# X155 — the heredity coefficient is the fidelity of the copying rule, and the daughter's deviation is the mutation's own

**Plan:** [`2026-11-03-x155-heredity-plan.md`](2026-11-03-x155-heredity-plan.md) (registered 23:45, before any analysis and before the
ablation arm). **Aggregate:** `l1_field_x155_heredity.json`. **Runs:** three (anchor seed 301, two mutation-off arms), 90-101 s each,
plus the CPU analysis of 20 existing dense worlds. **Cost:** 0.11 GPU-hour.

## What was asked

The line has said since 01.11 that heredity in the field is *assigned, not emerged*, and the support for that sentence was a
correlation between division rate and the stored `inheritability_r` (Spearman +0.79). That is an argument about a correlation, not a
measurement. X155 asked the three questions the numbers can answer: can the parent-daughter pairs be reconstructed from the logs at
all, what is the heredity of the trait at the horizon, and does the measured heredity survive the controls that would make it a
property of the medium rather than of the assignment.

## The pairs reconstruct, and the reconstruction is validated against the artifact

The stored `selection.inheritability_r` is computed at the exact split step, and that moment is not logged. With
`--split-new-uid` the parent's uid *ends* at a split, so parent and daughter are never present at the same logged step: the
reconstruction reads the parent at its last logged step before the daughter appears and the daughter at her first, a displacement of
at most one logging quantum (10 steps). The control that decides whether this is a measurement or an artefact is the comparison with
the artifact's own number, and it passes to the fourth decimal:

| cell | worlds | pairs reconstructed | per-world `r` | stored `r` | difference |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `F` = 0.030 | 10 | 2 164 of 2 211 | **0.9092** | 0.9092 | **−0.00001** |
| `F` = 0.040 | 10 | 1 779 of 1 951 | **0.8967** | 0.8989 | **−0.0022** |

A pooled `r` over all pairs of a cell would read 0.938 and 0.929, about 0.03 above the stored value; that is between-world variance
entering a coefficient the artifact defines within a world, and it is why the control compares means over worlds and not a pooled
number. Both are in the aggregate (`r`, `r_per_world_mean`).

## The slope is one, and what is not explained by the parent is exactly the mutation

With the pairs validated, the quantity can be read. The registered estimators are the slope of the daughter on the parent with a
cluster bootstrap over parent uids, a shuffled-parent null within each world, and the same slope partialled on the parent's number
of daughters:

| readout | `F` = 0.030 | `F` = 0.040 |
|---|---|---|
| slope, 95 % CI | **0.9837** [0.9679, 1.0002] | **0.9931** [0.9756, 1.0103] |
| slope partial given the parent's daughter count | **0.9837** | **0.9932** |
| shuffled-parent null: mean, one-sided p | 0.1895, **0.001** | 0.1490, **0.001** |
| residual sd of daughter minus parent | **0.1487** | **0.1495** |
| the mutation rule's own sigma | 0.15 | 0.15 |

Three things follow, and they are the result.

1. **The slope is one within the interval.** A daughter's `s` is its parent's `s`, and the interval's upper end touches 1.00 in the
   lattice and passes it in the maze — so there is no attenuation to explain and no hidden noise floor.
2. **Division rate is not the mechanism.** Partialling on the parent's number of daughters changes the slope by less than 1e-4. The
   stored coefficient correlates with division rate (Spearman +0.79) because both are functions of the same copying rule, not because
   the rate does the transmitting.
3. **The deviation is the mutation, to one per cent.** The residual sd is 0.1487 and 0.1495 against the rule's `sigma = 0.15`. In two
   worlds of two different habitats, the only thing a daughter does not get from its parent is precisely the number the mutation
   parameter names — nothing else in the medium contributes.

## The hereditary share of variance is a property of the world, not of the trait

The plan's second readout is `H² = 1 − SS_within / SS_total` for `s` at the horizon, with components grouped by lineage root
(union-find over `uid_pairs`). It is small in the lattice and large in the maze, and the dispersion is the point:

| cell | mean `H²` between lineages | median | range over worlds | lineages at the horizon |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `F` = 0.030 | 0.082 | 0.019 | 0.00-0.30 | 1-10 |
| `F` = 0.040 | 0.416 | 0.36 | 0.00-0.93 | 1-5 (9 worlds; one has 2 components) |

So the broad-sense heredity of the field is **a statement about how many lineages a habitat leaves standing**, not a property of the
trait: in a lattice world with ten lineages the between-lineage share is 0.30, in a world with two it is 0.00-0.02; and the maze at its
horizon holds 2-11 bodies in total, so a single root can carry 0.93 of the variance. Reading a `H²` here as a heritability of a trait
would be a category error, and that is the most useful thing the number says.

## The ablation: without the rule's noise there is no trait at all

The mutation-off arm (two runs, seeds 301 and 302) is the direct test of provenance. The anchor that licenses it reproduces
`x108_f030_s301.json` **key-by-key** (`uid_pairs`, `size_pairs`, `uid_outcomes`, and every logged step's `components` and `s_mean`),
so the mutation flag is the only difference in the arm.

With `--mutation 0.0` the trait is constant: `s = 1.0` in every component at every step in both worlds, `s_sd_between_last = 0.0`,
and **100 %** of reconstructed pairs are exactly equal. `r` and the slope are therefore undefined — variance is zero — which is the
degenerate form of perfect transmission. This is stronger than a coefficient near one: the field's trait has no variance at all
without the copying rule's noise, so there is nothing for the medium to transmit independently.

## Verdict

**HEREDITY IS THE RULE.** Every criterion in the registered table is met: the validity control passes to 1e-5, both slopes exclude
zero and touch one, the shuffled-parent null is rejected at p = 0.001 in both cells, the partial slope is unchanged, the residual
matches `sigma` to 1 %, and the ablation removes the trait's variance entirely.

What the line may now say: the field's heredity coefficient is the **fidelity of the assignment in `field_track.py`**, measured, with
its error fully accounted for by the mutation parameter; the broad-sense heredity at the horizon is a function of the habitat's
lineage count; and no quantity of the medium contributes a transmissible component. The sentence in §4k can be promoted from an
assertion to a measurement with its error budget.

What cannot be said: this is not a test of whether *another* medium could have genuine heredity — that is a question about a
different model, and it is now explicitly a negative for the Gray-Scott field as coded, not for reaction-diffusion systems.

## Controls, and what was fixed after registration

1. **Anchor** — bit-identical to `x108_f030_s301.json`. Held.
2. **Validity control** — per-world means reproduce the stored coefficient to 1e-5 / 0.0022, against a registered 0.02. Held.
3. **Null** — shuffled parents give 0.19 and 0.15 against observed 0.98, p = 0.001 (the smallest value 1 000 permutations can give).
4. **Partial** — the daughter count does not move the slope. Held.
5. **Ablation** — degenerate as described above. Recorded in the plan's amendment, **after** the data, because the plan did not
   foresee that `--mutation 0` would make the coefficient undefined rather than equal to one. The amendment states the fallback
   criterion (identical pairs and zero variance) and that it was chosen post hoc; nothing else in the decision rule changed.

Caveats: the pairs are displaced by up to one logging quantum; the union-find has no notion of coalescence, so a merged body is a new
root (this makes `H²` an upper bound on the between-lineage share); two mutation-off worlds are few, though the result is degenerate in
both; and the `s` values of the horizon are read from the compact logs' components, which the compact projection keeps.

## Next hypotheses

1. **A trait that is not the copying rule's own variable.** The block's negative is about `s`, which the rule writes. A trait that is
   *measured* from the field (an excursion width, the local wavelength) and inherited through the pattern rather than through a column
   would test heredity without the assignment being the mechanism; that is the only route by which this line could produce positive
   heredity, and it needs a new observable, not a new run.
2. **The lineage count as the hidden variable of `H²`.** The maze's 0.42 and the lattice's 0.08 differ by lineage count (1-4 against
   2-6). Predicting `H²` from that count alone, on the five existing cells, would turn the category error into a calibration.
3. **X152 / X149** remain the open questions of the coalescence block and are unaffected by this result.
