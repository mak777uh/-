# X147 — the boundary moves inward with the radius, while the time stays the gap's

**Plan:** [`2026-11-03-x147-radius45-plan.md`](2026-11-03-x147-radius45-plan.md) (17:55).
**Aggregate:** `l1_field_x147_radius45.json`. **Runs:** 5 new (radius 4.5, surface gap 8, separation 17) plus 20 reused
from X140 and 15 radius-3 comparison arms.

## The two radii side by side

| radius | gap 7 | gap 8 | gap 9 | gap 10 | gap 11 | gap 13 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **4.5** | M 5/5, t 39 | **M 3/5, A 2/5, t 49** | M 2/5, A 2/5, P 1/5, t 89 | – | P 5/5 | P 5/5 |
| **3** | M 5/5, t 39 | M 5/5, t 49 | M 3/5, A 2/5, t 69 | M 2/5, A 2/5, P 1/5 | M 2/5, P 3/5 | – |

The registered outcome that fires is the **third**: at radius 4.5 a surface gap of 8 cells is M 3/5, not 5/5, so the
bigger discs begin to fail **one cell earlier in gap** than the radius-3 discs do. The boundary is therefore **not** a
pure function of the surface gap: it belongs to the pair, with the radius shifting it by about a cell.

Two things hold, though, and they are the interesting part:

1. **The time is untouched.** At gap 7 both radii dissolve at **39** logged steps and at gap 8 both at **49** — the
   medians are identical to the step, on four arms and ten seeds per radius. So the radius changes *whether* a division
   can intervene, not *when* the two bodies meet. That is the sharpest form of the X142 timing law: it survives a
   change of the structure's size by 2.2× in area even at the cell where the outcome starts to differ.
2. **The far side is qualitatively the same.** Radius 4.5 goes M (gap 7) → mixed (9) → P (11, 13), exactly the order
   radius 3 follows, with persistence complete by gap 11 on both.

At gap 9 the medians differ (69 against 89), but these are medians over the seeds that coalesced — three at radius 3 and
two at radius 4.5 — so the comparison there is not like for like and is not used for anything.

## What this changes

The gap rule of §10hd was "the crossing is at `2R + 9`", and X142 strengthened it to "the whole coalescence-time curve
depends on the gap alone". X147 puts the two halves together and finds they are **not the same law**:

- the **time** to mutual coalescence is a function of the surface gap, at both radii, to the logging quantum;
- the **boundary** where coalescence stops being universal is a property of the pair, at gap 8 for radius 3 and gap 7
  for radius 4.5 — one cell inside, in the direction the bigger structure would need, as if the larger body reaches the
  dividing state a cell sooner.

The mechanism X145 and X148 left open (what makes a seed divide) now has a second handle: whatever it is, it is **more
likely with a larger radius at the same timing**, which neither "the clock" nor "the size of the fusion product"
predicts on its own.

## Caveats

- One cell of gap and two seeds of outcome separate the two boundaries: radius 4.5 gap 8 is M 3/5 against M 5/5 at
  radius 3. That is a real difference in the registered sense (>3/5 rule) but a small one, and it is not resolved
  further by this sweep.
- Radius 4.5 has no arm at gap 10, and radius 3 has none at gap 13; the far sides are not matched arm for arm.
- The times are quantised at 10 steps, and medians at gap 9 are over unequal numbers of coalescing seeds.
