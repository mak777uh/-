# X134 — the mixing length: two seeded lineages, and a threshold at one pattern wavelength

**Plan:** [`2026-11-03-x134-mixing-plan.md`](2026-11-03-x134-mixing-plan.md) (07:20, amended twice before reading the sweep).
**Aggregate:** `l1_field_x134_mixing.json`. **Series:** 35 runs (six separations plus the degenerate arm, five seeds each), `F = 0.030`, anchor exact.

## What it asked

Every result in the identity block is observational: the separation between lineages and the kinship of a neighbour are whatever the world happens to produce. This is
the first **intervention**: a new additive initial condition places **two discs and nothing else** — radius 3 cells, 29 cells of area each, the lattice's median dead
area — at a chosen separation, and the question is the distance at which the habitat stops keeping two lineages apart.

## Controls

The anchor is exact with the flag absent (`uid_pairs` identical against `x108_f030_s301.json`; the only declared difference is the inert `init_spots` parameter key),
the smoke run at `SEP = 12` starts as exactly two components at `cx = 42.0` and `54.0`, every run is alive under the structural rule, and the sweep spans both
outcomes (5/5 in one direction, 0/5 in the other).

## The sweep

|---|---|---|---|
| 8 | **5 / 5** | 0 / 5 | **0** |

**The mixing length is between 12 and 18 cells, and the transition is complete: 10 of 10 seeds merge at 8 and 12 cells, 20 of 20 keep both lineages at 18, 24, 30 and
36.** No seed sits on the other side of its group, so the threshold is not a noisy crossing but a wall between two adjacent separations.

The number is not arbitrary: the pattern wavelength in this habitat is **12 cells** (`q' = 8`, X84), so two lineages merge when they are within one wavelength and
survive as two when they are further apart than one. At 12 cells the merge takes a median of 19 steps — one third of a generation in the lattice — and at 8 cells it
happens at the first logged step.

The degenerate arm is worth keeping in the record for what it shows: at `SEP = 6` the two discs **touch** (`SEP = 2R`), the world starts as a single blob, and
genealogically both lineages survive 5/5 — because the tracker names one component and later splits create fresh uids. It is not a two-lineage experiment, which is
why it is excluded from the threshold, and it is also a warning: a component count would have read this arm as "apart", the exact opposite of the truth.

## What this settles, and what it does not

**Settled:** distance is a **lever**, not a correlate. The block's reading — the lattice keeps lineages apart, percolation mixes them — now has an interventional
basis: place two lineages a wavelength apart and the habitat keeps them separate for 6 000 steps in every seed; place them closer and one takes the other.

**Also settled, and it sharpens the kinship story:** the two seeded discs are **not kin** — different uids, no common ancestor. So what this experiment measures is
the **stranger** case: two unrelated lineages merge if they are within a wavelength. In the lattice a structure's only neighbour is its sibling (X129), which is why
its absorptions are family events; the percolating body puts strangers within a wavelength and they merge, which is why its absorptions are not.

**Not settled:** the mixing length at `F = 0.040`. The maze's wavelength is long (q' = 7, and the body is percolating), so its mixing length is plausibly larger, and
that would be the natural next sweep. One radius (3 cells = one spot), one inflow, five seeds per point, and a sweep spacing of 6 cells — the threshold is bracketed to
that spacing, not to a cell.

## Amendment 3 (08:25, after the sweep, before the report's numbers)

The first analysis of this sweep used the plan's original readout — the **component count** — and it was wrong in a way worth recording: after the two discs coalesce
the same run fragments into **60–73 components by step 6 000**, so "one component" cannot mean "two lineages became one". The readout was replaced by the
genealogical one (does one seeded subtree disappear into the other), which is X128b's absorption rule applied to a seeded pair, and the `SEP = 6` arm was excluded for
touching discs. Both changes were written into the plan before the sweep was read as a result, and both were forced by facts visible in the first runs rather than by
their outcome.

## Next hypotheses

1. **X135 — the mixing length in the maze.** The same sweep at `F = 0.040`, where the wavelength is longer: if the threshold moves with the wavelength, mixing is a
   property of the pattern scale and the habitat's "segregation" is a length, not a regime.
2. **X136 — does the mixing length move with the inflow continuously?** Three inflows, threshold bracketed at 2-cell spacing: a length that interpolates would put
   the whole block's habitat contrast on one axis with one unit.
3. **X137 — kin versus strangers at the same distance.** Seed two discs *and* label one as a daughter of the other's ancestor (a controlled genealogy), then repeat the
   sweep: if the threshold is the same for kin, then kinship matters only through the distance it imposes — which would simplify the block's mechanism to a single
   variable.

## Caveats

- One inflow (`F = 0.030`), one radius (3 cells), five seeds per point, sweep spacing 6 cells; the threshold is bracketed between 12 and 18, not resolved to a cell.
- The absorption readout uses the seed uids 1 and 2; a run in which the tracker named the two discs differently would be mis-read. The smoke run and the first-step
  counts confirm the naming, and the `SEP = 6` arm shows what the failure looks like.
- Both lineages' subtrees are small (the disc is 29 cells), so this is a test of two *structures* merging, not of two large lineages competing.

## Correction (09:40, by X135's control and X134b)

Two errors in this report, both found by the single-disc control of X135:

1. **The five seeds were one world measured five times.** The `--init-spots` branch as first written placed the discs at fixed centres and discarded the seed-dependent
   mask, so every "seed" at a given separation produced the same merge step and the same subtree sizes (verified per seed in `l1_field_x134_mixing.json`). The
   threshold below stands — it is a deterministic fact about a forced world — but "10 of 10 seeds" was one world counted ten times, and the replication language is
   withdrawn.
2. **The bracket was coarse and its edge was not measured.** The sweep started at 12 cells, which is the wavelength itself; with the placement freed (X134b) the wall
   sits between **15 and 18** cells, and at 12 cells the merge takes **20 steps in all five placements** — deterministic inside the wavelength, placement-dependent at
   its edge.

So the corrected sentence is: **the mixing length is between 15 and 18 cells, slightly above the 12-cell wavelength, and it is replicated across placements** (X134b).
The numbers in the table above are unchanged; their labels are corrected here, and the aggregate `l1_field_x134b_mixing.json` carries the replicated version.
