# X107 — heredity survives the shared-room test

**Plan:** [`2026-11-02-x107-shared-room-plan.md`](2026-11-02-x107-shared-room-plan.md), registered before the run.
**Aggregate:** `morphogenesis/experiments/E09_pond/l1_field_x107_shared_room.json`. X100's logs, no GPU.

## Why this mattered

X106 found that the distance to the nearest non-ancestor peak predicts the **mother's** own area (+0.397 in the lattice) slightly
better than her daughter's (+0.322). Since daughters are born a median of 4.9 cells from their mothers, mother and daughter usually
sit in the same room — so part of the line's central number (parent–daughter `ρ = 0.531`) could have been a shared environment
wearing the name of inheritance. This is the one internal check that could have reduced every heredity number in the line.

## Numbers

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.030 | 2 004 / 2 165 | 0.926 | 10.15 | **+0.389** | 0.519 | **0.455** | 0.425 | **0.064** |
| 0.040 | 1 089 / 1 761 | 0.618 | 13.61 | −0.023 | 0.395 | 0.395 | 0.392 | **0.000** |

Both controls fired: heredity on the kept pairs (0.519 / 0.395) and the room coefficient reproduces X106 (0.389 against 0.397, −0.023
against −0.038).

Registered verdict: **heredity is not a shared-room effect** — in both cells the coefficient falls by 0.064 and 0.000, inside the
declared resolution of 0.10.

## Reading

The room coordinate is real: it correlates with the mother's area at 0.389, so the shared variance exists and was there to be
removed. Removing it costs the heredity coefficient **0.064**, about 12 % of its value; removing both the mother's and the daughter's
room costs 0.094 (0.519 → 0.425). The maze cell loses nothing at all, consistent with X106's finding that there is no room effect
there to remove.

So the picture from X100–X107 is coherent rather than circular: the medium passes an excursion width from mother to daughter
(0.52 here, 0.38 in the maze), and independently a structure's size depends on how much open space surrounds it — but the second does
not generate the first. A daughter that inherits a wide excursion remains wide even in a room like her mother's.

Two honest qualifications:

- 12 % is not zero. The claim is "not a shared-room effect **at the declared resolution**", i.e. the room cannot account for the
  heredity, not that it accounts for nothing. If the line ever needs the coefficient to three decimals, this is the correction to
  apply first: 0.455 / 0.395 are the room-adjusted values.
- The maze cell's coverage is 0.618 and its room coordinate does nothing; the maze statement is therefore "no room effect", not
  "room tested and excluded".

## Caveats

- One box, one grid, one `k`, one excursion cut (0.20).
- The room coordinate is the nearest non-ancestor peak at the structure's own peak step; in crowded frames a peak's nearest
  neighbour is often a daughter or a sibling, which the ancestor ban does not remove. That makes the coordinate slightly
  "relatedness-blind" — it is a geometric neighbour, not a genealogical stranger.
- Controlling for both rooms by summing them is a one-dimensional summary of two coordinates; a cleaner version would use both
  separately, which the data support (partialling twice in sequence) and which would not change the conclusion by more than a few
  hundredths.

## What it changes downstream

- The heredity numbers in `CLAIMS.md` can now be quoted with "room-adjusted: 0.455 / 0.395" beside them, and the answer to the
  obvious referee question ("isn't this just the daughters staying near their mothers?") is a number: 0.064.
- X105 (running) will retest this at dense logging, where the maze cell stops being a long-lived sample.
