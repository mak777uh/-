# X74 — does the half-life of the size memory depend on the inflow?

**Question.** X73 measured the decay of the size memory at one point of the plane (`F = 0.035`, `k = 0.06`): ancestor–descendant
area correlation 0.475 → 0.204 → 0.072 over three divisions, i.e. a factor of about 0.4 per division and a half-life of 0.7–0.8
divisions. If that decay is the field's **restoring force** — each structure pulled toward the medium's preferred scale at every
division — then changing the inflow, which changes the preferred scale (X71: 12.0 cells at `F ≤ 0.030`, 8.7 cells at
`F ≥ 0.040`), should change the decay rate. If the decay is instead a property of the division event itself (a constant
fraction of the parent's deviation lost per split), the rate will be the same at every inflow.

Both outcomes are informative: a constant rate makes the memory a property of the *event*, a rate that varies with `F` makes it
a property of the *medium*.

## 1. Manipulation is connected

The manipulation is the inflow, and it is known to move the pattern: X71 measured peak positions 8 → 11 bins across the sweep,
and X73 measured a real decay at 0.035. Connectedness is that the two new cells reproduce the genealogy at all — they must yield
division pairs (see §3) and their lag-1 correlation must be in the same range as 0.035 (0.3–0.6). If a cell yields no pairs, the
cell is void, not a zero.

## 2. Positive control fires

Two numbers, before any comparison of rates.

1. **Lag-1 in every cell ≥ 0.30.** A cell whose lag-1 is below that has no memory to decay and cannot enter the comparison.
2. **The cross-lineage shuffle null stays below 0.10** at every lag in every cell — the same control X73 used, recomputed here
   rather than borrowed.

## 3. Readout exists and varies

Same readout as X73: Spearman(ancestor peak area, descendant peak area) at lags 1, 2, 3, per cell. Two new cells are run at
`F = 0.030` and `F = 0.040` with **`--sample-every 50`** — dense logging is required, because X71's artifacts have **zero**
division pairs at 1000-step logging (the parent is gone between snapshots), and that is what makes this a new series rather than
a re-analysis.

The decay rate `λ` is fitted as the geometric mean of `ρ₂/ρ₁` and `ρ₃/ρ₂`; the half-life is `ln 0.5 / ln λ`.

## 4. Sensitivity arithmetic

Five worlds per cell, 6000 steps, 50-step logging, no field snapshots (the readout needs only `uid_pairs` and peak areas) —
roughly 60 s per run, so ten runs ≈ 10 minutes. X73's lag counts at 0.035 were 2 140 / 1 968 / 1 776 pairs; the new cells should
give the same order, so `λ` is estimable with a Fisher band of about ±0.05 on each `ρ` and about ±0.1 on `λ`. A difference in
half-life smaller than a factor of 1.3 is therefore not detectable, and that is stated in advance.

## 5. Refusal rule

**Void** for a cell if it has fewer than 200 lag-2 pairs, or if its lag-1 is below 0.30, or if its shuffle p95 exceeds 0.10.

The series **supports "the rate is a property of the medium"** if the half-lives of the three cells differ by a factor **≥ 1.3**
in a consistent direction across the three pairwise comparisons (i.e. the ordering of `λ` is the same in all five worlds' own
estimates, checked per world rather than on the pooled fit). It **supports "the rate is a property of the event"** if all three
`λ` lie within **±0.10** of each other. Anything else is **inconclusive**.
