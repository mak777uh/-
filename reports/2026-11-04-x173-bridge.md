# X173 — Not decidable: the world's own proliferation closes the window (04.11)

**Question.** X171 made the corridor at contact a size-independent criterion. A criterion is only useful if it *predicts*: if
two bodies merge when the bridge reaches a fraction of the body peak, then the maximum the bridge ever reaches should separate
the pairs that merge from the pairs that do not — including the ones that never merge, where that maximum is a value they
never reached.

Pre-registration: [`2026-11-04-x173-bridge-plan.md`](2026-11-04-x173-bridge-plan.md). Three arms at `F` = 0.030, radius 3,
surface gaps 8 (fuses 10/10), 10 (about half) and 11 (about none), ten seeds each (1011-1020), **3 000 steps** so the gap-11 arm
would have time to build a bridge it had to cross. The pre-registration named its own chief risk in §6: that the non-fusing
arm's window might close before its bridge finished building, in which case its maximum would be censored rather than negative.

## Result: the risk fired, and it is worse than censoring

| gap | fused | bridge maximum, median | range |
|---|---|---|---|
| 8 | 10/10 | 0.44 | 0.37-0.50 |
| 10 | 2/10 | 0.43 | 0.31-0.53 |
| 11 | 1/10 | 0.35 | 0.23-0.48 |

The best single threshold (0.43) **misclassifies 7 of 30 runs**, so the pre-registered rule gives its negative branch: the
bridge's maximum does not by itself decide which pairs merge. But that branch is not the finding, because the readout it
rests on does not measure the seeded pair for long enough to be about them:

- the world's bodies **proliferate**. In the gap-11 arm the seeded pair can be identified by its uids (they are 1 and 2) for
  **13 to 18 of the 301 sampled steps**, i.e. to step 120-170 of 3 000; after that the pair's identity is gone from the log —
  not merged, just buried in a crowd of new bodies;
- reading the corridor between "the two largest components" instead is not a substitute: after step ~150 those are arbitrary
  members of the crowd, and a corridor measured between two arbitrary bodies is not a bridge between the pair;
- so for the non-fusing arm, the maximum the bridge reaches is a **lower bound observed over the first fifteenth of the run**,
  not the run's outcome. The two arms' maxima are therefore not comparable, and the misclassification count measures the
  proxy, not the physics.

The honest verdict is **not decidable with this readout**, and the reason is a property of the world rather than a bug: bodies
bud from the field continuously, so the window in which a *non-fusing* pair can be followed is about a hundred and fifty steps —
less than the time the bridge needs at a non-crossing gap.

## What the item does establish

One thing survives, and it is consistent with X171 rather than with the predictor claim. Among the runs **that did fuse**, the
bridge maximum sits at 0.44 (gap 8) and 0.43 (gap 10) — the same 0.47-ish fraction X171 measured at contact from a different
seeds set and a different definition (contact-time corridor, not a maximum). That repetition across definitions is worth
having. It does not rescue the prediction test.

Two process findings, recorded because they cost the item its result:

1. `components_log` entries carry `uid`, `s`, `area`, `cx`, `cy`. The first version of the analysis sorted components by a
   `cells` field that does not exist, so "the two largest" silently degenerated to an arbitrary pair; that was caught and fixed
   before any number was reported, and the fix is in `scripts/x173_bridge_analyse.py`.
2. Identity by uid is available only while the seeded bodies stay distinct — which in this world is about 150 steps. Any future
   design that needs to follow a specific pair must either work inside that window or change the world.

## Limits

The gap-10 arm fused 2/10 here against 7/15 at the same rate and gap in X170 (seeds 986-1005 versus 971-985 here). The two
intervals overlap ([0.06, 0.51] and [0.25, 0.70]) but the drop is large enough to note: the share at gap 10 is less stable
across seeds than the ladder in X170 suggested, which is a second reason to prefer the corridor over the share as the ladder's
readout.

## What would kill it

Nothing about this item can be killed by more seeds — it is a censoring limit. A design that measures the same prediction must
first obtain a world where a pair can be followed for a few hundred steps without a crowd: either a much larger grid (so the
pair is a smaller fraction of the world), or a lower inflow (fewer new bodies), or an explicit marker carried through
divisions.

## Next hypotheses

1. **Follow the pair with a marker, not with a uid.** The trait `s` is inherited and logged per component, so a run with a
   distinctive `s` at seeding (and mutation left on) could track descent statistically even after division — the same trick the
   heredity line used for parent-daughter pairs.
2. **A bigger grid, same pair.** At 192 × 192 the crowd takes four times longer to reach the pair, which may be enough to
   watch a gap-11 bridge through to the end of its build-up.
3. **The prediction at contact time instead of over time.** For every run the corridor is measured *at* the moment of contact, and
   for the non-fusing runs at the moment their identity is lost; if the two distributions separate at that single common step,
   the criterion predicts without needing the build-up. This is a re-analysis of the dumps already on disk and answers the same
   question with the confound folded in.
