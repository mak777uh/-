# X173 — The bridge's build-up predicts who fuses (pre-registration, 04.11)

**Status:** pre-registered, not yet run.

## 1. Manipulation is connected

X171 settled the first half of the criterion: at a fixed rate the field between two bodies at the moment they become one is the
same fraction of the body peak at every size (0.48, 0.49, 0.47 at radii 3, 4.5 and 6 at `F` = 0.030). X170 had already shown that
fraction moves with the source. What neither measured is the thing the criterion is *for*: if a merge happens when the bridge
reaches a threshold, then **the maximum the bridge ever reaches should separate the runs that fuse from the runs that do not**,
and that maximum is measurable for both — on the non-fusing side it is simply a value the bridge never got to.

This item measures the corridor **as a function of step** in three arms at `F` = 0.030, radius 3: surface gap 8 (fuses 5/5),
gap 10 (fuses about half) and gap 11 (fuses about none), ten seeds each, field dumped every ten steps.

## 2. Positive control fires

The control fires if the gap-8 arm fuses 10/10 and the gap-11 arm fuses at most 2/10, reproducing the shares that the
crossing's own measurement reported (5/5, 7/15, 2/15); the threshold is therefore "gap 8 at 1.0 and gap 11 at or below 0.2". If either arm is out of its expected band the item stops and says so: the arms are
supposed to sit on the two sides of the crossing, and an arm that does not is not evidence either way.

## 3. Readout exists and varies

For every sampled step before the run becomes one component, the corridor is read with the X170 definition between the two
largest components (centres taken from the log's own component list, which for these arms is the pair itself). The readout per
run is **the maximum of that series**, `c_max`, normalised by the peak field at the same step. It must vary across runs
(standard deviation at least 0.02) and must be below 1 in every run, else the readout is degenerate.

## 4. Sensitivity arithmetic

The prediction is a separation, not a difference of means: with ten runs per arm and a pooled spread of about 0.04, a threshold
placed between the arms' `c_max` distributions can be resolved if their medians differ by more than 0.05 (about 1.2 pooled
spreads across two groups of ten), and the claim will be stated as the number of runs misclassified by the best single
threshold, not as a p-value. A perfect or near-perfect split (0-2 misclassified of 30) is the outcome the criterion predicts; a
large overlap falsifies the predictive reading.

## 5. Refusal rule

If `c_max` separates the fusing from the non-fusing arms with at most two misclassified runs out of thirty, the item is recorded
as **the crossing is predictable from the bridge alone**: the share of runs that fuse at a gap is the share whose bridge build-up
clears the threshold, and the threshold is X171's constant times a rate-dependent factor. If the split misclassifies three or
more, the item is recorded as **the corridor is a readout of contact, not a predictor of it**, and the criterion is demoted to a
description of the moment of merging.

## 6. What would kill it

`c_max` for a non-fusing pair is not a neutral quantity: the bodies keep growing and the bridge keeps building, so the two arms'
maxima may both approach the same value and differ only in whether they crossed it in the sampled window. If the dumps stop
before the build-up is over, the non-fusing arm's `c_max` is censored and the test is biased towards a split. The report will
state the last sampled step of each arm so that the censoring is visible, and any run whose `c_max` occurs at its final sampled
step will be flagged as censored rather than counted as a clean negative.

## 7. Implementation notes

Runner `scripts/x173_bridge.py` (three arms × ten seeds at `F` = 0.030, radius 3, 3 000 steps so that the gap-11 arm has time to
build a bridge it would have to cross, field dumps every ten steps into a gitignored directory), analysis
`scripts/x173_bridge_analyse.py` (per-step corridor series, `c_max`, censoring flags, the best single threshold and its
misclassification count). Aggregate `morphogenesis/experiments/E09_pond/l1_field_x173_bridge.json`. Cost about 20 minutes of CPU.
