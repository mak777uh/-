# X130 — sisters rarely merge back, and when they do it takes 60–120 steps

**Plan:** [`2026-11-03-x130-remerge-plan.md`](2026-11-03-x130-remerge-plan.md) (05:35).
**Aggregate:** `l1_field_x130_remerge.json`.

## What it asked

X128b found the absorber is a **sibling** in 54.6 % of lattice absorptions and X129 found this is not a distance effect. X130 asked the dynamic version: a split
produces two daughters standing next to each other — **do they merge back, and after how long?**

## The numbers

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.030 | 1 142 | 210 | **18.4 %** (16.1–20.8) | 81.6 % | 8.99 cells | 8.06 / 9.27 | **80 steps** (70–90) |
| 0.035 | 1 106 | 95 | **8.6 %** (7.1–10.3) | 91.4 % | 12.62 cells | 9.46 / 13.35 | 120 steps (90–150) |

All controls held: the sibling-absorption census **reproduces X128b exactly** (186 / 31 / 57 — the same local budget and partner rule, computed independently in this
script), end-survival reproduces X126 to four decimals, heredity is 0.548 / 0.421 / 0.480, and every cell has more than 1 100 usable sister pairs.

## The answer, with its two denominators

The registered rule called this at 18.4 % against a 25 % bar: **sisters separate**. Most sister pairs never meet again inside the 6 000-step run (82 % censored), and
the merge, when it happens, is fast — a median of 80 steps in the lattice, 60 in the maze, 120 in the middle cell, i.e. 6–12 logging quanta.

That does **not** contradict the 54.6 % sibling share of absorptions, and the two numbers must be quoted with their own denominators, which are different:

- **among sister pairs** (denominator 1 142): 18.4 % ever re-merge;
- **among absorptions with a partner** (denominator 290): 64 % take a sibling (186 of 290, X128b).

Both are true because absorption is a **rare event concentrated in the minority of pairs born close**: in the maze the median birth separation is 22.3 cells, the
merged pairs were born at 11.75 and the unmerged at 24.52 — a factor of two at the same inflow. So the lattice is not a world where families continuously collapse
into each other; it is a world where the **rare** collision that does happen is a family one, and it happens fast.

## What it adds to the chain

1. The middle cell is again the outlier in the direction its survival requires: the **lowest** re-merge share (8.6 %) and the **fewest** sibling absorptions (31), which
   is the same cell where a large lineage survives best (+0.2984). Fewer family collisions, more persistence.
2. Birth separation is the variable that decides a merge (a factor of two between merged and unmerged pairs in the maze, 8.06 against 9.27 in the lattice) — a
   geometric handle that a future manipulation could move.
3. The kinship mechanism of X129 is now timed: the neighbour that takes the cells is the sister, and the whole event is over in ~80 steps.

## Two defects in my own script, both caught by the controls

1. The survival readout was computed on the daughters in **birth order** instead of on the larger/smaller pair, so the differences cancelled: it printed 0.0219
   where X126 says 0.2496. The control that demanded reproduction caught it before the run was interpreted. **Rule:** when a statistic is a difference inside a pair,
   the pair's ordering must be applied in *every* block that uses the pair, not only in the block where it was defined.
2. The interval printed next to the median time was a bootstrap of the **mean** — a median of 80 with an interval [127, 211] that does not contain it. Fixed to
   bootstrap the statistic that is quoted. **Rule:** an interval belongs to the statistic it is printed with; a bootstrap of a cousin is not a conservative version,
   it is a different claim.

## Caveats

- Censoring is 82–91 %: a pair that did not merge inside 6 000 steps may merge later. The median time is therefore a median over the pairs that merged *early*, not
  over all merges, and it is quoted as such.
- A re-merge is detected through the surviving sister's position only; a merge into a *cousin* while the sister is gone is not counted here (X128b counts kin more
  broadly).
- Ten worlds per cell, one seed set, one box, the 0.20 cut.
