# X147 — the radius-4.5 transition: is the boundary at the same surface gap?

**Why.** X142 measured the transition from mutual coalescence to absorption/persistence at radius 3 and found it at a
surface gap of 8–9 cells: every seed coalesces out to gap 8, and gap 9 is 3 of 5. The same question has never been put
to the second radius, and the arms that would answer it are **already in git** — X140 ran radius 4.5 at separations 16,
18, 20 and 22, which are surface gaps 7, 9, 11 and 13. Read with the tolerance-free classes of §10hg they give:

| radius | gap 7 | gap 8 | gap 9 | gap 10 | gap 11 | gap 13 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | M 5/5 | **M 5/5** | M 3/5, A 2/5 | M 2/5, A 2/5, P 1/5 | M 2/5, P 3/5 | – |
| 4.5 | M 5/5 | **not measured** | M 2/5, A 2/5, P 1/5 | – | P 5/5 | P 5/5 |

The two radii already agree at gap 7 (5/5 coalescence) and at gap 9 (mixed, both): the break is bracketed by a
missing measurement at radius 4.5 and gap 8.

## 1. Manipulation is connected

Same probe (`--init-spots R:SEP`, seed-dependent placement, anchored and re-verified bit-for-bit by X142's probe
control), same inflow `F = 0.030`, same seeds 601–605, same 6 000 steps, same dense logging every 10 steps. The
manipulation is the **surface gap**, and it is measured at a second radius, which is what separates a gap rule from a
radius-3 artefact.

| arm | separation | runs | source |
|---|---|---|---|
| radius 4.5, gap **8** | **17** | **5 new** | this run |
| radius 4.5, gaps 7, 9, 11, 13 | 16, 18, 20, 22 | 20 | reused from X140 |
| radius 3, gaps 7, 8, 9 | 13, 14, 15 | 15 | reused from X142 / X136 |

## 2. Positive control fires (numeric)

Four controls. **The control fires if** all four hold:

1. **The reused radius-4.5 arms classify as recorded above** (gap 7: M 5/5; gap 9: M 2 A 2 P 1; gap 11: P 5/5; gap 13:
   P 5/5). A change means the readout moved.
2. **The new arm starts as two components** of area ≥ 20 cells at the first logged step.
3. **The new arm's world never empties** (occupancy ≥ 5 % of the grid at the last step).
4. **The radius-3 comparison arms reproduce X142** (gap 7: M 5/5; gap 8: M 5/5; gap 9: M 3 A 2).

## 3. Readout exists and varies

The outcome is a discrete class per seed and it already spans all three values in the population (M at gaps 7–8, mixed
at 9, P at 11–13), so variation is established at the resolution used before the new arm is run.

## 4. Sensitivity arithmetic

Five seeds per arm, three classes; the boundary is bracketed when an arm is 5/5 M and its neighbour is mixed. The
resolution is **one cell** in the gap and one seed in the count. The declared minimal effect is the difference between
"5/5 M" and "at most 3/5 M", i.e. two seeds, which is what the existing arms already show at gap 7 against gap 9 on both
radii.

## 5. Refusal rule (fixed before the run)

**Void** if a reused arm misses its recorded classes (control 1 or 4), if the new arm does not start as two components,
or if it ends empty.

The registered outcomes:

- **The boundary is the same at both radii**: radius 4.5 gap 8 is M 5/5. Then radius 3 and radius 4.5 both coalesce in
  every seed out to gap 8 and both are mixed at gap 9, and the crossing is a property of the surface gap alone — the
  strongest form of the gap rule, at one-cell resolution.
- **The boundary moves inward with the radius**: radius 4.5 gap 8 is at most 3/5 M. Then the bigger structures start
  failing a cell earlier in gap — a different rule, and the report must say by how much and in which direction.
- **Indeterminate**: radius 4.5 gap 8 is 4/5 M. Then the two recesses differ by one seed, the sweep is unresolved at
  this resolution, and the next run doubles the seeds at gaps 8 and 9 rather than adding gaps.
