# X169 — The seam probe failed its own control; the body is a dumbbell while it lasts (04.11)

**Question.** X167b ended with a negative it could not turn into a positive: components cannot say whether a fused body is the
same object as its parts, because any elongated blob divides at its ends and gives the same geometry. The only remaining
discriminator is the field — a preserved seam should show up as structure between the two parents' positions after the merge.

Pre-registration: [`2026-11-04-x169-seam-plan.md`](2026-11-04-x169-seam-plan.md). Six runs (a fusing pair at radius 3, surface
gap 8, separation 14; and a single body from the default seed, as the control), three seeds each, 1 500 steps, the field
dumped every 10 steps.

## The pre-registered measure failed, and that comes first

The plan defined a bimodality index as the saddle between the outermost local maxima along the parents' axis, over the larger
of them, with a floor of 0.9 for the single-body control. Measured on the dumps, the control's index is **0.05**, not 0.9: a
body's cross-section is a **ring**, peaking on its two rims, so a single body has a central dip of its own and the "outermost
local maxima" land on the shoulders of one hump. The measure had no headroom, its own refusal rule fired, and the
pre-registered question is **unanswered**.

A second attempt — the central dip, `min` over the middle third of the segment over the profile's peak — fails the same
control for the same reason (0.03 against a 0.9 floor). Both are recorded in the aggregate's `measure_note`.

## What the probe did establish, and what it did not

Two facts come out of the dumps without any index:

1. **The pattern fades fast.** The peak falls from 0.75 at step 10 to 0.30 at step 60, 0.12 at step 120 and 0.02 at step 300,
   in both arms. There is no long-lived field to relax or to keep a seam: whatever the merge does, it does it inside about
   two hundred steps and then the light goes out.
2. **While the light lasts, the merged body is a dumbbell.** An auxiliary measurement — not pre-registered, and labelled as
   such — takes the body's width above threshold 0.2 along the perpendicular axis at the midpoint between the parents'
   positions, and the same width through the lobes. For the pair, from step 50 to step 120, the waist is four to five cells
   against lobes of ten to fourteen: a **median ratio of 0.36** (values 0.25-0.63). A single round body has no lobes to
   compare with, so the ratio is undefined-to-≥1 there — the comparison is asymmetric by construction and that is a weakness,
   not a result.

So the honest statement is: **after the two components merge, the field is pinched between the parents' positions — the union
is a bridge with two lobes, not a round blob — and the pattern dissolves before the question of relaxation can be asked.**

## Why this does not rescue "identity"

A dumbbell that has not had time to relax is exactly what a *blob* would look like too, for a while: the neck is the geometry
of a merge in progress. Distinguishing "a seam that persists" from "a neck that is still closing" needs a world where the
pattern outlives the merge, and this world does not have one — the field is gone in 300 steps.

## What would make it answerable

1. **A long-lived field.** The whole line's fusion work happens in the first hundred steps because the pattern is brightest
   then; a run with the inflow held at a level where the field persists for thousands of steps (the pond line's regime, not
   the field line's) would let the neck either close or persist.
2. **A symmetric control.** A single **elongated** body of the merged area, so the waist ratio has a meaningful counterpart
   rather than an undefined one.
3. **A measure that respects the ring.** Any intensity profile normalised by the peak is confounded by the annulus; a measure
   based on the body's **skeleton** (the number of separate ridges inside one component) would not be.

## What this changes

The fusion line cannot settle "identity" — not because the answer is no, but because the probe this world affords gives a
dumbbell and then the field dies. §4k.3 should say that the question is **open and needs a different regime**, rather than
implying that the component-level negative of X167b settles it. That phrasing is now in the article.

## Next hypotheses

1. **The seam in the pond regime**, where the field persists (X160's world, 1 800 steps of a live field): the same neck
   measurement, with a symmetric elongated control.
2. **A skeleton measure**: count separate ridges inside one connected component, which is immune to the annulus.
3. **The neck's closing time** as a function of the inflow rate: if the neck closes in a time that scales with `F`, the seam
   is a transient of the merge and the question becomes how long a pattern must live to keep it.
