# X132 — the power run: thirty worlds resolve the survival contrast the ten worlds could not

**Plan:** [`2026-11-03-x132-power-plan.md`](2026-11-03-x132-power-plan.md) (06:31, size set by a power calculation).
**Aggregate:** `l1_field_x132_power.json`. **Series:** forty new worlds (twenty per cell, seeds 311–330), anchor exact, 60–98 s each.

## What was asked

X131 showed that the contrast the whole consequence chain rests on — the larger sister's lineage survives more often in the lattice than in the maze — was **not
resolved** by ten worlds: −0.077 with an interval of −0.166…+0.012, the world-to-world spread (sd 0.14) being as large as the effect. The power calculation said
`n ≥ 26`; thirty worlds per cell were run.

## The result: everything resolves, and the effect is larger than ten worlds suggested

| statistic | lattice | maze | **maze − lattice (paired, 30 seeds)** | outcome |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **end-survival contrast** | 0.2960 (sd 0.1023) | 0.1636 (sd 0.1010) | **−0.1323** (−0.1819…−0.0840) | **resolved** |
| **depth contrast** | 1.4889 (sd 0.5885) | 3.3229 (sd 2.2803) | **+1.8340** (1.0442…2.7231) | **resolved** |
| absorbed share | 0.5386 | 0.4430 | −0.0911 (−0.1580…−0.0330) | resolved |
| isolated share | 0.2708 | 0.1930 | −0.0754 (−0.1211…−0.0235) | resolved |
| vanished share | 0.0929 | 0.2347 | **+0.1419** (0.1103…0.1716) | resolved |
| kin share | 0.8058 | 0.3894 | **−0.4164** (−0.4708…−0.3631) | resolved |

All five controls held: the anchor is exact key-by-key against `x108_f030_s301.json` (`uid_pairs` identical), the ten old worlds reproduce X131's per-world means
and spreads to 0.005, every one of the forty new runs is alive under the structural rule, heredity is above the floor in both cells, and every new world contributes
both sister pairs and deaths.

**The ten-world estimate was too small, not wrong.** The paired difference grows from −0.077 (ten seeds) to **−0.132** (thirty), and the per-world mean survival
rises in both cells (lattice 0.222 → 0.296, maze 0.146 → 0.164) because the new seeds are not a random subsample of the old ones. That is worth stating plainly:
a ten-world estimate of this quantity is not only imprecise, it was **biased low** by the particular ten seeds, and the correction came from adding worlds rather
than from re-reading them.

## What this restores

X131's correction stands in its method and is now superseded in its conclusion: the survival contrast **is** a property of the habitat, at −0.132 with the interval
comfortably away from zero. The depth contrast is likewise resolved and is **larger** than before (+1.83 against +1.05 on ten worlds), with the maze's per-world
depth spread (sd 2.28) nearly four times the lattice's (0.59) — the maze is not only deeper, it is far more variable.

The rest of the identity block is confirmed at the world level in the same run: the lattice absorbs more (0.539 against 0.443), dies alone more often (0.271 against
0.193), loses fewer cells outright (0.093 against 0.235), and keeps its absorptions in the family (0.806 against 0.389).

## The cost, and what it says about the block's other numbers

Forty runs, 50 minutes, 11.5 MB of committed projections, no GPU training. That is cheap enough that the rule should now be: **any cross-habitat claim carries a
power calculation before it is written**, and ten worlds is the default only for within-world effects where the inference is already over thousands of pairs.

A corollary worth recording: five of the block's cross-habitat claims (the death fates, the kinship, the survival and depth contrasts) were all resolvable at thirty
worlds with the same budget, and the one that looked fragile at ten (survival) turned out to be the second largest of them.

## Caveats

- Thirty worlds per cell, one seed set extended (301–330), one box, the 0.20 cut; the paired bootstrap has thirty pairs, so it is coarse but no longer marginal.
- The new seeds are not exchangeable with the old by construction — they are a contiguous block (311–330) — so a drift between seed blocks would masquerade as an
  effect. The per-world values are printed in the aggregate so the block structure can be checked, and the ten old and twenty new worlds agree in sign in both
  cells.
- Depth's per-world spread in the maze (sd 2.28) is of the same order as its mean (3.32), so the maze's depth is a heavy-tailed quantity, not a level.

## Next hypotheses

   the body, that is a different mechanism from "the maze is deeper".
2. **X134 — the mixing-length intervention.** The chain now says segregation keeps lineages and mixing breaks them. The direct test is a world seeded with two

   `field_track.py`, an exact anchor, and it is the first interventional test of the whole consequence chain.
3. **X135 — the cost of the claim.** With thirty worlds the cross-habitat numbers are settled; the remaining question for the owner's goal is not "does the habitat
   matter" but "which lever moves it", and the two candidates the block has produced are spatial segregation (mixing length) and the split distance (X130).
