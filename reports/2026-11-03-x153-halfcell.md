# X153 — the outcome steps, the time mixes

**Plan:** [`2026-11-03-x153-halfcell-plan.md`](2026-11-03-x153-halfcell-plan.md) (19:58).
**Aggregate:** `l1_field_x153_halfcell.json`. **Runs:** 10 new (radius 6.0, separation 19.5, surface gap 7.5, seeds
601–610).

## The arm against its two neighbours

| surface gap | seeds coalescing | their dissolution steps |
|---|---|---|
| 7.0 | 5 of 5 | 39, 39, 39, 39, 39 |
| **7.5** | **10 of 10** | **39 ×8, 49 ×2** |
| 8.0 | 3 of 5 | 49, 49, 59 |

The registered outcome that fires is the first: **10 of 10 coalesce**, so the half-cell does not move the boundary. It
stays where the sweep put it — the last integer gap at which coalescence is universal, 8 cells for the 28-cell disc and 7
for the 64-, 87- and 113-cell ones.

The interesting part is the second column. **The half-cell is visible, but only in the time.** Two of ten seeds dissolve at
49 (the step gap 8 uses) while eight keep gap 7's 39; the median stays 39. So the outcome is a step and the time is a
**mix**: the half-cell buys a fifth of the seeds one extra logged step, without changing any seed's fate.

That is the third time these two laws behave differently — the time follows the gap to the step while coalescence is
universal and grows a tail exactly where the boundary is crossed (X151), and now the crossing itself is resolved: the
tail exists *before* the boundary, at 7.5, where every seed still coalesces.

## Two things this arm does not do

1. **The half-cell was not free.** Separating the discs by 19.5 instead of 19 moved the rasterised discs by one cell each:
   the first logged frame has areas **110 against 108**, so the arm is a gap *plus* about 2 % of area per disc. The
   confound is conservative — a larger disc is the case where the boundary breaks earlier — so "10/10 at 7.5" cannot be an
   artefact of the discs being smaller. But it is not a pure gap manipulation, and the report says so.
2. **A sub-quantum time change would be invisible.** The dissolution step is logged every 10 steps, so 39 and 49 are the
   only two values available to a slow-or-not seed; a real shift of 4 steps could not be seen. The outcome count, not the
   time, is the primary readout of this arm.

## The lesson about controls

Control 1 of the registration asked that the artifact record a surface gap of 7.5 "not rounded to 7 or 8" — and it
passed, because `params.init_spots` says `6.0:19.5`. That check was on the **parameter**, not on the **field**: had the
probe rounded the separation internally, the parameter would still have read 19.5 and the arm would have been a silent
duplicate of gap 7. The field check (first logged frame: 110 cells per disc against 108) is the one that proves the
manipulation arrived, and it was not in the registration. `docs/design-checks.md` gains the rule.

## What the block says now

1. **Time**: 29 / 39 / 49 logged steps at gaps 6 / 7 / 8, exact per seed while coalescence is universal; at gap 7.5 eight
   of ten keep 39 and two slip to 49. So the time is the gap's, quantised by the logging step, with a mixing tail as the
   boundary is approached.
2. **Outcome**: universal to a gap of 8 cells at 28 cells of disc area, to 7 at 64, 87 and 113; the half-cell at 7.5 does
   not break it. The boundary is a whole-cell step, and the move between radii is a single step between 28 and 64 cells.
3. **They interact once**, and now we know which side of the crossing the interaction lives on: the timing tail starts
   **before** the outcome weakens.

## Controls

Ten usable runs; every arm starts as two components (110 cells each) and none ends empty; `params.init_spots` records
`6.0:19.5`; the median time 39 lies inside the registered bracket [39, 49]; medians carry their n, and seeds 601–605 are
reported separately so the arm is comparable with its neighbours.
