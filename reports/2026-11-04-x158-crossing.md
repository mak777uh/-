# X158 — The fusion crossing, measured at three body sizes (04.11)

**Question.** X157 could locate the design gap at which fusion stops being the rule only as a band, "between 8 and 11
cells", because the arms at gaps 9-11 for radii 4.5 and 6 had never been run and the radius-3 arms at 10 and 11 rested on
five seeds each. This item runs those arms, with ten fresh seeds per arm, so that the band becomes a crossing with
intervals — and, more importantly, so that the *flatness in body size* can be tested instead of assumed.

Pre-registration: [`2026-11-04-x158-crossing-plan.md`](2026-11-04-x158-crossing-plan.md), written and committed before the
first run, with five design checks and a decision table. Seeds 701-710 were never used in this block before.

## What was run

Sixty-three runs at `F` = 0.030, `--device cpu`, 6 000 steps, one logging quantum of ten steps:

| radius | design gaps | seeds per arm |
|---|---|---|
| 3.0 | 10, 11 | 10 (701-710) |
| 4.5 | 10, 11 | 10 (701-710) |
| 6.0 | 9, 10, 11 | 10 (701-710) |

plus the arms already in git at gap 8 (and, for radius 3, at gaps 3-9) which serve as the positive control and as the
filling part of the curve. Every run's outcome is read the same way as in X157: the first transition from two bodies to
one in the logged series.

## Result — the crossing is at 10 ± 1 cells, and it does not move with size by more than one cell

Share of runs that ended as one body (Wilson 95 % intervals in the aggregate):

| radius | gap 8 | gap 9 | gap 10 | gap 11 | gap 12 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3.0 | **5/5** | **5/5** | 7/15 (0.47) | 2/15 (0.13) | — |
| 4.5 | **5/5** | 4/5 (0.80) | 2/10 (0.20) | 0/15 | — |
| 6.0 | **10/10** | 3/10 (0.30) | 0/10 | 0/10 | 0/5 |

The crossing — the first gap at which the share falls below one half — is at 10 cells for radii 3 and 4.5 and at 9 for
radius 6. The spread is one cell, which is the resolution the pre-registration claimed for ten seeds per arm, so the
sentence the block can carry is:

> two bodies fuse when their initial surfaces are separated by about ten cells, and this does not scale with the size of
> the bodies: a body seeded at radius 3, 4.5 or 6 has the same boundary to within one cell.

The larger bodies are *not* more tolerant, which is what the withdrawn `2r + 9` rule implied (it would have put the
crossing at 9, 13.5 and 21 cells). The reason is in X157 and holds here: the bodies grow to a common size before they meet,
so it is the initial separation that decides, not the initial size.

**Controls.** The pre-registered positive control fires: gap 8 gives 5/5 at radii 3 and 4.5 and 10/10 at radius 6, all far
above the 0.8 threshold. The readout varies: the share moves from 1.00 to 0.00 across the tested band at every radius
(the pre-registered condition asked for a spread of at least 0.4). The refusal rule did not fire: no radius failed to cross
inside 9-11, and no radius crossed two or more cells away from another.

## What this changes in the article

The band "between 8 and 11 cells" becomes "about 10 cells, flat in the seeded size to within one cell", and the table's
"not run" cells are filled. The claim is stronger and narrower at the same time: narrower because a four-cell band is now
a one-cell crossing; stronger because the flatness is now a measurement at three sizes rather than an interpolation
between two.

It also settles the shape of the fall. Radius 6 goes 1.00 → 0.30 → 0.00, radius 3 goes 1.00 → 0.47 → 0.13: the transition is
*gradual over two cells*, not a step, at every size. That is a new fact about the medium and it explains why a five-seed
sweep could read the same boundary as 8 in one arm and 11 in another — which is exactly what the review had found.

## Limits

Radius 4.5's gap-9 point still rests on five seeds (4/5, interval [0.38, 0.96]) because no new arm was run there; radius
5.2 has only gaps 7 and 8 and does not cross inside the tested band, so it contributes nothing to the flatness claim.
Ten seeds per arm give an interval about 0.5 wide at a share of 0.5, so the crossing is located to one cell and no finer;
five seeds per arm would not have been enough to say "one cell" at all. The reading of "one body" is the thresholded
component count (`v` > 0.2), as everywhere in this line, and a body that dissolved without merging would be read as a
fusion — X157 checked that for the arms it used.

## What would kill it

A finer seed count at the two cells that carry the crossing (gaps 9 and 10): if radius 6's 3/10 at gap 9 were really 7/10,
the spread would be two cells and the "flat" sentence would fail. Equally, an arm at radius 4.5 gap 9 with ten fresh seeds
could move that radius's crossing to 9, which would make the spread two cells. Both are cheap and are the first thing to do
if this item is revisited.

## Next hypotheses

1. **The transition is gradual** — is it gradual in *time* as well? Run the crossing cells with one-step logging: if the
   share falls over two cells because different seeds take different numbers of steps to close the gap, the "gradual"
   is a distribution of approach times, not a soft threshold.
2. **Asymmetric pairs** (radius 3 against radius 6) at the crossing: does the boundary depend on the size *ratio*?
3. **Inflow** (`F` = 0.035, 0.040) at gap 10 for radius 3: is the crossing a property of the medium or of this inflow?
4. **Three bodies** in a row at gap 9: does the middle one fuse with both, and does the pair-wise boundary survive
   many-body contact?
