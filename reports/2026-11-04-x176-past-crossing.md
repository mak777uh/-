# X176 — The arm never fused: the prediction's premise is confirmed, its readout is not testable (04.11)

**Question.** X175 refuted X174's single curve and left a two-factor picture: the corridor at contact rises with the arm's
marginality and with the initial gap. This item tested it where both factors point the same way — **radius 6 at surface gap
10**, one cell past that size's crossing (radius 6 crosses at gap 9), so the arm should be marginal and its bridge should span
the longest distance of any arm in the plane.

Pre-registered before the run: **share 0.05-0.25** and **corridor 0.50-0.55**.

## Result: nobody fused, so there is no corridor

| readout | predicted | observed |
|---|---|---|
| fusion share | 0.05-0.25 | **0.00** (0 of 15) |
| corridor at contact | 0.50-0.55 | **not measurable** (no contact event) |

The registered rule covers this: with fewer than three contact events the arm is recorded as **undecided** rather than as a
confirmation or a refutation, and neither number is claimed. But the run is not empty, because it makes the premise sharper
than the plan did: radius 6 at gap 10 does not fuse *sometimes*, it fuses **never** — 0 of 15 here, on top of 0 of 10 in X158,
so **0 of 25**. The prediction of a low share was right in kind and slightly wrong in fact.

## What this says about the two-factor picture

Nothing yet, and it is worth being precise about why. The picture predicted a corridor at a cell that turned out to have no
merges to read: to test the band's upper edge one needs an arm that is marginal but still fuses, and this arm overshot into
"never". A better cell for the same question is **radius 4.5 at gap 11** (that size crosses at 10, so gap 11 should fuse rarely
but not never) or **radius 3 at gap 11 with a colder source**.

The one thing the item does contribute is a bound: at radius 6 the gap-10 share is 0 of 25, and since X175's marginal arms all
had shares of 0.27 or more, the (share, corridor) relation has no measured point below 0.27. The band's upper edge therefore
rests on the three arms at 0.27 (0.437, 0.492, 0.516) and on nothing more marginal.

## Limits

Zero contact events: no corridor, no spread, no comparison. The registered corridor interval was not tested and must not be
counted as either hit or miss.

## What would kill it

Nothing — there is no measurement. The next cell must be chosen so that fusion still happens: radius 4.5 at gap 11, or radius 3
at gap 11 in the cold medium (`F` = 0.026), where X170's ladder gives a share of about 0.1.

## Next hypotheses

1. **Radius 4.5 at gap 11** — past that size's crossing, expected to fuse a few times, which is exactly the marginal-but-alive
   cell the band's upper edge needs.
2. **The share's floor.** Radius 6 goes from 3/10 at gap 9 to 0/25 at gap 10 — a fall from 0.3 to 0.0 within one cell. Whether
   that is a plateau or a cliff decides whether the crossing is a threshold at all, and it is cheap to check at radius 6, gap 9
   with more seeds.
3. **The band measured by geometry rather than by marginality.** Running a fixed radius at a gap well below its crossing and
   reading the corridor at the moment of contact would place a point on the band without relying on how lucky the arm is.
