# X159 — Is the 1.9-cell contact gap a threshold, or the size of a logging step? (04.11)

**Question.** X157 replaced the published "9 ± 1 cells for all sizes" with a measured quantity: at the last logged step
at which the two bodies are still separate, their surfaces are a median of 1.9 cells apart (range 0.9-4.9 over 132 fusing
runs), the same at every seeded size. That number has an obvious vulnerability. The log samples every tenth integration
step, and the bodies grow between samples, so 1.9 is an **upper bound** on the gap at which fusion actually becomes
possible, not the threshold itself. If the growth hidden inside one logging quantum is comparable to the number, then
"fusion at about two cells" is a statement about our sampling rate, not about the medium.

**Method.** No new runs. For every two-body arm of the block, find the first transition from two bodies to one in the
logged series (the same rule as X157), take the last two logged two-body steps before it, and measure the growth of the
mean effective radius there: `dr/dt` over the logged interval, times the logging quantum (`sample_every`, ten steps), times
two, is how much the sum of the two radii grew unobserved. The threshold is then bounded as

    g_threshold  in  [ g_last − 2·(dr/dt)·quantum ,  g_last ]

with `g_last` the 1.9-cell reading. 119 fusing runs carry enough logged two-body steps to be estimated; the growth is
taken as linear over one quantum, which makes the window an **over**-estimate of its own width, so the test is
conservative against the claim.

Run: `python scripts/x157_quantum.py --write`.

**Result — the number survives as a bound, not as a threshold.**

| radius | runs | `g_last` (median) | window width (median) | lower bound (median) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3.0 | 30 | 1.99 | 0.94 | 0.90 |
| 4.5 | 31 | 1.93 | 1.49 | −0.29 |
| 5.2 | 10 | 1.69 | 1.04 | 0.66 |
| 6.0 | 48 | 2.12 | 1.34 | 0.74 |
| all | 119 | **1.9** | **1.33** | **0.70** |

So the pair is still two bodies while their surfaces are 1.9 cells apart, and one logged step later it is one body; the
threshold itself lies between 0.7 and 1.9 cells at the median, and only in 21 % of runs does the lower bound stay above
two cells. At radius 4.5 the lower bound crosses zero, i.e. the data there cannot even exclude fusion at contact in the
geometric sense.

**What this changes.** The article's sentence "at contact the surfaces are 1.9 cells apart" becomes "the pair remains two
bodies at 1.9 cells and is merged within the next logging step, so the threshold is at most 1.9 cells and its window is
about 1.3 cells wide". The *second* half of X157 — the design gap at which fusion stops being the rule, between 8 and 11
cells — is untouched, because it is a property of the initial condition, not of the sampling.

This is the same class of error as the one the review caught in the first place, one level down: the first was arithmetic
on the design, this one is resolution of the record. It is worth stating plainly, because the value of the correction is
that it can be repeated on itself.

**Limits.** Growth is measured from the two logged steps before fusion and extrapolated linearly over one quantum; the
motion of the centres is ignored (it changes `d`, not the radii); the "one body" rule is the same thresholded
connected-component count as everywhere in the line, so a body that dissolves without merging would look like a fusion
(the X157 report checked this for the arms it used).

**What would kill it.** A one-step log of a few arms would settle the window directly: if the threshold turns out to sit
within 0.2 cells of the same value across radii, then 1.9 was a threshold after all and the window was our resolution. If
it scatters, the honest statement is a band, and the "about two cells, independent of size" sentence becomes "at most two
cells", which is weaker but true.

**Next hypotheses.** (1) Re-run five arms (radius 3 at gaps 8 and 9, radius 6 at gap 8, radius 4.5 at gap 9, radius 5.2
at gap 7) with `--sample-every 1` for 2 000 steps, five seeds each: the threshold becomes measurable to a tenth of a cell.
(2) The same one-step logging on a fusing pair with a **seeded asymmetry** (different radii) to see whether the threshold
depends on size *ratio* rather than on absolute size. (3) Check whether the threshold moves with `F`, which would make it a
property of the medium rather than of the pair.
