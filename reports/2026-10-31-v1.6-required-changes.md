Three findings qualify what is currently published:

| finding | where it is recorded | what it contradicts |

## Edit 1 — §4.5, the heterogeneity result

**Now** (v1.5.1):

> If the inflow is spatially heterogeneous, the number of components is roughly **twice** as large, and this is stable…

**Proposed:**

> If the inflow is spatially heterogeneous, the number of components is larger at every step size tested, but **the size of
> the effect belongs to the numerical scheme, not to the equations**. Averaged over 6000 units of physical time and ten
> seeds, the contrast is **+3.57 components at `dt = 1.0`** and **+1.24 at `dt = 0.5`** — the sign survives, the magnitude
> does not (§10dv). At `dt = 2.0` the explicit scheme is unstable and the comparison is uninformative.
>
> The contrast is also **not stationary**: split by quarters of physical time it falls from +4.00 to a plateau of
> **+1.00** at `dt = 1.0` and to **0.00** at `dt = 0.5` (§10dw). The figure quoted above is therefore a time integral that
> includes an early transient, not a steady-state property. Both estimators are lawful and give different numbers
> (+3.57 as a median over per-seed time means, +1.00 as a pooled median of slices); we name which one we use wherever a
> number appears.

## Edit 2 — §4.7, the grid comparison

**Now:**

> Difference is reproduced on a 128 grid: 5.64 versus 3.71 (n = 20)…

**Proposed:** keep the number, change the label:

> Difference is reproduced on a 128 grid: 5.64 versus 3.71 (n = 20). **This is not a refinement test.** In this model
> family the spatial resolution enters as a `1/Δx²` coefficient multiplying the diffusion terms (Davis 2023, DOI
> `10.54854/imi2023.01`), so holding `Du` fixed while changing the grid changes the effective diffusion rather than the
> accuracy. What the 128-grid run shows is that the effect survives a change of scale; a genuine grid-convergence test
> requires rescaling `Du` and `Dv` and is queued (`X58`).

## Edit 3 — §3.1, methods

**Add one sentence after the numerical scheme** (which is already there since v1.5.1):

> The grid of 96 is **below the range tested in the founding study of this model** — Pearson (1993, *Science*
> 261:189–192, DOI `10.1126/science.261.5118.189`) reports no qualitative difference for time steps from 1.0 to 0.01 and
> grids from 256 to 1024 — so our numbers are a statement about this coarse scheme, not about the continuum.

## Edit 4 — abstract

**Now:** "…durable structures are the same in heterogeneous and homogeneous worlds (1.3 versus 1.2)…" — unaffected.

**Add after the first quantitative sentence:**

> The heterogeneity effect strengthens with the time step: it is +3.57 components at `dt = 1.0` and +1.24 at `dt = 0.5`,
> and in steady state it falls to +1.00 and 0.00 respectively — a property of the discrete scheme, reported as such.

## What this does **not** license

Writing "the effect does not transfer to the continuum because X". We do not know `X`: the window in `dt` is measured,
the mechanism behind it is not. Per §10dz, only "narrow the claim" is available to us, not "name the mechanism".
