## What the two readouts say, and why only one of them can be trusted

**Lifetime is the honest readout, and it is small and changes sign.** In the lattice (`F = 0.025`, `0.030`) a larger structure does live
longer, but weakly (+0.19, +0.11). In the percolating arm (0.035, 0.040) the sign **reverses**: bigger components are *less* durable
(−0.07, −0.05). That is consistent with the morphology: in a maze the "components" are temporary thickenings of a structure that keeps
rearranging, so the biggest ones are precisely the ones about to be cut. Median lifetime collapses across the same range — 424 steps at
0.025 against 89 at 0.040.

**The daughters correlation is large and probably mechanical.** An out-degree is counted from `uid_pairs`, and with the split convention a
component that divides is by construction the large one: the same object appears on both sides of the correlation. Shuffling areas breaks
the pairing but cannot break that structural link, so the 0.62–0.86 says "big things are the ones that split", not "size buys
descendants". I am not quoting it as evidence of consequence.

## What would make the question answerable

The clean form is a **sibling contrast**: among daughters of the same mother, does the larger sister leave more daughters of her own? That
holds the mother, the place and the moment fixed, and it cannot be produced by the mechanical link, because it compares sisters rather
than areas to their own splits. The artifacts already carry the pairs needed to define siblings; what they do not carry is a second
generation deep enough to follow sisters' descendants — that needs a longer horizon or a denser log, i.e. new runs (about 1 GPU-hour).

## Honest status of the third leg

The field line still has **no measurement of consequence**. What this series adds is a negative and a warning: in the percolating arm
larger structures are shorter-lived, so the naive "bigger is fitter" prior is wrong there; and the tempting large correlation between area
and division count is an artefact of the identity rule, not a fitness measurement. X29 failed with an invalid meter; X92 fails with a
mechanical coupling. The third leg needs a design that holds the mother fixed, and it is now specified.
