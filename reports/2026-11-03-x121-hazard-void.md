# X121 — void twice over: the death rate is too low for the band, and the daughters column was identically zero

**Plan:** [`2026-11-03-x121-hazard-plan.md`](2026-11-03-x121-hazard-plan.md) (02:08, registered before the run).
**Aggregate:** `l1_field_x121_hazard.json`.

## Two independent reasons for the void

1. **The registered control failed.** The per-snapshot death rate in the lattice is **0.58 %**, below the declared 1 % floor. With a snapshot every ten steps and a
   horizon of 6 000, a structure that lives the whole run contributes 600 person-period rows and exactly one death at most; the table therefore describes a
   very rare event, and the plan said in advance that a rate below 1 % voids the model.
2. **The daughters column had no variance.** Every coefficient for `daughters` came back as **0.0 with interval [0.0, 0.0]** — the signature of a constant
   column. The table was built but the count never became non-zero, so the coefficient is not a measurement of anything: it is an artefact of a predictor that
   carried no information.

The first reason would have voided the run on its own. The second is a coding defect and is the more serious of the two, because it produced a number that
*looks like* a result — a zero effect with a suspiciously tight interval — and only the rule "report the predictors' variation before their coefficients" makes
it visible.

## What is not claimed

Nothing. The `age` and `log_area` coefficients (age −0.004 / −0.029, size +0.012 / +0.001) are printed in the aggregate and are **not** read here: with one
predictor degenerate and the event rate below the registered floor, the model as a whole is unvalidated, and the remaining coefficients inherit that.

## What the failure teaches

- **A hazard model needs a different snapshot spacing.** With one death per 600 rows, the discrete-time model is measuring a rare event at a resolution the logs
  cannot support. Either the analysis uses every step (not every tenth) or it does not use a hazard model at all.
- **A constant predictor must fail loudly.** The line's analyses now routinely build variables by counting or joining; a column of zeros is the typical result of
  a join that silently matched nothing, and it must be checked before the model runs, not inferred from a coefficient of 0.0 afterwards.
- The registered control did its job: it stopped a model whose event rate made it meaningless, and it fired before the degenerate column was noticed — which is
  why the second defect is recorded rather than published.

## Next

- **Diagnose the join:** the person-period table's daughter count never became positive although the genealogy has 161 pairs in the first world alone. The next
  round starts by printing the count's distribution on one world, then rebuilds the table.
- **Re-register** with (a) every logged step rather than every tenth, so the death rate per row is ~10 %, and (b) a mandatory variance and coverage check for
  every predictor, printed before the coefficients.

## Caveats

- The void is about the **model**, not about the hazard question: the age-versus-division confound remains open, exactly as X119/X120 left it.
- The aggregate is committed as the record of the failed run; its numbers are not to be quoted.
