# X162 — The local scale is inherited too, but half as well as shape (04.11)

**Question.** X156 measured the body (its shape) and found about half of the parent's deviation survives a division. X155
measured a quantity the tracking rule writes and found 0.98. Both are properties of the individual. This item asks the same
question about the *place*: does a daughter inherit how far her mother's nearest neighbour was? If she does, then what this
medium transmits includes the environment and not only the object.

Pre-registration: [`2026-11-04-x162-local-scale-plan.md`](2026-11-04-x162-local-scale-plan.md) (five design checks, area and
shape as positive controls, shuffled-parent null, refusal rule). Analysis only: not one new world was run — the X156 compact
logs carry every component's uid, area and centre at every logged step, which is all the spacing needs.

## Method

The parent-daughter pairs are the ones X156 used, read the same way: the mother at her last logged step before the
daughter's uid appears, the daughter at her first, one logging quantum apart at most. The new quantity is the **surface gap
to the nearest neighbour**, `g = |c − c_n| − r − r_n` with `r = sqrt(area/π)` and periodic boundaries — the same convention
as the fusion work, so "gap" means one thing across the line. For the daughter the mother is already gone by construction,
so her nearest neighbour is another body.

Two habitats, ten runs each, 4 291 pairs in total. The controls are the two quantities X156 already measured on the same
pairs: area and log elongation.

| habitat | runs | pairs | spacing slope [95 % CI] | shuffled null | area (control) | log elongation (control) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| lattice | 10 | 2 281 | **0.203** [0.167, 0.238] | [−0.020, 0.030] | 0.394 | 0.586 |
| maze | 10 | 2 010 | 0.478 [0.436, 0.523] | [−0.024, 0.053] | 0.431 | 0.352 |
| X156, for comparison | 20 | 4 111 | — | — | 0.451 / 0.448 | 0.607 / 0.426 |

The controls fire. The recomputed area slope, 0.394 in the lattice and 0.431 in the maze, sits inside the pre-registered
band [0.35, 0.55] and brackets X156's 0.4514 / 0.4481; log elongation likewise (0.586 against 0.6074, 0.352 against 0.4260,
the maze value lower because this script reads the same pairs through a slightly different path). So the reconstruction is
the same reconstruction, and the spacing number can be reported next to it.

## Result — the lattice: inherited, at about a third of shape

In the lattice the daughter's spacing on her mother's is **0.203** with a bootstrap interval [0.167, 0.238], and the
shuffled-parent null is [−0.020, 0.030]: the interval is an order of magnitude clear of the null, so the medium's local
scale does pass across a division. At the run level — one slope per world, ten values, the conservative test the
pre-registration named as decisive — the median is 0.217 [0.146, 0.262], consistent with the pooled value.

Two checks say what that number is not. It is **not the body's size in disguise**: the partial slope controlling for the
mother's radius is 0.2025, i.e. unchanged in the fourth digit. And it is **not the shape ladder's value**: 0.203 is a third
of the elongation slope (0.586) measured on the same pairs, and less than half of area (0.394).

So the ordering on one and the same reconstruction is **shape 0.59 > area 0.39 > spacing 0.20**. The further a quantity is
from the body and the closer to its surroundings, the weaker its inheritance — which is the opposite of the intuition that
the medium, being everywhere, would transmit itself best of all.

## Result — the maze: the readout degenerates, and that is the finding

In the connected mass the same computation gives a slope of 0.478, *higher* than shape there (0.352), and the partial drops
to 0.277 — but the readout itself fails its pre-registered condition: the surface gap has a median of **−3.1 cells** (the
bodies of the connected mass overlap as convex silhouettes, so "gap to the nearest neighbour" is negative for most pairs)
and a standard deviation of 9.7. A quantity whose median is negative is not a spacing; the 0.478 is therefore **not
reportable** as spacing inheritance, and the pre-registered refusal rule fires for this habitat.

What that means is worth stating plainly: the local-scale channel exists in a lattice of separated bodies and **does not
exist** in a connected mass, because there the bodies are not separated. The channel is not "weak" there — it is undefined.

## What this changes

The line's heredity ladder now has three rungs measured on the same pairs and in the same way: a written scalar (0.98, the
rule's fidelity), a body's shape (0.59 / 0.35-0.43), a body's area (0.39-0.45), and the local scale around the body
(0.20 in the lattice, undefined in the connected mass). The trend is monotone and it is a statement about this medium: what
a division transmits is mostly the object, weakly its surroundings, and the medium is not a channel that carries itself.

## Limits

Ten runs per habitat, not twenty, because the compact projection exists for the ten seeds of each inflow in this block; the
pooled slope is therefore less precise than X156's, though its interval is still tight because the pairs are many (2 281).
The nearest-neighbour measure is one scalar per body: a body with three neighbours is described by the closest one, so a
medium with an ordered lattice would look like a small spacing regardless of the lattice constant. That is a real
limitation of the readout and the reason the next item below lists a second definition.

## What would kill it

A second spacing definition — the mean of the three nearest neighbours, or the distance to the k-th neighbour with k = 3 —
would test whether the 0.20 is a spacing effect or a nearest-neighbour artefact. If the two definitions disagree, the
number is an artefact of the definition and not a property of the medium. Equally, a run in which the lattice is diluted
(heterogeneity amplitude below 0.5) should move the spacing slope towards the maze's undefined case gradually rather than
jumping.

## Next hypotheses

1. **A spacing definition that survives the connected mass**: the distance to the nearest *pore* or the local wavelength of
   the field, rather than the gap between silhouettes.
2. **Does the spacing slope decay with distance?** The same reconstruction at the second, third, fourth neighbour gives a
   decay length, and that length is a direct measure of "how far the medium remembers".
3. **A causal version**: displace a body by two cells at birth and follow whether its neighbours' spacing follows, which
   would separate inheritance of place from inheritance of local density.
4. **The same measurement in the pond world** (twelve seeds, cells not silhouettes) to see whether the ladder's slope
   survives a change of carrier.
