# X178 — Is the crossing a plateau or a cliff? Radius 6 at gap 9 on thirty seeds (pre-registration, 04.11)

**Status:** pre-registered, not yet run.

## 1. Manipulation is connected

X176 found that radius 6 fuses 0 of 15 at gap 10, which with X158's 0 of 10 makes that cell 0 of 25 — while the same size at
gap 9 was 3 of 10 in X158. A share that falls from 0.30 to 0.00 inside one cell of gap is either a **cliff** (a threshold, which
is what "the crossing" means) or a **floor** (the arm fuses occasionally and ten seeds simply saw three of them).

This item doubles the seeds at the one cell that decides it: **radius 6, gap 9, `F` = 0.030, thirty fresh seeds**. It is not a
new question so much as the cheapest way to tell a threshold from a small share, and it matters for every claim the block has
made about "the crossing", because all of them assume a cell where the share moves from "mostly fuses" to "mostly does not".

## 2. Positive control fires

The control fires if the peak stays inside [0.05, 2.0] at every read, the corridor reads below the peak in **every** contact run,
and the arm's share lands in **[0.05, 0.55]** — a band wide enough to contain both "a small share near a floor" (0.1-0.3) and
the earlier reading (3/10), and narrow enough that 0 of 30 would fall outside it and would itself be the result. The gap-10 cell
(0 of 25) is the registered negative control: the same seeds' family at one more cell of gap must not fuse.

## 3. Readout exists and varies

The primary readout is the **share** (fraction of runs whose component count reaches one), with its Wilson interval; the
secondary is X170's contact-time corridor, which must vary (sd ≥ 0.02) if there are three or more contact events.

## 4. Sensitivity arithmetic

Thirty seeds resolve a share of 0.10 with a Wilson half-width of about 0.11, so the two hypotheses this item separates —
"share ≈ 0.30" against "share ≈ 0.10" — are distinguishable at the arms' expected counts (9 of 30 versus 3 of 30), and the
registered band [0.05, 0.55] contains both. A share at or below 0.05 with zero contacts in the gap-10 control would say the
crossing is a cliff at about one cell; a share near 0.30 would say the earlier 3/10 was representative and the cliff is at gap
10, not 9.

## 5. Refusal rule

If the share lands inside [0.05, 0.55] and the gap-10 control fuses nothing, the item is recorded as **the crossing's shape
measured at this size**: the share at gap 9 is stated with its interval, and the cliff's position is named as between gap 9 and
gap 10. If the share exceeds 0.55, radius 6's crossing at gap 9 is confirmed and **X158's 3/10 was on the low side of noise** —
which is worth recording, because it means the crossing's own report understated that arm. If the share is at or below 0.05, the
item is recorded as **the crossing is a cliff**, with the earlier 3/10 re-read as the top of the noise.

## 6. What would kill it

The gap-10 negative control uses X158's and X176's existing runs rather than fresh seeds of the same family, so "the same
seeds' family" is not exactly true; the report will say so. And thirty seeds is enough to separate 0.10 from 0.30 but not 0.20
from 0.30, so a middling outcome will be recorded as middling.

## 7. Implementation notes

Runner `scripts/x178_cliff.py` (radius 6, gap 9, `F` = 0.030, seeds 1121-1150, 2 000 steps, field dumps every ten steps into a
gitignored directory), analysis `scripts/x178_cliff_analyse.py` (share with Wilson interval, the contact-time corridor, and the
comparison against X158's 3/10 and X176's 0/25). Aggregate
`morphogenesis/experiments/E09_pond/l1_field_x178_cliff.json`. Cost about twenty-five minutes of CPU.
