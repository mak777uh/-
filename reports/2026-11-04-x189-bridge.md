# X189 — the bridge between two bodies, by local inflow and size (04.11)

**Question.** X187 reproduced X186's sign flip under selection, and X188 showed it is **not** bodies appearing around the pair
(warm-minus-cold count +0.234 at radius 6, below the registered bar). X188's refusal rule moved the question to the
**field**, which is what this item measures: at the last slice on which the bodies are still separate, is the bridge between them
weaker when the site is warm? Preregistration `reports/2026-11-04-x189-bridge-plan.md`, bar **0.05** and prediction unchanged by
the amendment recorded there.

**Result: supported, with a caveat that has to be read with the number.** The registered readout on the tightest two-component slice gives −0.0865 at radius 6 and +0.0039 at radius 3; the contact-moment
reading (the last saved slice on which the pieces are separate) gives −0.2016 and +0.0896. The contact moment is reported second because
it is the unregistered one, and the ratio it uses (field value midway between the two bodies, over the maximum inside a radius-10 disc
around them) is

| arm | level | `F_local` | fused | bridge ratio at contact |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| radius 6 gap 9 | cold | 0.0157 | 3 of 3 | 0.38304 |
| radius 6 gap 9 | warm | 0.0300 | 1 of 3 | 0.18146 |
| radius 3 gap 10 | cold | 0.0176 | 0 of 3 | 0.22686 |
| radius 3 gap 10 | warm | 0.0300 | 3 of 3 | 0.31649 |

so at the largest size warmth thins the bridge (**-0.20158** at the contact moment, **-0.08655** on the registered
tightest slice, bar 0.05), and the pair that touches separates again; at the smallest size warmth moves it **+0.08963**, i.e. the *opposite* sign — there the bridge is slightly thicker and
the pair coalesces more. The **registered** reading carries the direction on its own: the tightest two-component slice gives −0.08655 at
radius 6 (past the bar) and +0.00387 at radius 3, the same sign and the same refutation. The three readings are nevertheless **one and a
half, not three**: the tightest slice and the min-over-central-half are computed on the same slice (step 40 in 9 of 12 runs) and
correlate 0.672, while the contact reading correlates 0.673 with the tightest and 0.940 with the min. The headline is therefore the
registered −0.08655, the contact moment is a labelled secondary reading, and its **magnitude is exploratory** — it is 2.3× the
registered value because it is taken on a different slice.

**The caveat, plainly.** The manipulation *is* the lattice site, so three seeds are not three independent sites: radius 6 has
2 distinct cold sites against 1 distinct warm one, and radius 3 has 2 against 1. Within the single warm
radius-6 site the contact ratio still spans 0.0073 to 0.3992, so the between-group difference is **not larger than the within-site
spread**, and the mean leans on one low run; dropping it leaves −0.114, still beyond the bar. The registered *tightest-slice*
summary is contaminated: it lands on step 40 — the seed-independent initial profile — in 9 of 12 runs, so it is
reported and not relied on. And the radius-3 half of the registered prediction is **refuted**: warmth does not move the bridge in
the same direction there, it moves it the other way, on a single replicated site.

**Measurement, and two corrections made while reading the data** (recorded in the plan, with the reviewer's independent check). The
segment and the discs follow the bodies' **current** centres from the component log, but the reason first stated for that — "the bodies
drift about five cells by step 40" — is **wrong and withdrawn**: matched drift is median 0.4 cells (p75 2.4, max 21.6, and the max is an
order swap), and the five-cell offset was the ring radius misread as motion. And the bodies are **rings**:
their logged centroid sits in the hollow, where the field is background, so a disc of radius 3 around the centre contains the hole
and not the body — the peak is taken over a radius-10 disc and the bridge is read midway between the centres. Neither correction
changes the bar or the prediction; the second one *is* the difference between a readout that returned nothing usable (peak 0.10-0.15
against a 0.3 floor) and one that measures the ring.

**What would kill it.** Five sites per level (the level is a site, so the sample has to be *sites*, not seeds) with three seeds
each would separate site-to-site variation from the level effect; that is X190. Until then this is a candidate mechanism, not a
measured one: the bridge and the outcome move together within each arm, but the arms have one to two sites each.
