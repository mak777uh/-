# X170 — The crossing does depend on the source, and the corridor says how (04.11)

**Question.** X168c and X168d found no dependence of the crossing on the inflow rate, and the block was ready to write the
ten-cell crossing as a property of the reaction-diffusion pair alone. That is the claim this item was built to give a
mechanism to: if the crossing ignores the source, then at the moment two bodies become one the field *between* them should be
in the same state at every rate.

Pre-registration: [`2026-11-04-x170-corridor-plan.md`](2026-11-04-x170-corridor-plan.md). Three rates, fifteen seeds each
(971-985), surface gap 10, separation 16, radius 3, 2 000 steps, the field dumped every ten steps. The corridor is the
minimum of the field over the middle third of the segment joining the two bodies' centres at the last step at which there are
still two bodies — that is, at the moment of coalescence — divided by the peak field at that step.

## Result: the corridor is not the same at every rate

| `F` | contact events | corridor / peak, median | spread |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.026 | 4 of 15 | **0.516** | 0.012 |
| 0.030 | 7 of 15 | **0.479** | 0.036 |
| 0.036 | 10 of 15 | **0.392** | 0.040 |

The gap between the bodies is **less filled** at the moment they become one when the source is stronger: 0.52 against 0.39, a
difference of 0.124 against a within-group spread of 0.040 — three standard deviations, well past the one-sd bar the
pre-registration set. The refusal rule therefore fires, and in the direction it was written to report: **the corridor depends
on the source, and the crossing's insensitivity needs a different explanation.**

## And the crossing is not insensitive after all

The contact counts in that table are themselves a fusion share at gap 10, and they run **4/15, 7/15, 10/15** (0.27 → 0.47 →
0.67). That is a monotone increase with the source, and with fifteen seeds per rate it is large enough to test: a
Cochran–Armitage trend test gives **z = 2.20, one-sided p = 0.014**, and Fisher's exact test on the extreme pair 4/15 against
10/15 gives p = 0.066.

So the corrected statement for the fusion line is: **the crossing moves with the source**, by about a cell across the range
where the world lives, and the gap at contact is correspondingly less filled. X168d's "no trend detected" was true of its own
cell — gap 11, ten seeds — and it was not a licence to say the crossing is independent of the source; that reading, which was
written into §4.1 and into the claims, is withdrawn here.

## Why the two cells disagree

Gap 11 sits beyond the crossing, where the share is small (0/10, 1/10, 3/10) and ten seeds cannot resolve a difference of
this size (15 % power for a 0.3 difference). Gap 10 sits at the crossing, where the share is near a half and the same
difference is visible. The lesson is the block's own: **a negative at the cell where the effect is marginal is not a negative
about the effect.** The pre-registration chose the cell where the trend was seen in five-seed data, which was the right cell
for the question as asked and the wrong one for the conclusion that got written.

## What this does and does not change

It does not change the two-cell contact band, the additivity over contacts, the size-ratio insensitivity or the reversed
size dependence — those were measured against the size and the geometry, and none of them involved the source. It does change
one sentence, which was the strongest one the fusion line had: the crossing is **partly** the medium's and **partly** the
source's. The mechanism is in the corridor: a stronger source means the bodies coalesce while the bridge between them is less
built, which is why a stronger source pushes the crossing outward by about a cell.

## Limits

Contact events are 4, 7 and 10, so the per-rate medians rest on small numbers and the F = 0.026 median has four values (0.50,
0.53, 0.53, 0.50) — the spread is tight but the count is thin. Gap 10 was not in X168d's pre-registration, so this is a
**re-analysis of a new arm**, not a re-reading of the old one; the arm was run for the corridor and its fusion counts are a
by-product. The corridor's definition (the middle third of the segment) sits inside the bodies at small separations, which is
not the case here but would be for gaps below 8.

## What would kill it

Twenty-five seeds per rate at gap 10 would put the shares at about 7/12/17 and the trend beyond argument; a corridor
measurement at gap 11 with enough contact events would show whether the same 0.39-versus-0.52 shift is there. If the corridor
difference vanished at larger n, the mechanism would go and the crossing's dependence would remain unexplained but still real.

## Next hypotheses

1. **Gap 11 with enough seeds** (40 per rate) to see whether the corridor shift is the same there — the cell where X168d was
   run is the cell where the mechanism should now be checked.
2. **The bridge's build-up before contact**: the corridor at a fixed distance from the midpoint, sampled over the ten steps
   before coalescence, would show whether a stronger source merges "earlier in the bridge's life" — the mechanism the
   corridor median only implies.
3. **The corridor as a predictor of the crossing**: if the coalescence happens at a corridor value near 0.4-0.5, the crossing's
   position at other sizes and geometries could be predicted rather than measured, which would turn this from a description
   into a model.
