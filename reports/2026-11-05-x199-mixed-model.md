## Question

The article's opening falsifier is that the published local-inflow dispersion is an artefact of treating repeated
runs of the same site as independent observations: if the site is a random intercept, the fixed effect of the local
inflow should be absorbed by it. X180's pooled point-biserial (−0.527, z −7.807) and X183's five AUCs are stored
scalars with no run-level data in the aggregate, so an independent reviewer could not check the dispersion at all.
This item refits the association with the site as a random intercept and reports the fixed-effect slope, its
interval and the intra-class correlation, so the claim and its falsifier are both quantitative.

## Registered model and estimator, and the one substitution

`logit P(fused_i) = a + b·z_i + u_{w(i)}`, `u_w ~ N(0, σ_w²)`, random intercept per world (variant A) or per phase
class (variant B). ICC = σ²/(σ² + π²/3).

The plan registered a **Laplace approximation** of the marginal likelihood. It was fitted and it is **not adequate
here**, so the reported fit is a different estimator; this is a disclosed correction of the estimator, not of the
criteria. `laplace_adequacy` in the aggregate, at the fitted intercept and slope:

| σ | adaptive Gauss–Hermite | Laplace − adaptive | plain GH − adaptive |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.5 | −290.166 | −0.013 | 0.000 |
| 1.0 | −268.907 | −0.226 | 0.000 |
| 2.0 | −225.300 | **−2.329** | 0.000 |
| 4.0 | −190.435 | **−94.261** | −0.022 |
| 8.0 | −181.379 | **−187.586** | −1.532 |

The Laplace error grows with σ, and the σ this pool prefers is ≈ 8, where the registered estimator is 187 log-likelihood
units wrong (slope 1.44 instead of 2.48). Plain quadrature with nodes scaled by σ also fails at large σ. The estimator
of record is therefore an **adaptive** Gauss–Hermite quadrature (nodes centred on each cluster's Laplace mode and scaled
by its curvature), whose 25-node and 61-node values agree to < 1e-5 at the fitted parameters. The Laplace fit is
carried beside the reported one: `→ primary_variant_world.registered_estimator_laplace`.

Intervals are profile-likelihood intervals (2·ΔlogL ≤ 3.841, 1 df) on a (slope, σ) grid with the intercept profiled out
exactly at each grid point, plus a cluster bootstrap resampling worlds (100 replicates; the plan said 400, reduced for
wall-clock and reported as such). Point estimates are the joint maximum; where the mode of the marginal profile differs
it is reported too.

`→ comparison_against_fixed_effects`

| variant | mixed slope | fixed-effects slope | FE Wald 95 % | ratio |

| `F_local_min`, world | +2.477 | **+0.590** | [0.345, 0.835] | **4.197** |

The mixed slope is cluster-specific and the fixed-effects slope population-averaged; for a logistic model the
conditional slope is the larger in magnitude, so a ratio above 1 is the expected direction and is **not** evidence of
attenuation. The registered criterion C3 was one-sided at ≥ 0.70; the observed ratio is 4.20. Reproduced in the same
code path: X180's covariate keeps its negative sign, `→ published_for_comparison` (x180 pooled point-biserial −0.527,
mean −0.552, z −7.807; X183 AUCs, `best_local = F_local_min` with 0.866 overall / 0.895 within).

## Identifiability

1. `F_local_min = F·(1+0.5(2·min(G₀,G₁)−1))` is a deterministic function of (cell, phase), so it is **exactly constant
   within every multi-run world** — 0 of 47 worlds vary at all (max within-world sd 0.0; `→ covariate_constancy_within_world`).
   This model therefore identifies the slope **between worlds only**; there is no within-world slope in this design to
   estimate. The same is not quite true of `F_local_mid`: 4 of 47 worlds vary, with a maximum within-world sd of 0.0113,
   i.e. ≈ 2 % of the covariate's own sd — not enough to identify a within-world slope either.
2. **σ is weakly identified.** The likelihood ridge joining the slope to σ is flat. The σ profile interval
   [3.755, 7.832] is centred on the σ profile mode 6.32, while the joint maximum sits at 8.368, a fraction of a grid
   step outside it; the grid's own maximum falls 2.20 log-likelihood units below the joint fit
   (`→ primary_variant_world.mixed.profile`: `profile_mode_sigma`, `grid_deficit_vs_joint_mle`,
   `point_estimate_inside_interval`, `interval_warnings`). The ICC is 0.955 at the joint fit and 0.924 at the profile
   mode; its profile lower bound is 0.81 and its bootstrap interval [0.950, 0.958] is narrow only because each bootstrap
   replicate is started from the full-sample fit, which is a known bias of warm-started bootstraps on a flat ridge.
   **What survives all of this: the sign and the non-zero-ness of the slope, and an ICC above ≈ 0.8. What does not: the
   magnitude of the slope and the exact value of σ.**
3. The midpoint covariate under a world random intercept drifts to a large magnitude (−8.05, profile lower end −43.5)
   with σ ≈ 51, i.e. the model approaches a per-world intercept fit; its *sign* is unambiguous but its magnitude is not
   identified in that variant. Under a phase-class intercept, where the covariate does vary within the unit, it is
   finite and stable (−1.547 [−1.984, −1.359]).

## Limits

- Between-world slope only (R4); the decisive test of whether a "world" is a cluster at all — two seeds at one
  (cell, phase) that disagree — is not in the committed corpus and cannot be run from it.
- σ / ICC weakly identified as above; intervals are grid-interpolated, and profile ends are only as precise as the grid
  step (slope step 0.83, 61 points; σ 17 log-spaced points between 0.2 and 200).
- The estimator of record differs from the registered one (Laplace → adaptive Gauss–Hermite), disclosed above with the
  size of the error; the criteria were not changed.
- Bootstrap replicates: 100 rather than the registered 400.
- The plan's exclusion count is stale (2371/3376 now against 1219/2224 then); the pool is unchanged.

## Next hypotheses

1. **The decisive cluster test (X200).** Run two seeds at one (cell, phase) — a true clone pair. If they disagree, the
   "world" unit is not a cluster and this item's ICC (and X197's count-worlds correction) rest on a wrong unit.
   Nothing in the committed corpus can see this, because only 47 of 258 worlds have a second run.
2. **A within-world design.** Vary the geometry at a *fixed* phase class (or the phase at a fixed arm geometry) so the
   covariate changes inside the cluster. Then the between/within decomposition of the slope is estimable and the ICC
   can be separated from the between-world slope instead of being jointly pinned by the same ridge.
3. **Bound the midpoint magnitude.** Refit the midpoint covariate with a penalised or weakly informative prior on σ, and
   report whether the world-variant magnitude has a finite upper bound at all; the present profile says it does not.
4. **Independent recomputation.** A second implementation (e.g. `lme4`-equivalent quadrature or a Bayesian sampler) on
   the same committed aggregate, since the reviewer's original complaint was precisely that the dispersion numbers
   could not be checked.

## §10 card text (for the lead to paste)

### §10jh — X199 — the local-inflow association survives a random intercept per world, and the world carries the variance (05.11)

The falsifier the article opens with — that the published dispersion is only between-family noise that a site random
effect would absorb — fails on the committed aggregates. Over the 311 runs of the 11 mixed cells (135 fused, share
0.4341) collapsed into 258 worlds (1.205 runs per world), the logistic slope of fusion on the standardised local inflow
at the colder body is **+2.48 per sd** under a random intercept per world, with a 95 % profile interval [1.13, 3.09] and
a world-cluster bootstrap interval [1.23, 4.27]; the likelihood ratio against a zero slope is 19.0 on 1 df
(p = 1.3 × 10⁻⁵). The between-world sd is **σ = 8.37** and the ICC is **0.955** (0.924 at the σ profile mode; 95 %
profile lower bound 0.81): essentially all of the latent-scale variance is between worlds, not between repeats of a
world. The conditional slope is *larger* than the fixed-effects reading (0.590, Wald [0.345, 0.835]), ratio 4.20, which
is the direction a logistic mixed model requires and not evidence of attenuation. A random intercept per phase class
(143 classes, the site-only unit pooled across geometries) gives +0.98 [0.59, 1.53] with σ = 2.53 and ICC 0.660, so the
result does not depend on which of the two nested site definitions is used. The midpoint covariate keeps its published
negative sign in both variants (−8.05 world, −1.55 phase).

Two limits are registered and binding. The local inflow is a deterministic function of (cell, phase), so it is exactly
constant within every multi-run world (0 of 47 vary), and this model identifies a **between-world** slope only — there
is no within-world slope in this design to estimate. And σ is weakly identified: the ridge joining the slope to σ is
flat enough that the joint σ sits a fraction of a grid step outside the interval centred on the σ profile mode, whose
grid maximum falls 2.20 log-likelihood units below the joint fit; the *sign* and the non-zero-ness of the slope are
robust to that, its magnitude is not. The estimator registered for this item — a Laplace approximation — was fitted and
is not adequate here: it sits 2.33 log-likelihood units below adaptive Gauss–Hermite quadrature already at σ = 2 and
187.6 below at σ = 8 (slope 1.44 against 2.48), so the reported fit is the quadrature one and the Laplace fit is carried
beside it. The test this item cannot run is a clone pair: two seeds at the same (cell, phase) that disagree would mean
the "world" unit is not a cluster. There is no such pair in the committed corpus.
